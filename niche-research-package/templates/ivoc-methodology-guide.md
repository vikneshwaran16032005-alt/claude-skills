# IVOC Research — Methodology Guide

## What IVOC is

**IVOC = Internal Voice of Customer.**

It's the discipline of capturing how your prospect speaks about their problems — in their own words, lifted verbatim from places where they didn't know anyone was watching. Not paraphrased. Not summarised. Not "what I think they probably feel." The exact words.

The deliverable is a document filled with italicised quotes, sorted into buckets, ranked by frequency and emotional intensity. From that document, you can write copy that reads as if their best friend wrote it.

This is the single highest-leverage research discipline in direct-response copywriting. It's also the one most people skip — because reading 200 Reddit threads in a weekend feels like work, and clever marketers convince themselves they "already know what the market wants."

They don't. Nobody does. Until you read the words.

---

## Why this matters

People buy when they see their own conscious desires reflected back at them. *Their* conscious desires — not the version a marketer reverse-engineered from a persona document.

A prospect's brain runs a fast, mostly subconscious match: *"Does this person understand my world?"* If the copy uses their phrases, their metaphors, their idioms, the answer is yes — within the first sentence. If it uses corporate generalisations, the answer is no — within the first sentence.

IVOC is how you guarantee the answer is yes.

The principle (from the Original Script Prompts on the fundamentals of copywriting): *"We need to source VOC data from places where people have no idea they're being probed — to get to know our prospects intimately and get to grips with their real thoughts, desires, and concerns."*

Public Reddit threads, anonymous forum posts, software-review sites where people vent at 11pm — those are the watering holes. Sales calls and surveys are not. People perform in surveys. They tell the truth in r/recruiting at midnight.

## The seven IVOC buckets

These are the categories you sort verbatim quotes into. Same buckets used by the Copy That crew and embedded in the IVOC Research Template.

### 1. Desires
What they consciously want. Not what we think they should want. The dream outcomes, in their language.

*Example signal:* an agency MD on a podcast says — *"I just want my Friday afternoons back."* That's a desire. Capture it verbatim. Don't translate it to *"reduced administrative overhead."*

### 2. Firm Notions
Beliefs they hold solidly. Things they will not change their minds on, regardless of how good your copy is. Wasted breath to argue with.

*Example signal:* *"Recruitment is a relationship business. Always was, always will be."* You don't fight that — you ride it.

### 3. Shakeable Notions
Beliefs they hold but only loosely. Things they could be talked out of with the right evidence or framing. **This is where copy does its real work.**

*Example signal:* *"I have to use Bullhorn — there isn't really an alternative."* That belief moves with the right counter-example. Worth the effort.

### 4. Identifications
How they see themselves. The labels and self-images they wear. Your hero archetype lives here.

*Example signal:* *"I built this from a desk in my kitchen."* That's an identification — the kitchen-table founder. Mirror it back to them in the copy.

### 5. Characteristics
Demographic and behavioural signal. Age range, role, agency size, geography, what tools they use, how they spend their week, who they work with.

*Less emotionally loaded than the others — but informs everything from channel choice to tone calibration.*

### 6. Other
The bucket for anything that doesn't fit cleanly above but feels important. Frequently includes pains/fears, objections, and surprises that pop up mid-research and don't yet have a category. Don't force-fit. Hold loose, sort later.

### 7. Common Words & Phrases
The vocabulary itself. Specific words and phrases that recur across multiple posters, multiple platforms, multiple weeks of data. The recurring language is the brand of the niche.

*Example signal:* across thirty Capterra reviews, the words *"clunky," "click-heavy," "antiquated"* appear thirty times. That's not coincidence — it's the niche's collective vocabulary for one specific kind of pain. Use those exact words in copy. Never *"suboptimal user experience."*

## Where to look (the watering holes)

Sorted by signal density. Higher in the list = louder, more honest, more useful.

### Tier 1 — anonymous, complaint-rich, peer-to-peer
- **Reddit** — niche-specific subreddits + r/recruitinghell-style complaint hubs. *Hard-blocked from automated tooling — you must browse manually.*
- **Software review sites** — G2, Capterra, Trustpilot, Software Advice. **Specifically the 1- and 2-star reviews.** That's where the real language lives. Five-star reviews are PR.
- **Niche-specific forums** — StaffingHub, RecruitmentBuzz, The Recruiting Forum, etc.
- **Quora** — niche-specific questions and answers. Lower volume, higher signal-per-thread.

### Tier 2 — public but authored
- **Industry-leader blogs** — Greg Savage (`thesra.co`), Hung Lee's Recruiting Brainfood, etc. The bloggers themselves give you Firm Notions and Shakeable Notions; the comment threads give you raw VOC if any commenters are vocal.
- **LinkedIn comment threads** — under industry-influencer posts. Often the most candid agency-owner language on the public internet. Requires scrolling, but the signal is high.
- **Podcast transcripts** — Recruiting Brainfood, The Full Desk Experience, The Staffing Show. Use podcast-transcription services or platform-native transcripts where available.
- **YouTube comments** — under industry-channel videos. Niche-specific channels especially.

### Tier 3 — private but askable
- **Facebook groups** — niche-specific, often invite-only. Apply early, lurk for a week, then read the archive.
- **Slack and Discord communities** — increasingly where industry conversation happens. Harder to access, higher signal once in.

### What does NOT count as IVOC
- **Sales call transcripts.** People perform on sales calls. They posture, they posture-defend, they say what they think makes them look good. Useful for objection mapping, **not** for IVOC.
- **Survey responses.** Same problem — performance bias.
- **Industry reports, market analyses, vendor whitepapers.** Useful for context. Not IVOC.
- **Anything you wrote yourself or that another marketer wrote about the niche.** That's not voice of customer. That's voice of marketer.

The bar for IVOC is one question: *did the speaker know they were being recorded as input to copywriting?* If yes, it's not IVOC. If no, it's gold.

## The five-step research process

### Step 1 — Source

Sit down with one platform at a time. Block 90 minutes per platform. Use a research-only browser profile so your normal cookies don't pollute the algorithmic surfacing.

For each platform, run 8–12 specific search queries — exact phrases your prospect is likely to use, problem keywords, tool names, "I hate," "drowning in," "wish there was." Sort by Top → All Time first, then by Recent.

### Step 2 — Record verbatim

The discipline is brutal but simple: **copy the exact words. Italics on. No edits. No paraphrasing. No "tidying up the grammar."**

Open the IVOC sheet (the template included in this engagement, or your own) and paste each quote into the bucket it most plainly fits. If it doesn't fit cleanly, drop it in *Other* — sort later.

For every quote, capture:
1. The verbatim text (italicised)
2. The source URL
3. The platform and thread title (so the citation is traceable)
4. The date if visible
5. Optional: speaker role / handle if useful

If you find yourself rewriting a quote to make it "sound better" — stop. The clumsy, unpolished real version is the version that converts.

### Step 3 — Summarise

After 12–15 hours of recording, you'll have hundreds of quotes. Now you summarise — but only from inside the buckets. Look at every quote in *Desires*. What's the underlying conscious desire across the loudest 5–7 quotes? Write that summary in your own words — but place it BELOW the raw quotes, not on top of them. The raw stays. The summary is a layer.

Repeat for each bucket.

The Original Script Prompts say it cleanly: *"Your IVOC sheet should be filled with italics — you should let the raw data do the talking and only note your own thoughts down when you notice frequent patterns or similarities."*

### Step 4 — Rank

Now rank the summaries — within each bucket — by **intensity** and **frequency**.

- *Intensity* = how emotionally loaded was the language? *"I'm drowning in admin"* > *"admin is a bit annoying."*
- *Frequency* = how many independent posters said the same thing in different words?

A summary that hits high intensity AND high frequency is your top tier. That's the language and the angle that goes straight into the lead of your copy.

### Step 5 — Adapt

The final stage. Take the ranked summaries and walk through your copy structure. For each section of copy, find the IVOC quote or summary that fits. Use the prospect's exact phrases — their metaphors, their adjectives, their tells. Don't translate them up to corporate language. The whole point is they don't sound corporate.

Where your product has a benefit that maps to a top-tier IVOC summary, that's your headline angle. Where there's no overlap, that's a benefit you're probably overweighting in your copy.

## The "anonymous probe" principle (and why most research is bad)

If your prospect knows you're researching them, they perform.

The whole game of IVOC is to catch them when they're unguarded. Reddit at 11pm. A 1-star Trustpilot rant the day they cancelled their contract. A LinkedIn comment that someone fired off without thinking. A YouTube comment under a video about their industry where they're venting to no one in particular.

That's where the truth lives. That's where the language lives. That's where you mine.

This means:
- **No interviewing prospects for IVOC** — that's research-call territory, different methodology, different output.
- **No reading vendor case studies** — the agency was paid to say what they said.
- **No surveys** — even anonymous surveys carry performance bias because the respondent knows it's being read.

The only real IVOC source is the prospect being themselves to other prospects, with no idea anyone outside the conversation is reading.

## When sources are blocked (and they will be)

Reddit is increasingly hard-blocked from automated tooling. Mirror services come and go. APIs require auth keys. This is a real constraint.

**The workaround is manual browsing.** Open Reddit in a browser, log in, sort the niche subreddits by Top → All Time, copy quotes into your IVOC sheet by hand. Slow. Effective. Unblockable.

Other workarounds when a primary source is blocked:
- Pivot to **adjacent platforms** that cover the same niche from a slightly different angle. Quora often surfaces the same vocabulary as Reddit, with different phrasing. Industry-specific forums (RecruitmentBuzz, StaffingHub) cover the same territory.
- **Software-review sites are often the best fallback** — agency owners writing 1- and 2-star reviews of the tools they regret are extraordinarily candid. Capterra, G2, Trustpilot.
- **Comment threads under industry-leader posts** — LinkedIn especially. The post is the prompt; the comments are the IVOC.
- **Podcast transcripts** — most major industry podcasts publish transcripts or have YouTube versions you can transcribe. The guest interviews are particularly rich because guests aren't selling.

When you cannot verify a quote is real, **do not use it.** Better fifteen verified quotes than fifty fabricated ones. The whole IVOC framework collapses the moment you allow yourself to invent quotes "to fill out the bucket."

## Quality bar — what good looks like

You're done with IVOC research when:

1. You have **80–150 verbatim quotes** captured across the seven buckets, with source URLs.
2. Each major bucket (*Desires, Firm Notions, Shakeable Notions, Common Phrases*) has **at least 10 quotes**.
3. **Common Words & Phrases** has at least 30 distinct entries — words and phrases that you've now seen used by multiple independent posters across multiple platforms.
4. The summaries within each bucket are **2–4 sentences max**, in your own words, with the raw quotes clearly visible above them.
5. The rankings are explicit — top three summaries per bucket flagged.
6. Anyone on your team (or you, six months later) could read the document and **draft cold copy that sounds like an insider**, without doing any further research.

If any of those checks fail, the research isn't done. Don't write copy off half-built IVOC — you'll know the difference within five sentences.

## Time budget

Two weekends, roughly 24–30 hours total, for a niche this rich.

**Weekend 1 — discovery and capture (12–15 hours):**
- Saturday: Reddit-equivalent platforms + niche forums + Quora (8 hrs)
- Sunday: Industry-leader blogs + LinkedIn comment archaeology + Facebook groups (4–7 hrs)

**Weekend 2 — validation and synthesis (12–15 hours):**
- Saturday: Software review sites + podcast transcripts + the long tail of sources you didn't get to in week 1 (8 hrs)
- Sunday: Summarise → rank → adapt. Build the final IVOC document. (4–7 hrs)

If you're rushing the second weekend's synthesis to fit "I want to start writing copy on Monday" — stop. The synthesis IS the value. The raw quotes without the summary and ranking are just noise. The summary without the raw quotes is just opinion. You need both, properly stacked.

---

## Output format

Your final IVOC document should have, in this order:

1. **Product / One Reader header** — what's being sold, and who the one reader is, in two sentences.
2. **Things searched for** — a short list of the queries and platforms used (so the work is reproducible and auditable).
3. **Raw IVOC data**, by bucket, with verbatim quotes in italics + source URLs.
4. **IVOC summaries** — 2–4 sentence pattern reads per bucket, placed below the raw quotes.
5. **Rankings** — top 3 summaries per bucket, by intensity × frequency.
6. **Final IVOC summary** — one page, the top-of-stack synthesis. The brief that anyone could write copy from.
7. **Sources** — full URL list at the end. Every quote traceable.

That's the artefact. It's a working tool — not a presentation. It gets messier as you add more data. That's healthy. Resist the urge to "tidy up" the raw quotes. They're meant to feel raw.

## Hard rules

1. **No fabricated quotes. Ever.** If you can't verify it's real, leave it out. Fifteen real quotes beat fifty plausible ones.
2. **Verbatim only.** No paraphrasing, no "cleaning up the grammar," no translation to corporate language. The clumsy real version is the version that converts.
3. **Source URL on every quote.** If you can't cite it, you can't use it. The audit trail is the integrity.
4. **One platform at a time, deeply.** Don't bounce between Reddit and LinkedIn every ten minutes. Block 90-minute sessions per platform.
5. **Italics for raw, plain text for summaries.** Visual separation between data and your interpretation. Never blend.
6. **The summary lives BELOW the raw quotes, not on top of them.** The data leads. Your interpretation supports.
7. **Every quote belongs to one bucket** — but if you can't decide cleanly, drop it in *Other* and move on. Don't force.
8. **Rank by intensity × frequency, not by what you find most interesting.** Your interest is bias. The rankings are the corrective.
9. **Stop when you stop hearing new patterns.** If your last 50 quotes are all variations of things you already captured, you're done sourcing — move to synthesis.
10. **Re-read the IVOC document before every piece of copy you write.** Once isn't enough. The point of the artefact is that it's *active* — referenced every time, not built and shelved.
