# Workflows by Task Type

The master workflow in SKILL.md assumes "write new copy from scratch." In practice, many tasks are variations. This file covers the common variations — use the relevant sub-workflow instead of the full master workflow when these scenarios match.

---

## Variation 1: Critique existing copy

The user pastes existing copy and wants your feedback (not a rewrite).

### Shortened workflow
1. **Phase 0 (mini):** Infer RIOA from the copy itself. Confirm guesses with the user if ambiguous.
2. **Skip Phase 1-3** unless you need research to diagnose problems.
3. **Phase 4 (the whole thing):** Run the 3-level editing protocol + 20 principle validation + 5 subconscious questions.
4. **Phase 5:** Deliver as a critique report — not a rewrite.

### Output format for critique
```
# Critique — [piece]

## What's working
- [strength 1]
- [strength 2]

## Level 1 issues (value / idea)
- [issue] — why it matters: ...

## Level 2 issues (structure)
- [issue] — fix: ...

## Level 3 issues (line-level)
- [issue with before/after] ...

## Principle violations
- Principle X: [specific violation]

## Subconscious questions failures
- Question N: [what fails, why]

## Priority fixes (in order)
1. [highest impact fix]
2. ...
```

If the copy fails at Level 1 (value), say so explicitly. Don't waste the user's time fixing sentences in a fundamentally broken piece.

## Variation 2: Improve existing copy (rewrite)

User wants you to rewrite / improve existing copy, not just critique.

### Shortened workflow
1. **Phase 0:** Confirm RIOA (mostly inferable from existing copy).
2. **Phase 1 (partial):** Usually skip IVOC/competitor unless problems point to research gaps. Always do a quick product-analyst pass to make sure you have FAB-DB at your fingertips.
3. **Phase 2 (minimal):** Re-validate awareness + sophistication. If the existing copy has the wrong stage-match, this is probably the #1 fix.
4. **Phase 3:** Rewrite. Often you'll keep much of the original structure and just swap in sharper hooks, better mechanism language, and tighter dimensionalization.
5. **Phase 4:** Full edit protocol.

### Key question
"Is the existing copy's Big Idea the right one, or is the problem at the idea level?"
- If idea is right → edit for sharpness (mostly line + function edits with some value-tightening).
- If idea is wrong → rewrite the lead and body from scratch.

## Variation 3: Tiny-format copy (< 100 words)

Headlines, button labels, one-line ads, subject lines, Google PPC headlines, TikTok captions.

### Compressed workflow (15-30 min total)
1. **Establish RIOA in your head** — reader, idea, offer, action
2. **Check awareness stage** — match lead type
3. **Write 10-20 variants** using Schwartz's 38 verbalizations as a menu
4. **Filter to 3-5** that pass the 5 subconscious questions
5. **Deliver all 3-5** as options for testing — not just your favorite

### Example: Facebook ad headline for dog joint supplement
Generate variants:
- "Why your senior dog's joint pain won't respond to standard supplements" (secret / problem-aware)
- "From limping to playing in 48 hours — the new glucosamine formula vets don't prescribe (and why)" (promise + mechanism)
- "12-year-old dogs running again: the supplement breeders are recommending" (testimony + identification)
- "The tragic reason most dog joint supplements fail (and the one that works)" (secret + intrigue)
- "If your senior dog stopped playing, this is what's really happening" (problem-solution)

Deliver all 5 with a note on which best matches the strategy brief and recommended for A/B testing.

## Variation 4: Long-form sales letter / VSL

> 2,000 words. Typical direct-response territory — financial, info, health.

### Full workflow — do NOT shortcut
All 5 phases at full depth. Specific format notes:

- **Research**: IVOC especially crucial. Long-form copy that doesn't sound like the reader's internal voice fails catastrophically.
- **Strategy**: sophistication diagnosis is critical. Stage 3+ markets need a new mechanism or identification pivot.
- **Structure**: full 5-part (envelope/headline/lead/argument/close) OR 16-Word Sales Letter framework (One Belief + 10 Questions).
- **Writing approach**: write the lead, then the close, then the middle. This ensures the argument is framed by the entrance and exit.
- **Lead length**: typically 10-20% of the total piece. If your piece is 10,000 words, the lead is 1,000-2,000 words.
- **Body**: OCPB cycles (2-3 per page). 3-5 proof points minimum. Bullets (10-20) for scanners. Section headlines for navigation.
- **Close**: 25% of total copy effort goes here (Halbert). Clear offer. Multiple CTAs through the close section. P.S. with key benefit or deadline.
- **Editing**: full 3-level edit. Expect 3-5 editing passes across several days.

### Time budget for a 5,000-word sales page
- Phase 0-1: 8-16 hours (research-heavy)
- Phase 2: 2-4 hours (strategy)
- Phase 3: 4-8 hours (first draft)
- Phase 4: 4-8 hours (editing passes)
- Total: 20-40 hours

## Variation 5: Email sequence

Multi-email welcome / nurture / launch sequence.

### Sequence planning (BEFORE writing any individual email)
1. **Map the arc** — the reader's journey from joining the list to taking the terminal action (purchase, upgrade, etc.). What do they need to BELIEVE at the end? What belief-transitions happen across emails?
2. **Assign email roles:**
   - Welcome (1) — deliver lead magnet, set expectations, whitelist, one low-friction CTA
   - Value emails (2-5) — educate / entertain; build trust; NO hard sell
   - Soft-sell emails (1-2) — introduce the offer; soft CTA
   - Hard-sell emails (1-3) — full sell; urgency; deadline
3. **Choose structure per email type:**
   - Welcome: HBC, single CTA
   - Value: DIC (for lift-to-content) OR a standalone value email with soft PS link
   - Soft sell: HBC + 1 OCPB
   - Hard sell: Full sales-letter in miniature; multiple CTAs; P.S. emphasizing deadline
4. **Message-match between emails.** Each opens referencing the last; each closes setting up the next.

### Writing
Write all emails in one session to keep voice / idea consistency.
Pass each through the 3-level edit after the full sequence is drafted.

### Special elements
- **Subject line suite**: write 3 variants per email
- **Preview text**: deliberately chosen (either complement the subject line or add a second hook)
- **P.S. on sell emails**: almost always read; use for deadline or key benefit

## Variation 6: Cold outreach copy

Cold emails, LinkedIn DMs, cold pitches to potential clients / contacts.

### Mindset
Cold outreach IS COPY. Apply the same principles. The fact that you're writing to individuals doesn't change anything — actually it raises the bar, because the reader's defenses are higher.

### The universal checklist (every cold pitch)
1. **Grab attention** (be DIFFERENT and/or RELEVANT)
2. **Prove value** (demonstrate you can help + be likable)
3. **Present a fair offer** (link value to deliverables + minimum-viable-vision for next step)

### Structure (DIC-ish)
- **Disrupt (first line)**: specific reference to something only a real person would mention. Their recent podcast appearance, a specific bullet on their careers page, a pun on their product, a headline they published last week.
- **Intrigue (body)**: one concrete thing you noticed + one concrete proposal of how you'd help. Specific. Not "I could help with your marketing" — but "I could rewrite the homepage hero section because your current one is solution-aware but your Facebook ad traffic is problem-aware; there's a mismatch costing you conversions."
- **Click (close)**: minimum-viable-vision for next step. "Worth a 15-min call to see if I can help?" not "let me know if you're interested."

### Don'ts
- Don't open with "Hope you're well" or "I know you're busy but…"
- Don't attach 5 pages of a proposal to a cold email
- Don't mention price
- Don't apologize
- Don't send the same template to 200 people

### Don't send it from a generic name / email
Personal address, real name. Editorial look (A-pile) beats ad look (B-pile).

## Variation 7: Product description for ecommerce

Short product page copy. Very format-specific rules apply.

### Structure
- **Hero headline**: the most compelling single benefit (dimensionalized)
- **Sub-hero**: the mechanism in one sentence (how it achieves the benefit)
- **Bullet list of features-with-implied-benefits** (3-6 items — match the platform's preferred density)
- **Proof row** (logos, testimonials, star rating, award icons)
- **Specs** (dimensions, materials, weight, etc. — feature-only)
- **Shipping / returns / guarantee** (usually required by platform or brand standard)

### Voice
Match the brand voice. If the brand is playful, be playful. If it's clinical, be clinical.

### Rules
- Cut adjective stacks
- Use picture words for hero headline and sub-hero
- Specific numbers in features where possible ("lasts 18 hours on full charge" > "all-day battery")
- Don't let bullets go generic — each should have a specific claim

## Variation 8: Welcome email / onboarding

The first email after someone joins a list or signs up.

### Must-haves
- Thank / welcome
- Deliver what was promised (lead magnet, confirmation, etc.)
- Set expectations for future emails (how often, what type of content)
- Whitelist instruction (get onto their allow-list — this is critical for deliverability)
- ONE primary CTA (often: follow on social, or read a pinned content piece, or low-ticket upsell)

### Optional
- Personal intro from founder / author / brand voice
- Quick win / tip to establish value
- Invitation to reply (can boost inbox reputation)

### Don'ts
- Don't hard-sell in the welcome. Trust is zero; sell = spam signal.
- Don't cram 10 CTAs in. Pick ONE.
- Don't be generic. The welcome is often the highest-open-rate email they'll ever get from you — use it well.

## Variation 9: Content marketing (blog post, LinkedIn post, editorial)

Different beast — primarily content, not copy. But the Copy That crew still apply principles:

### Key differences from sales copy
- Primary goal: build trust / establish authority / inbound lead-gen
- No hard sell in the body
- Soft CTA at end (download, follow, contact)
- Must actually DELIVER value — teach something real

### Structure (loose)
- **Hook**: curiosity gap, counter-intuitive claim, or specific story
- **Body**: teach / demonstrate / share; use examples, specifics, proof
- **Close**: soft CTA + next steps + (optional) P.S. linking to a deeper resource

### Apply the value edit especially ruthlessly
Content that doesn't teach something the reader didn't know is waste. Your audience has infinite content options; they'll spend 15 seconds on yours before deciding to continue or leave.

## When to fall back to the full master workflow

If the task doesn't clearly fit one of the variations above, default to the full master workflow from SKILL.md. Better to over-invest in research than under-invest in a piece that matters.

## When to ask the user before starting

If any of these are unclear after reading the task:
- Who exactly the reader is
- What format / platform
- What the offer actually is
- What the desired action is
- What message-matching constraints exist (what the reader has seen before / after)
- Any brand voice constraints
- Deadline

Ask. Don't guess. A 5-minute question saves 3 hours of wrong-direction writing.
