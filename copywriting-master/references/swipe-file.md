## Headlines / hooks (ranked by reusable pattern)

**Top patterns (recur across many creators — use these first):**

## Subject lines

**Cold-email subject lines (corroborated rules + Gong 85M-email data):** 1–5 words, lowercase, read like an *internal email from a peer* — vague + emotional or loss-framed so they think "*what?*". No buzzwords, no numbers, no questions, no "AI". One-word winners that pulled 90%+ opens: **"welders," "dropouts," "delays," "missing time."** Lowercase lifts opens ~10% (looks like someone they know). `[5 src]` (Matt Macnamara, Patrick Dang, Daniel Fazio, Trent Dressel, Josh Braun, 30MPC)
- **7 subject-line formulas (30MPC/Gong):** (1) pervasive problem ("participant dropouts"), (2) industry trend ("remote engagement"), (3) company initiative / internal codeword ("FAPI trial recruitment," "imagination gap"), (4) exec quote ("Joel's comments"), (5) shared connection ("Carol's upcoming study"), (6) short pattern-interrupt ("delays"), (7) competitor name ("see Moderna"). First line must stay true to the subject — no bait-and-switch.
- **Write the subject like ad-copy a peer would forward:** "Ronald's beef isn't with us" (McDonald's vegan-patty pitch) · "Austin envy — SF's answer just landed" · "did you see the McDonald's news?" (30MPC)
- **Curiosity-gap unrelated to the offer:** "lost keys on Marshall Street" drove mass opens for an entrepreneurship event. (Josh Braun)
- **Personalized-image hook:** subject "She Shed" + a custom cartoon of the founder's origin-story shed in the body — research-driven, impossible to ignore. (30MPC)
- **Names-only subject (pure curiosity, forces the open):** "Matt + Pranav + Coral" — just the names of people on the thread/account, no solution language; reads like an internal forward. (30MPC)
- **Mirror their public obsession:** subject "The ultimate cheeseburger" tied a prospect's well-known food obsession to the pitch — instant reply. Tie a known hobby/passion to your ask. (30MPC)
- **Parrot their exact words (don't paraphrase):** subject "The power of a smile" lifted *verbatim* from the prospect's own LinkedIn quote — repeating their phrasing beats summarizing it. (30MPC)
- *Craft tip (Neville Medhora):* write 3–4 throwaway subject lines per email purely to jog your brain past your first instinct; the contrast between weak ("20–25 new companies that make money") and strong ("3 things I genuinely think would improve your day") is "write the line the way you'd text a friend."
- **Re-engage with the quantified prior priority itself:** "12% cost cutting initiative" · past-client-moved-companies "LAUSD in Seattle" (old proof + new location). (30MPC)
- **Name the ask outright (high open rate):** "[First Name] - Meeting Request" (claimed 41% open) · "Are you doubling down on [their goal]?" (claimed ~60% open). (Trent Dressel)
- **The "Triplet" (Josh Braun):** expected + expected + UNexpected — "iPhones, Verizon, my girlfriend"; first line ties all three. (Matt Macnamara)
- **Ghost / re-engagement (highest reply rate) — psychology of quitting:** "did i lose you" · "Have you given up on this project?" · "Are you alive?" · empty body, replied to the original thread. People resist feeling like quitters. **On a forwarded thread, *wipe* the old subject line so the next thing they see is your one-liner** — "Did I lose you?" pulled 42 positive replies of 48. `[4 src]` (Matt Macnamara, Frank Kern, Ian Koniak, **John Barrows**)
- **Plain-text lowercase 2–3-word winners (reads like a peer's internal email):** "right direction?" · "quick chat?" — John Barrows credits these with "making millions"; corroborates the lowercase-peer rule above. (John Barrows)
- **Pattern-interrupt / shared-history:** "[First name] — remember us from [past company]?" · a name tied to the recipient's known hobby ("LaDainian Tomlinson"). (Trent Dressel)
- **Plug their own category/initiative in:** "Digital marketing solutions for your private school" · put the prospect's internal project/code-name in the subject ("almost guaranteed to get opened"). (Patrick Dang, Joel Kaplan, Ian Koniak)
- **From-line beats subject line:** if subscribers trust your name, a weak subject still gets opened. `[2 src]` (Khe Hy, Robert W. Bly)
- *Negative swipe (flagged too me-focused):* "Increase your sales performance with [Your] Coaching." — generic, 5/10. (Ian Koniak)

## Cold email — openers & bodies
- **OPPS / OPS-CTA-PS body (Brian LaManna via Macnamara/Dressel):** Observation (buyer-focused, never start with "I") → Problem tied to it → one-sentence Solution → interest-based CTA → PS (most-read line). 50–150 words, mobile eye-test. `[5 src]` (Matt Macnamara, Patrick Dang, Daniel Fazio, Joel Kaplan, Trent Dressel — sourced 73 meetings)
- **2-sentence / <300-char cold email (Dressel):** S1 = "We provide [X] for [audience] to [benefit] — the ultimate advantage as it relates to [dream outcome]." S2 = interest CTA: "Can we find 20 minutes to review [current state] and discuss how [their co.] can benefit?" (Trent Dressel)
- **Time-respect humanizer open:** "I'll keep this short to make the 26 seconds it takes to read worth your time. (Yes, I timed it.)" (Trent Dressel)
- **Hyper-personalized exec email (Koniak):** (1) sincere compliment from real research → (2) "you're trying to go HERE" (their stated goal) → (3) "here's the challenge with how you do it today" (gap) → (4) "we can bridge it" (linkage: "our stuff helps your stuff") → (5) soft CTA. Cold-to-CEO variant leads with a *personal* hook: "Love what you said about changing how people eat out — as someone who lost 70 lbs, 500-calorie meals are my go-to." (Ian Koniak)
- **The 4-paragraph cold email (Tim Ferriss / Colin & Samir):** (1) "this'll take 60 seconds to read"; (2) ≤2 sentences who you are + why relevant TO THEM; (3) ONE specific one-handed question; (4) "even a one-line reply will make my day." (Colin and Samir)
- **The "Golden Email" (Joel Kaplan):** dead-simple, vague, curiosity-driven; sell the destination not the airplane ride; single-step CTA "reply yes with your best number"; add "reply no to be taken off the list."
- **Personal > personalized (Josh Braun):** *personalized* = unique to one prospect; *personal* = a true situation the whole segment faces ("Steve — end of the month, lots of commissions to pay"), so it scales. Use personal as the fallback when 1:1 research doesn't scale.
- **The "Why You, Why You Now" email (Jeff Hoffman via Barrows):** "Hey [name], I was on your website and noticed [trigger]. The reason that prompted me to reach out is many of our clients who have that use our solution to drive [result]. Who can I talk to about that?" Only the *first sentence* needs personalizing. `[corroborates the reason-for-call / Reason-Why opener]`
- **20% personalization rule (Jason Bay via Barrows):** a 20%-personalized email *beat* a 100%-personalized one on response rate — personalize only the first 1–3 sentences (tie to the trigger) and keep the value-prop standard. Value must carry context: not "here's a white paper" but "I heard you say X on your podcast — sharing because it's directly relevant." Corroborates *Personal > personalized*.
- **Frontload the personalization** so it isn't clipped in preview text: "Saw the Tacoma office opening on East Main St while researching you, Jason" beats burying it mid-email. The earned, specific opener signals "I reached out to you on purpose": "Congratulations on opening your newest locations in Ferndale, Michigan and starting the process of hiring + onboarding 10–15 baristas." (30MPC)
- **Competitor-displacement opener (skip your own company name):** "We see you're using [competitor X] — we sell ours 40% cheaper. Interested?" Leads with *their* current spend, not your brag. (Neville/MFM)
- **Case-study-mining line (lift verbatim):** skip the marketing page, read "customer success stories" — the before/after section is "the black-and-white version of the infomercial." (Josh Braun)
- Avoid entirely (instant "this is a cold email/AI" tells): "I hope this email finds you well" · "Quick question" · subject-line flattery · generic praise ("I admire how your team invests in pet wellness" = "sounds so AI"). `[4 src]` (Matt Macnamara, Patrick Dang, Daniel Fazio, 30MPC)

## Cold-call openers (verbatim, ranked)
- **Tailored Permission Opener — the single most-corroborated cold-call structure (Gong-best, 300M calls: 11.18% vs 2.15% for "did I catch you at a bad time"):** lead with something about THEM → own it's a cold call → ask permission. "[Name], I just finished reading the press release about your new office in Tacoma. I'll be honest — this is a cold call, but a well-researched one. Can I get 30 seconds on why I called, then you can totally hang up on me?" *Context-first is what breaks the telemarketer stigma.* Reason-I'm-calling variant: "The reason I'm calling you *specifically* is [you lead X / just promoted]…" `[9 src]` (Trent Dressel — dominant, Matt Macnamara, Patrick Dang, Rory Vaden, Ian Koniak, Josh Braun, 30MPC, Giulio Segantini, Armand Farrokh)
- **"Heard the name tossed around" opener (Gong 11.24%):** lead with a shared thread (investor / competitor / geography) *before* your name, dipped low tone + a shrug — "We work with a few other Andreessen portfolio companies — it's Armand from Pave, have you heard our name tossed around?" Goal: sound like they *should* know you, in 15 seconds. `[2 src]` (30MPC, Josh Braun)
- **Good-news/bad-news + permission:** "Good news — this is *not* about your car warranty expiring. Bad news — this is still indeed a cold call. Mind if I take 22 seconds, then you can hang up on me?" (30MPC)
- **Pattern-interrupt openers (Giulio):** "If I told you this is a sales call, would you smash your phone against the wall?" · "Am I going to ruin your Wednesday if I tell you this is a sales call?" (Giulio Segantini via 30MPC)
- **Familiar-tone open (Jeremy Miner):** never "Is this John?" (guard goes up) — "Nick, it's Jeremy… Jeremy Miner with XYZ. I was wondering if you could help me out for a moment?" Repeat the name twice, slight stutter/paper-shuffle; their brain loops on "how do I know this person?" (Jeremy Miner via 30MPC)
- **Permission / self-outing variant:** "You're probably going to hate me for this — this is actually a cold call. Want to hang up, or can I steal 30 seconds?" Delivered slow, warm, light laugh. (Matt Macnamara, Patrick Dang, Josh Braun)
- **Humility re-open for burned/dark accounts (Josh Braun):** "You have absolutely no idea who I am — my name is Josh with DocuSign and I'm just calling to apologize. Do you have a couple minutes?"
- **"How have you been?" beats "How are you?"** — manufactures false familiarity; Gong rated it a top opener. `[2 src]` (Matt Macnamara, Trent Dressel)
- **Disarm-the-stranger / name-the-cliché first (Josh Braun):** "I got your name from a B2B list — congrats on being list-worthy." · "We've never met, but I noticed something interesting about your post — hoping I could ask a couple quick questions."
- **Permission opener with relevance up front (verbatim):** "Hey Dave, it's Jason. I was calling about the recent plant you opened in Dallas… you got a minute for me to share the reason for my call?" Skip "this is X from [company]" (instant wall) — lead with the trigger. `[reinforces the Tailored Permission Opener]` (30MPC)
- **Voicemail — the double-tap formula:** lead with *context, not your name*, so it survives the auto-delete — "Hey Nick, we work with a few other Skadden partners in the LA office." Structure = relevance + social proof + a low-lift CTA pointing to the email: "…I emailed you, subject line 'estimates.' No need to call me back — mind replying if it's even moderately interesting?" Leave exactly two (VM1 ≈15s context only, VM2 ≈30s context + social proof). `[1 src]` (30MPC)
- **Reason-for-call built from a case-study result (Chris Orlob / John Barrows):** "Hey John, thanks for taking my call — real quick, the reason for my call is we're showing VPs of sales like you how to drive [sector result] with our solution; I just wanted to see if that's worth a deeper-dive conversation." **"The reason for my call is ___" is the most timeless opener** — humans crave reasons (Cialdini's "because"); if you can't finish that sentence, don't dial. Beats trendy, short-shelf-life openers. `[reinforces the Tailored Permission Opener]` (Chris Orlob, John Barrows)
- **Internal-referral cold open (the line execs actually open):** "Someone inside your company thought you might be interested in this." Implied warm intro beats any clever cold line. (Jonathan Lister via Barrows)
- **Hypothesis opener (Karen Kelly via Barrows):** "We're working with a lot of sales execs like you, and what we're seeing right now is X, Y, Z. My hunch is you're dealing with X — am I on the right track?" Give them a point of view to react to, not a blank question.
- **Story-based pain opener (replaces "what keeps you up at night?"):** list 3 specific persona pains, then "I don't suppose any of those sound familiar?" — they insert *themselves* into the story instead of being interrogated. (Dave Mattson/Sandler via Barrows)
- **No-oriented opener (Chris Voss via Barrows) — use with care:** "Is now a bad time?" / "Have I caught you in the middle of something?" A "no" makes people feel protected and in control, which dials them in — Barrows reports the "middle of something" variant gets the **second meeting ~80% of the time** (answer is almost always "No, but what's this about?" = full attention). ⚠️ *Caveat: Gong's data rates the near-identical "Did I catch you at a bad time?" the single WORST opener (2.15%) — the field conflicts on this exact line. The permission / reason-for-call opener out-performs both, so prefer it for a true cold open; reserve the no-oriented version for re-engaging an already-resistant prospect.*
- **Anti-patterns (worst openers, data-backed):** "How's your day going?" (Gong 7.6%) · "Did I catch you at a bad time?" (2.15% — "they'll say of course you did"). Both instantly flag *telemarketer*. `[4 src]` (Matt Macnamara, Patrick Dang, Trent Dressel, 30MPC)

## Sales page / VSL leads
- **Lead with the quantified result, never the mechanism:** "get 20 to 60 extra sales calls each month" beats "we do cold email." (Daniel Fazio)
- **Sell the transformation, not the pieces (Myron Golden):** "Nobody ever buys your product — they buy your *offer*: your description of the transformation they get when they say yes." Offer definition to swipe: "a valuable opportunity you give somebody the chance to pay for."
- **Before/After value hook (make the magical "after" concrete):** "You know how hard it is to get a cab in rush hour? With Uber you press a button and a black Mercedes takes you there without taking out your wallet." Paint the painful before, then a vivid, specific after — no product/feature talk. `[1 src]` (Josh Braun/30MPC)
- **John Carlton 3-step skeleton:** "Here's what I got. Here's what it'll do for you. Here's what I want you to do next." Benefits talk about the reader, not your credentials. `[2 src]` (Frank Kern, Stephen Pope)
- **Founder-letter launch page (no product shown):** big simple page + short letter — "things used to be a certain way → they've changed → it's gotten ridiculous, agree? → we'll fix it → if you want in, do this." Collects emails before a product exists; the hard part is having a real point of view. (Neville/MFM)
- **Visceral future-pacing first line (direct-mail classic):** "If you've got a special bottle of bubbly that you've been saving for celebrating great news, now is the time to open it up." (Maria Duvall letters, via Neville)
- **Envelope/curiosity-scarcity teaser:** "After you've sealed this envelope, we will be the only one to have ever opened it." + a fake "date/time received" box to fill in — drives opens via curiosity + implied scarcity. (Neville/MFM)
- **Reframe to reset perceived value:** SaaS "just a couple bucks a month" → reframe the buyer as a renter — "you can get evicted, you don't own it, they hike the rent, they own your data… you should own this by now." (Neville/MFM)
- **Re-enter-the-nightmare open:** "Think about the last time your computer crashed right before a deadline. Remember that sinking feeling? What happened next was probably a nightmare…" (Frank Kern)
- **Personal-revenue story hook:** "This email sequence made me $212,355 in one weekend. It was my 50th birthday and I had COVID at the time." (Frank Kern)
- **Aristotle's triangle for a long open letter:** ethos (credibility) + pathos (emotion) + logos (logic) — won a multi-million Delta deal. (Brandon Fluharty via Koniak)
- **VSL problem-agitate-solution stack with income ceilings (John Barrows):** walk through every flawed approach (spray-and-pray, mass-sequencing, referrals, brand-and-wait, legacy methods) and cap each with the income ceiling it produces ("$55–75K," "$60–80K"…), then position your named system as the only end-to-end fix. Each rejected path *raises* the stakes for the next.
- **4-step C-suite pitch / open letter (John Barrows, Voss-flavored):** (1) a polarizing belief that earns "hell yes" or "I disagree," (2) the stat proving the industry is changing, (3) your unique angle, (4) a story. First job is agreement that the *status quo* is the problem.
- **Authority / "still in it" credibility stack:** "30 years… trained Salesforce, LinkedIn, Amazon, Google, Dropbox, Box… featured in Forbes, Inc., HBR — and I'm still in it: I prospect every day, run my own discovery, negotiate my own contracts." The "still doing it" line differentiates you from gurus who "had one good run and aren't even selling anymore." (John Barrows)
- **Founder-letter all-inclusive offer line (kills "nickel-and-dimed"):** "Upfront, you get one price and everything is included — I'm going to standardize luxury." (Sandals, via MFM) — pair a one-price promise with a speed/service guarantee ("AC installed within 8 hours; if it breaks we fix it free, fast — nobody else offers you this").
- Swipe subheads: "Why terrible copy still wins with the right positioning" · "What if selling could actually be easy?" (Frank Kern)

## Ads / social hooks
- **Cost-narration (inflate value with time/money spent):** "This weekend I flew to Chicago and spent close to $20,000 to attend the Hormozi event — here are my top 3 takeaways." (Devin Jatho)
- **RSV (Result + Scene + Vehicle):** state the result, paint them *living* it, then your system. Stating the result lights up 2 brain regions; describing the lived experience lights up 7 (~3× engagement). (Devin Jatho)
- **Sell principles not specs (Steve Jobs / Myron Golden):** "1,000 songs in your pocket," not "5GB MP3 player." Benefit-as-name. (Myron Golden)
- **The 4 C's of a title (Myron Golden):** every strong title carries some of Curiosity / Contrast / Conflict / Controversy — "Why Evil People Are Rich" (2.7M views) hits all four. Open with "How/Why…" as a *statement* so the reader asks it of themselves.
- First 125 characters are the whole game on a caption — lead with a polarizing statement or a question they already have. (Vanessa Lau)
- **ROAS as a "magic box":** sell offer economics viscerally — "put $1 in, get $7 out." (Neville/MFM)
- **Front-load the hook ("4.2 seconds to hook everybody"):** after a click the retention curve "goes Space Mountain downwards" — open with the hook and flash visual proof in the first ~5 seconds. (Neville/MFM)
- **Winking caption that sells the dream without stating the taboo:** "Walk the dog. Relax. Water the flowers. Have a party with friends while your mouse does the work for you." (MFM)
- **Marketing-as-entertainment bar (the modern attention rule):** "Your little ad isn't competing against other beverages — it's competing against influencers and everything awesome in someone's feed." Never put something in the feed that "feels like marketing" — it must entertain or give value. In a commodity category ("most people assume water is the same") it becomes a *brand play* — "if we can make you laugh we have a way better chance at your $1.69 than the faceless brand shouting 'electrolytes.'" `[1 src]` (MFM)
- **Reframe what you're doing — find the deeper meaning in a "silly" product:** fake teeth reframed as "a permission slip to be the playful, fun version of yourself." Turns a gag into a mission you can sell. `[1 src]` (MFM)

## LinkedIn / DM openers
- **Warm DM (after they view/like/comment):** connect with NO message → "Hey, saw you visited my profile — curious, are you here for the content, or looking to [outcome]?" `[2 src]` (Patrick Dang, Matt Macnamara)
- **LinkedIn headline sells THEIR pain, not your title:** "Spending hours setting up your devices? Worried about employees on insecure Wi-Fi? Read this profile." beats "Account Executive at Gong." (Matt Macnamara)
- **Inbound DM with empathy + question (never cold-pitch):** "Hey [name], saw your post about feeling stuck — hope everything's okay. What's causing you to feel lost?" (Vanessa Lau)
- **Low-friction connect (Koniak/Leese):** "Hey [name], looking to connect with other [role]s in the industry — let me know if I can ever be helpful. [Name]" Blank requests convert best; wait two days, then a personalized voice/video message.
- **Competitor-switch icebreaker (booked meetings):** "Saw you're no longer at DocuSign — I guess we're not enemies anymore." (Sent to ex-DocuSign employees while selling PandaDoc.) Mine a job change for a disarming, true, slightly-cheeky first line. (Jed Mahrle via Barrows)

## CTAs & closes (swipeable)
- **Offer-based CTA beats asking for time (Gong, 85M emails):** time-ask ≈ **−44%**, interest ≈ +7%, **offer-based ≈ +28%**. Give a reason to meet even if they never buy: "We put together a couple campaign ideas to amplify your launch — open to seeing them?" / "Want to see how you benchmark against [peers]?" / "I'll tell you the 17 keywords you're missing — open to that?" `[3 src]` (Jason Bay/30MPC, Josh Braun, Vin Matano)
- **Ask for INTEREST, not for time:** "Does this sound like something worth exploring?" / "Want to know how?" / "Open to taking a peek?" — "Time is finite, interest is not." Low-friction language only — never "book a meeting / meet with a specialist." `[4 src]` (Matt Macnamara, Patrick Dang, Ian Koniak, Josh Braun, 30MPC)
- **Breakup email (lifts replies up to ~89%):** "I shared a few ways to cut your build times, but I'd hate to keep reaching out if we're not a fit — mind letting me know if this is relevant or not?" / "Would this be more relevant to Dave?" A *no*-oriented out + the easy exit. `[2 src]` (30MPC, Josh Braun)
- **One-line bump ("any thoughts?")** pulls reply rates up to 89% and equal to a personalized video; the champion-recap "How do you think it went?" reads like zero work, so they reply instantly. `[2 src]` (Josh Braun, 30MPC)
- **The two-outcome, risk-free meeting close:** "If we both see alignment we continue; if not, we part ways — how does that sound?" / "By the end you'll either feel more confident in what you have, or see a real opportunity." `[2 src]` (Trent Dressel — dominant, Ian Koniak)
- **The 7-word objection flip:** "That's exactly why we need to meet." Any price/turbulence/struggle objection becomes the reason to act. (Ian Koniak)
- **No-oriented ask (Chris Voss):** "Would it be a ridiculous idea if we found 30 minutes next week?" · "Have you given up on this project?" `[3 src]` (Matt Macnamara, Jose Rosado, Patrick Dang)
- **Payment-bypass close (Myron Golden):** "Most people pay with Visa, Mastercard, Amex, or Discover — which will you be using to get started today?" Then the embedded-command close: "If you could benefit sooner or later, would you choose later, or sooner?" (order so "sooner" lands last).
- **Assumed / either-or close:** "Which investment option sounds best — A or B?" / "Visa or Mastercard?" Both options are a yes. `[3 src]` (Hormozi via Macnamara, Joel Kaplan, Patrick Dang)
- **Price-then-silence:** state the price plainly, then "I'm going to shut up" — whoever talks first loses. `[3 src]` (Matt Macnamara, Jose Rosado, Patrick Dang)
- **Cost-of-NO close (Myron Golden):** "I don't emphasize how much it costs if they say yes — I emphasize how much it costs if they say *no*, and I make the cost of no higher than the cost of yes."
- **Cost-contrast close (anchor price against the quantified problem):** after stacking a $1.7M problem — "Do you want to pay now or later? My solution's only 30K." The price reads as trivial next to the number you just built. `[1 src]` (30MPC)
- **Time-comparison / break-it-to-pennies (Dan Kennedy / Bornancin):** "$49/mo → $1.61/day → 7 cents/hour" · "$100/license ÷ 30 days = $3/day." Compare to "stuff they'd buy anyway." `[2 src]` (Frank Kern, Brandon Bornancin)
- **Low-CTA / no-CTA (Josh Braun):** "If someone wants to respond, they know how — you don't tell them 'respond to my email.'"
- **CTA close (high-ticket):** "Congratulations on making it this far — now you just gotta bet on yourself and click the buy button." (Ian Koniak)
- **Free lead-magnet CTA (compile-and-give-away):** take everything a guest/expert said across all their appearances, "took all the best stuff and put it in one place… it's dense, it's a document, it's available now, it's totally free. Link in the description." Stack the value-density, then a zero-friction ask. (Scott Galloway via MFM)
- **Repeatable refrain close:** "That's what happens when you stop winging it and start executing with intention." A one-line drumbeat you can repeat after each proof point. (John Barrows)
- **Inbound-spam qualification gate (reverse-CTA):** "Send me a one-minute voice note or Loom on exactly why you can help my company, and I'll take your demo." ~9/10 never reply; the 10% who do convert — friction *is* the filter. (John Barrows)
- **Anti-pattern — don't bribe for time:** "$5 Starbucks card for 30 minutes" / "a penny for your thoughts" reads as desperate and *insulting* — "you're telling me my hourly rate is 20 bucks an hour." Lead with a real value-add (a relevant insight/audit), never a gift card. `[avoid]` (John Barrows, Kelly Cheng)

## Objection-flip lines (swipeable verbatim)
*The corpus is now objection-heavy; the frameworks live in `field-frameworks.md` (Disarm-before-logic, Isolate-the-objection). These are the verbatim lines.*
- **Diagnose the "no" with a take-away + multiple-choice (the most-repeated move):** "Shoot, this one's on me — you'd have reached out if you were interested. Just so nobody from my team calls you again, be brutally honest: already have something, the problem isn't happening, or you just hate cold calls — and it's totally fine if it's that last one." `[2 src]` (Josh Braun, 30MPC)
- **"Is that because you hate cold calls, or you just hate me?"** (50%+ self-distance and re-engage) → "Do you hate *all* cold calls, or just the bad ones?" (Giulio Segantini via 30MPC)
- **Sell the test drive, not the car:** "Honestly you're probably not buying this for 6 months — crazy idea, would you be completely against a quick look, not for now, just so you know it's out there when budget opens?" `[2 src]` (30MPC, Josh Braun)
- **"Send me an email":** "Totally — before I send it, mind if I ask one question so it's not totally irrelevant?… Is this just a polite way to tell me to go away?" An emailed follow-up books ~1 in 100, so book on the call; once they reply, *call.* (Josh Braun, 30MPC)
- **Price reframe (John Barrows):** "We're not the cheapest — nor do we want to be." / "Too expensive *compared to what* — my competitors, missing quota this quarter, or losing your top reps? And do you mean total cost of ownership or the number on the proposal?"
- **"You're more expensive" → best-problem/worst-problem:** "Being premium just makes quantifying ROI more important" — then tie the price gap to the size of their #1 initiative. (30MPC)
- **"What's your best price?" disarm:** "You're looking at it." (30MPC)
- **Label the price-obsession (strip-line):** "Sounds like price is your number one priority." They either disqualify themselves or surface what actually matters more than price. (30MPC)
- **Cheaper competitor → create a knowledge gap (Josh Braun):** "Manufacturers often cut cushion density — 1.2 lasts ~3–4 yrs vs 2.4 with coils ~8–10 yrs. What's the density of the cheaper one?" Hand the buyer a question their other vendor can't survive.
- **Humbling disclaimer to surface the real objection:** "I feel a little awkward asking, but what would be the top one or two reasons you wouldn't move forward, if any?" Lowers the guard before a hard question; reusable as an FAQ frame. `[2 src]` (30MPC, Muhlbauer)
- **Isolate price:** "Let's put price aside — assuming it was in budget, is this something you'd want to move forward with this week?" / "If this were *free*, could you see yourself using it?" (Josh Braun, 30MPC)
- **"We already have a vendor" (agree, then trap question):** "Totally get it — 9 times out of 10 it doesn't make sense to switch. Just so no one calls you again… you must not be doing any stock-option bonuses in your merit cycles, right?" (knowing their tool can't). (30MPC)
- **Price-then-silence / the "I don't know" hold:** state the number and stop ("if you explain price after giving it, you sound insecure"); asked to hold a price, never yes (deal slips) or no (looks desperate) — "I don't know, let's talk about it October 1st." (30MPC)
- **"This only works for B2C":** "There's still a person at the end of every B2B email, and they don't like being bored either" — salespeople saw the biggest income lift from DR tactics. (Neville Medhora)
- **"It won't scale":** "Everything can scale at least a little bit, and secondly — who cares." Don't let scalability anxiety kill a strong offer. (MFM)
- **"This style feels douchy/salesy":** you don't have to be wacky — the engine is education-first hitting real psychological points; serious, vulnerable, true stories ("I got robbed and felt violated") sell just as hard. (Neville Medhora)
- **Pre-empt the price objection by *owning* it (radical transparency):** "We're not the lowest price. If you pick the lowest, here are the problems that come with that service — but we will [do the job better]." Name your premium first; reusable as an FAQ frame. `[corroborates Risk-Reversal radical-transparency]` (MFM)
- **Disarm "this is snake oil / a scam" by being human:** "I feel like there are real human beings behind this brand that I'd want to have a beer with, so I'd rather give them my money." Likability beats spec-shouting. (MFM)
- **Childlike-curiosity objection meet (Hormozi):** "Huh, that's so interesting — tell me more," then ask questions that lead the prospect to their own conclusion. Never confront.
- **Spouse/partner-won't-approve (Hormozi Socratic, never attack the third party):** "Why wouldn't he? → He wants what's best for you, right? → Does he know you're struggling with this? → Would he be happier if you kept struggling? → Then would you be opposed to moving forward today?"
- **"We need a trial / to evaluate vendors" (Dustin Crawford):** "Nobody actually wants to evaluate three vendors — what do we need to do to button this up by end of month?" Then pre-empt the *stated* reason (e.g., send a screenshot of the integration they wanted to test) so the excuse dissolves.
- **"We don't have the budget" → label, don't interrogate (Voss):** they've told you one of two things — under pressure, or your value isn't landing. Respond "Sounds like the value's just not here for you," NOT "help me understand what you mean by no budget."
- **"Take me off your list" save (Jason Bay):** reply "Sure," share a genuinely helpful resource, then "Is there anything I could've done to make this more helpful? Any feedback?" People respond, and you learn.
- **Reframe "no" as the opening (Vince Beese):** "No means I have an opening to start selling — now I know their position and can anchor to it. The 'I don't know / maybe' is the real garbage. Tell me no, and let's go."
- **"We're all set" → agree + self-deprecate to break the pattern (Armand/Nick via Barrows):** "Totally get it — I should've assumed you'd have something in place… honestly I'm almost surprised you didn't just hang up on me." Earns 20–30 more seconds.
- **"Where'd you get my number?" → honesty + benefit + redirect (Barrows):** "We use list services — happy to email them so you can take yourself off. Meantime, open to a minute on why I'm actually calling?"
- **"Is this a sales call?" → disarm with uncertainty (Barrows):** "I don't know yet — I don't even know if you need what I sell, but you fit the profile of clients we do great work with. Give me a few minutes of questions and we'll both find out if it makes sense."
- **Preemptive strike — surface the objection yourself (Colleen Stanley via Barrows):** "I'm guessing you think we're way too expensive — am I on the right path?" → "now it's not an objection, it's a conversation." (Reusable as an FAQ opener.)
- **Detachment-as-leverage close (Karen Kelly via Barrows):** "If I can only do a *little* better than what you're doing now, we shouldn't be talking. If I'm *much* better, I'll push hard. And if I'm not — I'll be the first to tell you to get out of here."
- **"Why hire you when I can build it myself (with AI)?" reframe (Kris Lawson via Barrows):** "That's not what you get paid for — do you really want to deal with boring infrastructure when you should be building software that makes your company millions?" (Directly answers the dominant 2025–26 build-vs-buy objection.)
- **"Permission to tell me no" (Doug Landis via Barrows):** "Yes is the best answer in sales, no is the second-best — it's a 'maybe' that turns me into the stalker neither of us wants." Relaxes the prospect and hands you a callback hook.
