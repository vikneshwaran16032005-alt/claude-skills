## Headlines / hooks (ranked by reusable pattern)

**Top patterns (recur across many creators — use these first):**
- **Result + specificity + timeframe + "you" / removed-objection.** "$44,000 in just 18 days without any sales calls or paid ads" · "get 30 qualified calls in the next 30 days or you don't pay" · "[Program] Helps A Woman Turn $297 Into $25K In 3 Days" · "I increased my income from $77,000 to $302,000." The number IS the hook. `[9+ src]` (Stephen Pope, Daniel Fazio, Patrick Dang, Joel Kaplan, Frank Kern, Matt Macnamara, Myron Golden, Trent Dressel, Ian Koniak)
- **Outcome + parenthetical objection-killer.** "She Earns $500K+ Every Year In Tech Sales (Without Working Nights & Weekends)" · "How To Make Millions From Social Media — Even With Zero Followers" · "Land a $70,000/yr tech-sales job in 3 weeks — no degree, no experience, no connections." Kill the reader's #1 objection *inside the headline*. `[4+ src]` (Ian Koniak, Trent Dressel, Myron Golden, Patrick Dang)
- **Before→after rags-to-riches (specific low state → specific high state).** "From Homeless Shelter to $1M in Tech Sales" · "From McDonald's to $120k/yr in Tech Sales at 18" · "From the Trash Man to the Cash Man" · "From Burned Out at 55 to Best in Company at 58." Specific dollars + age/origin = proof + relatability ("if I can, you can"). `[5+ src]` (Ian Koniak, Trent Dressel, Myron Golden, Patrick Dang, Matt Macnamara)
- **Contrarian / self-implicating pattern-interrupt.** "Sales influencers (including me) are completely full of sh*t" · "Cardone Capital Fees EXPOSED" · "It's your fault you're missing your number" (then: "…most likely it's not your fault") · "Time is money is a very expensive lie." Attacking your own category/claim disarms; Outbrain's 65,000-ad study: contrarian ads got 63% more clicks. `[6+ src]` (Matt Macnamara, Frank Kern, Daniel Fazio, Patrick Dang, Grant Cardone, Ian Koniak, Myron Golden)
- **"Stop [obvious thing]. [Counterintuitive thing] instead."** "Stop Selling Software. Sell Discovery Instead." · "Stop Loaning Your Money To The Bank For Free." Clean pattern-interrupt title formula. `[2 src]` (Ian Koniak, Myron Golden)
- **The "REAL reason" / "secret reason" debunk.** "The REAL Reason Tech Sellers Fail (And What To Do About It)" · "The Secret Reason Your Business Is Losing Money (It's Not What You Think)." Promise to overturn the obvious explanation; the dismissive parenthetical adds curiosity. `[2 src]` (Ian Koniak, Myron Golden)
- **Lead-magnet headline formula (swipe verbatim).** "The 5 biggest mistakes [married couples / doctors / plumbers] make that lead to [bankruptcy]" · "The 7 things you should never say to your spouse or you'll end up in divorce court." Lead *away from pain* for cold traffic. `[2 src]` (Myron Golden, Chet Holmes)
- **"[time/effort] away from [dream end state]."** "10 Minutes Away From Never Caring About Rejection Again" + opener "In the next 10 minutes you'll have all the tools to overcome all rejection." `[1 src]` (Trent Dressel)
- **Dream-outcome-without-the-nightmare title.** "How to buy the house of your dreams without paying the price of your nightmares." Swipeable for any niche. `[1 src]` (Myron Golden)
- **The "firstborn-child" / hospital-corridor test.** Write a headline so strong a prospect sprinting to the hospital for the birth of their first child would stop to read it — outrageous yet factually true. `[1 src]` (Frank Kern)

## Subject lines

**Cold-email subject lines (corroborated rules):** 1–3 words, lowercase, vague + emotional or loss-framed so they think "*what*?" — "this sucks", "headache", "out of breath", "losing winnable deals", "mitigate IT risk". Lowercase reads friendly; Title Case reads spammy. Tune the loss to the persona. `[4+ src]` (Matt Macnamara, Patrick Dang, Daniel Fazio, Trent Dressel, Josh Braun)
- **Name the ask outright (high open rate):** "[First Name] - Meeting Request" (claimed 41% open) · "Are you doubling down on [their goal]?" (claimed ~60% open). (Trent Dressel)
- **The "Triplet" (Josh Braun):** expected + expected + UNexpected — "iPhones, Verizon, my girlfriend"; first line ties all three. (Matt Macnamara)
- **Ghost / re-engagement (highest reply rate) — psychology of quitting:** "did i lose you" · "Have you given up on this project?" · "Are you alive?" · empty body, replied to the original thread. People resist feeling like quitters. `[3 src]` (Matt Macnamara, Frank Kern, Ian Koniak)
- **Pattern-interrupt / shared-history:** "[First name] — remember us from [past company]?" · a name tied to the recipient's known hobby ("LaDainian Tomlinson"). (Trent Dressel)
- **Plug their own category/initiative in:** "Digital marketing solutions for your private school" · put the prospect's internal project/code-name in the subject ("almost guaranteed to get opened"). (Patrick Dang, Joel Kaplan, Ian Koniak)
- **From-line beats subject line:** if subscribers trust your name, a weak subject still gets opened. `[2 src]` (Khe Hy, Robert W. Bly)
- *Negative swipe (flagged too me-focused):* "Increase your sales performance with [Your] Coaching." — generic, 5/10. (Ian Koniak)

## Cold email — openers & bodies
- **OPPS / OPS-CTA-PS body (Brian LaManna via Macnamara/Dressel):** Observation (buyer-focused, never start with "I") → Problem tied to it → one-sentence Solution → interest-based CTA → PS (most-read line). 50–150 words, mobile eye-test. `[5 src]` (Matt Macnamara, Patrick Dang, Daniel Fazio, Joel Kaplan, Trent Dressel — sourced 73 meetings)
- **2-sentence / <300-char cold email (Dressel):** S1 = "We provide [X] for [audience] to [benefit] — the ultimate advantage as it relates to [dream outcome]." S2 = interest CTA: "Can we find 20 minutes to review [current state] and discuss how [their co.] can benefit?" (Trent Dressel)
- **Time-respect humanizer open:** "I'll keep this short to make the 26 seconds it takes to read worth your time. (Yes, I timed it.)" (Trent Dressel)
- **Hyper-personalized exec email (Koniak):** (1) sincere compliment from real research → (2) "you're trying to go HERE" (their stated goal) → (3) "here's the challenge with how you do it today" (gap) → (4) "we can bridge it" (linkage: "our stuff helps your stuff") → (5) soft CTA. Cold-to-CEO variant leads with a *personal* hook: "Love what you said about changing how people eat out — as someone who lost 70 lbs, 500-calorie meals are my go-to." (Ian Koniak)
- **The 4-paragraph cold email (Tim Ferriss / Colin & Samir):** (1) "this'll take 60 seconds to read"; (2) ≤2 sentences who you are + why relevant TO THEM; (3) ONE specific one-handed question; (4) "even a one-line reply will make my day." (Colin and Samir)
- **The "Golden Email" (Joel Kaplan):** dead-simple, vague, curiosity-driven; sell the destination not the airplane ride; single-step CTA "reply yes with your best number"; add "reply no to be taken off the list."
- Avoid entirely: "Dear [Name], I hope this email finds you well / in these trying times." `[3 src]` (Matt Macnamara, Patrick Dang, Daniel Fazio)

## Cold-call openers (verbatim, ranked)
- **The reason-I'm-calling + permission micro-ask — the single most-corroborated cold-call structure:** "Hey [Name], this is [You] from [Company], how are you?" → "The reason I'm calling you *specifically* is [you lead X / just promoted / I sent you an email]" → "I know I'm calling out of the blue — can I steal 30 seconds to tell you why?" (~80–90% earn it). Humans need a *reason*; it settles the subconscious "who is this / why me." `[5 src]` (Trent Dressel — dominant, Matt Macnamara, Patrick Dang, Rory Vaden, Ian Koniak)
- **Permission / self-outing variant:** "You're probably going to hate me for this — this is actually a cold call. Want to hang up, or can I steal 30 seconds?" Delivered slow, warm, light laugh. (Matt Macnamara, Patrick Dang)
- **"How have you been?" beats "How are you?"** — manufactures false familiarity; Gong (90,000-call study) rated it the top opener, ~6× call success. `[2 src]` (Matt Macnamara, Trent Dressel)
- **The mood-tell diagnostic:** ask "how are you?" and *pause* — if they ask back, you're in; if not, get to the point fast. (Trent Dressel)
- **Disarm-the-stranger / name-the-cliché first (Josh Braun):** "I know, I know — another wizard telling you what to do…" · "I got your name from a B2B list — congrats on being list-worthy." · "Have you ever cold-called someone and they told you to f-off? Yep, happens." (Josh Braun)
- **Anti-patterns (worst openers):** "Hey [name] it's [you] with IBM, how's your day going?" · "Did I catch you at a bad time?" ("they'll say of course you did"). `[3 src]` (Matt Macnamara, Patrick Dang, Trent Dressel)

## Sales page / VSL leads
- **Lead with the quantified result, never the mechanism:** "get 20 to 60 extra sales calls each month" beats "we do cold email." (Daniel Fazio)
- **Sell the transformation, not the pieces (Myron Golden):** "Nobody ever buys your product — they buy your *offer*: your description of the transformation they get when they say yes." Offer definition to swipe: "a valuable opportunity you give somebody the chance to pay for."
- **John Carlton 3-step skeleton:** "Here's what I got. Here's what it'll do for you. Here's what I want you to do next." Benefits talk about the reader, not your credentials. `[2 src]` (Frank Kern, Stephen Pope)
- **Re-enter-the-nightmare open:** "Think about the last time your computer crashed right before a deadline. Remember that sinking feeling? What happened next was probably a nightmare…" (Frank Kern)
- **Personal-revenue story hook:** "This email sequence made me $212,355 in one weekend. It was my 50th birthday and I had COVID at the time." (Frank Kern)
- **Aristotle's triangle for a long open letter:** ethos (credibility) + pathos (emotion) + logos (logic) — won a multi-million Delta deal. (Brandon Fluharty via Koniak)
- Swipe subheads: "Why terrible copy still wins with the right positioning" · "What if selling could actually be easy?" (Frank Kern)

## Ads / social hooks
- **Cost-narration (inflate value with time/money spent):** "This weekend I flew to Chicago and spent close to $20,000 to attend the Hormozi event — here are my top 3 takeaways." (Devin Jatho)
- **RSV (Result + Scene + Vehicle):** state the result, paint them *living* it, then your system. Stating the result lights up 2 brain regions; describing the lived experience lights up 7 (~3× engagement). (Devin Jatho)
- **Sell principles not specs (Steve Jobs / Myron Golden):** "1,000 songs in your pocket," not "5GB MP3 player." Benefit-as-name. (Myron Golden)
- **The 4 C's of a title (Myron Golden):** every strong title carries some of Curiosity / Contrast / Conflict / Controversy — "Why Evil People Are Rich" (2.7M views) hits all four. Open with "How/Why…" as a *statement* so the reader asks it of themselves.
- First 125 characters are the whole game on a caption — lead with a polarizing statement or a question they already have. (Vanessa Lau)

## LinkedIn / DM openers
- **Warm DM (after they view/like/comment):** connect with NO message → "Hey, saw you visited my profile — curious, are you here for the content, or looking to [outcome]?" `[2 src]` (Patrick Dang, Matt Macnamara)
- **LinkedIn headline sells THEIR pain, not your title:** "Spending hours setting up your devices? Worried about employees on insecure Wi-Fi? Read this profile." beats "Account Executive at Gong." (Matt Macnamara)
- **Inbound DM with empathy + question (never cold-pitch):** "Hey [name], saw your post about feeling stuck — hope everything's okay. What's causing you to feel lost?" (Vanessa Lau)
- **Low-friction connect (Koniak/Leese):** "Hey [name], looking to connect with other [role]s in the industry — let me know if I can ever be helpful. [Name]" Blank requests convert best; wait two days, then a personalized voice/video message.

## CTAs & closes (swipeable)
- **Ask for INTEREST, not for time:** "Does this sound like something worth exploring?" / "Want to know how?" / "Interested?" — "Time is finite, interest is not." Out-converts "Are you free Wednesday at 2 or 4?" `[3 src]` (Matt Macnamara, Patrick Dang, Ian Koniak, Josh Braun)
- **The two-outcome, risk-free meeting close:** "If we both see alignment we continue; if not, we part ways — how does that sound?" / "By the end you'll either feel more confident in what you have, or see a real opportunity." `[2 src]` (Trent Dressel — dominant, Ian Koniak)
- **The 7-word objection flip:** "That's exactly why we need to meet." Any price/turbulence/struggle objection becomes the reason to act. (Ian Koniak)
- **No-oriented ask (Chris Voss):** "Would it be a ridiculous idea if we found 30 minutes next week?" · "Have you given up on this project?" `[3 src]` (Matt Macnamara, Jose Rosado, Patrick Dang)
- **Payment-bypass close (Myron Golden):** "Most people pay with Visa, Mastercard, Amex, or Discover — which will you be using to get started today?" Then the embedded-command close: "If you could benefit sooner or later, would you choose later, or sooner?" (order so "sooner" lands last).
- **Assumed / either-or close:** "Which investment option sounds best — A or B?" / "Visa or Mastercard?" Both options are a yes. `[3 src]` (Hormozi via Macnamara, Joel Kaplan, Patrick Dang)
- **Price-then-silence:** state the price plainly, then "I'm going to shut up" — whoever talks first loses. `[3 src]` (Matt Macnamara, Jose Rosado, Patrick Dang)
- **Cost-of-NO close (Myron Golden):** "I don't emphasize how much it costs if they say yes — I emphasize how much it costs if they say *no*, and I make the cost of no higher than the cost of yes."
- **Time-comparison / break-it-to-pennies (Dan Kennedy / Bornancin):** "$49/mo → $1.61/day → 7 cents/hour" · "$100/license ÷ 30 days = $3/day." Compare to "stuff they'd buy anyway." `[2 src]` (Frank Kern, Brandon Bornancin)
- **Low-CTA / no-CTA (Josh Braun):** "If someone wants to respond, they know how — you don't tell them 'respond to my email.'"
- **CTA close (high-ticket):** "Congratulations on making it this far — now you just gotta bet on yourself and click the buy button." (Ian Koniak)
