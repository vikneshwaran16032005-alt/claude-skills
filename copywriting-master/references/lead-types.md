# Lead Types + Schwartz's 7 Techniques + 38 Verbalizations

The "lead" is the first 400-800 words of a sales letter (or first 10% of any long-form piece). Masterson and Forde call it "the architecture of persuasion" — it does 80% of the emotional work. Match the lead type to the prospect's awareness stage (see `awareness-sophistication.md`) or the copy will bounce.

---

## The 6 Lead Types (Masterson / Forde — *Great Leads*)

### 1. OFFER lead
**Use for:** Most-aware readers.
**Structure:** Open with the deal. Price, discount, urgency, reason-why.

**Example:**
> Early Black Friday deal — 85% off a hand-selected collection of 17 private market trainings. Available right now, this week only. [LINK]

**Works because:** Most-aware prospects know the product. Giving them more product-description is a waste of their time. Give them the deal.

### 2. PROMISE lead
**Use for:** Most-aware (strengthening desire) or Product-aware / Solution-aware (demonstrating the product can deliver).
**Structure:** Open with the biggest, most specific, most believable benefit the product delivers.

**Examples:**
- "At 60 mph, the loudest noise in a Rolls-Royce is the electric clock." (Ogilvy — implied: this car is impossibly quiet)
- "How to win friends and influence people" (Carnegie — literal promise in the title)
- "Lose 20 pounds in 30 days without giving up carbs" (explicit promise with specificity)

**Works because:** the prospect already knows they want this outcome — you're just making it feel achievable and specific.

### 3. PROBLEM-SOLUTION lead
**Use for:** Problem-aware or Solution-aware readers.
**Structure:** Empathize with the pain FIRST. Demonstrate you understand it. THEN introduce the solution.

**Example:**
> You've tried everything for your dog's joint pain. Every supplement shelf, every vet visit, every "miracle" cream. Nothing works. Your dog still limps. Still won't play. Still cries when getting up. This is the video I wish I'd seen 3 years ago...

**Works because:** empathy lowers defenses. Without the empathy step, the reader rejects the solution as another empty claim.

### 4. SECRET lead
**Use for:** Problem-aware (where readers feel stuck) or Solution-aware (where readers don't know WHICH solution works).
**Structure:** Reveal a hidden fact / insider knowledge / suppressed information that explains why the reader has been failing.

**Example:**
> There's a reason your keto diet stopped working after week 3. It's not your willpower. It's not your workouts. It's a 12,000-year-old hormone your body produces when you hit a specific fat percentage — and it's designed to sabotage you...

**Works because:** positions the problem as not-the-reader's-fault and positions you as the holder of privileged information. Both move the needle.

### 5. PROCLAMATION lead
**Use for:** Unaware or Problem-aware readers you need to wake up.
**Structure:** Bold, confident, often counter-intuitive statement. Usually with a numeric or news-worthy flavor.

**Examples:**
- "The financial crisis of 2026 has already begun. You haven't heard about it yet because it started in a corner of the market the media ignores."
- "By 2030, 40% of American jobs will be performed by AI. Here's how to be in the other 60%."
- "You are not running out of time. You're running out of leverage."

**Works because:** creates a "wait, what?" moment that breaks the scroll-pattern. Useful when the prospect isn't actively seeking a solution.

### 6. STORY lead
**Use for:** Unaware readers (hardest case) or to build deep emotional resonance with any reader.
**Structure:** Tell a specific, relatable, human story. Build identification with the protagonist. Reveal the "aha moment" that maps to your product.

**Example:**
> Three weeks after the funeral, I found the shoebox in the back of my dad's closet. Inside was a folded yellow envelope and a note: "If you're reading this, you finally need it." I opened the envelope and — Jesus Christ, $184,000 in cashier's checks...

**Works because:** stories bypass rational defenses. Readers who would reject a direct claim accept the same claim wrapped in a story.

### Lead-type selection by awareness

| Awareness | Primary lead type | Backup options |
|---|---|---|
| Most-aware | Offer | Promise |
| Product-aware | Promise | Problem-Solution, Offer (if big discount) |
| Solution-aware | Promise OR Problem-Solution | Secret |
| Problem-aware | Problem-Solution OR Secret | Story |
| Unaware | Proclamation OR Story | (no direct options work) |

## Schwartz's 7 Techniques of Breakthrough Copy

Body-copy tools. Most useful for Product-aware / Solution-aware readers in sophisticated markets (Stage 3+).

### 1. Intensification
Make the existing desire feel stronger. Use vivid sensory detail, worst-case consequences, best-case rewards. "Imagine waking up tomorrow with..." / "Picture yourself 6 months from now..."

### 2. Identification
Frame the product as "for people like you" — the reader's in-group, values, lifestyle, profession, aspirations.

### 3. Gradualization
Never jump from the reader's current belief to the final belief in one step. Chain smaller beliefs each fully accepted. "First, let's agree that [obvious]. If that's true, then [slightly less obvious]. And if THAT's true, then [the big claim]."

### 4. Redefinition
Recast a familiar thing as something unfamiliar — new framing, new name, new context. "This isn't a diet. It's a hormone-reset protocol." "This isn't financial planning. It's sovereignty engineering."

### 5. Mechanization (new mechanism)
Explain HOW the product works via a specific mechanism — preferably one the reader hasn't heard. "The gut bacterium that controls fat storage" > "a probiotic that helps digestion". See Stage 3 of sophistication.

### 6. Concentration
Strip everything non-essential. One benefit, one mechanism, one promise — all others stripped. Used when the market is overwhelmed by too many competing claims.

### 7. Camouflage
Dress the sales message as something else: editorial content, a personal letter, a news article, a gift. Halbert's $40M Coat-of-Arms letter was a letter with a Polaroid attached, not an ad. Editorial look beats ad look.

## Schwartz's 38 Headline Verbalizations

When stuck on a headline, run through these. Each generates a usable headline by plugging in the relevant specifics of the product and audience.

### The list (abbreviated — full list in *Breakthrough Advertising*)
1. **Announcement** — "Announcing the new [product]..."
2. **News** — "New [discovery/device] now lets you..."
3. **How-to** — "How to [achieve desire] without [pain]"
4. **Question** — "Do you [have problem]?"
5. **Command** — "Get the [benefit] you deserve"
6. **Why** — "Why [X] — and what you can do about it"
7. **Reason** — "3 reasons [target audience] should [action]"
8. **Testimonial** — "Why I [achieved benefit] after [length of struggle]"
9. **Offer** — "[Price] — [product] — [bonus]"
10. **Intrigue** — "The [noun] that [counter-intuitive verb]"
11. **Warning** — "Before you [common action], read this"
12. **Urgency** — "[Deadline]: [consequence of missing]"
13. **Guarantee** — "[Bold promise] — or your money back"
14. **Challenge** — "Can you [difficult thing]? Here's how."
15. **Secret** — "The [adjective] secret of [authority] — revealed"
16. **Curiosity gap** — "What [authority] knows about [topic] that you don't"
17. **Story** — "How [I/character] went from [bad state] to [good state]"
18. **Before/after** — "From [bad state] to [good state] in [time]"
19. **Specific number** — "[Number] ways to [achieve benefit]"
20. **Dangling benefit** — "[Benefit] — without [common sacrifice]"
21. **If...then** — "If you're [condition], you need to [action]"
22. **You vs. them** — "What [successful group] does differently"
23. **Prediction** — "By [date], [prediction]. Here's how to prepare."
24. **Mistake** — "The [common mistake] almost everyone makes"
25. **Method** — "The [method name] that [benefit]"
26. **Little-known fact** — "The [fact] no one tells you about [topic]"
27. **Comparison** — "[Product] vs. [competitor]: here's which wins"
28. **Enemy / villain** — "Why [authority/industry] doesn't want you to know about [topic]"
29. **Confession** — "I [embarrassing admission]. Here's what I learned."
30. **Rhetorical question** — "Tired of [common pain]?"
31. **Statistic** — "[Surprising statistic]. Here's why it matters."
32. **Definitive list** — "[Number] things [target audience] should never [action]"
33. **Promise of ease** — "The easy way to [achieve difficult thing]"
34. **Promise of speed** — "[Benefit] in [short timeframe]"
35. **Promise of simplicity** — "[Benefit] — no [complexity] required"
36. **Trend / movement** — "The [movement/trend] changing how [target] [verb]"
37. **Mystery** — "[Intriguing phrase] — what it is and why it matters"
38. **Specific individual** — "How [real person] [achieved benefit]"

### How to use
Generate 10-20 headlines using different verbalizations. Pick the 3 that best match the lead type and awareness stage. A/B test if possible.

## Halbert's specific attention-grab tactics for direct mail

Not headline formulas — physical / visual tactics that made Halbert's envelopes earn the A-pile:
- Attach a dollar bill to the top of the letter (Collier's Dollar Bill Letter — 90%+ response)
- Attach a relevant coin (Japanese penny for a Japanese product, Mexican peso for inflation pitch, Iraqi dinar for currency collapse pitch)
- Attach a baggie of dirt (for a Hawaiian real estate pitch)
- Add "(over)" and "(go to page 7)" directives — lead the reader by the hand
- Use a day/date/time stamp above the salutation for urgency
- Personal salutation ("Dear Bond") never "Dear Occupant"
- First-class stamp, handwritten address, no teaser copy — editorial look always beats ad look

These translate to digital: email subject lines that look personal, not marketing. Sender-from-a-real-name. Minimal formatting. Lead with a physical or emotional hook, not the offer.
