# The 3 Levels of Editing (Sean McIntyre)

Most copyriters think editing = fixing typos and smoothing sentences. Wrong. There are three distinct editing layers that operate on completely different scales. You must do them **in reverse order of obviousness** — hardest/most impactful first, easiest/least impactful last.

This protocol is how Sean and the Copy That crew edit. It mirrors how pro copy chiefs at financial publishers and direct-response agencies edit.

---

## The order (non-negotiable)

1. **Value edits FIRST** — is the idea good enough?
2. **Function edits SECOND** — is the structure right?
3. **Line edits LAST** — is each sentence sharp?

### Why this order matters

If the IDEA is weak, no amount of structure or sentence polish will save the copy. "A polished turd is still a turd" (Sean). Sean's rule: if a piece fails the value test, STOP. Do not proceed to function or line edits. Go back and rethink the big idea.

If the STRUCTURE is wrong, your brilliant sentences will be buried in the wrong places and the reader will bounce before reaching them. Fix the skeleton before fixing the skin.

Line edits — the thing most newbies spend all their time on — are the LEAST impactful. Once you're billing clients, this is the layer to outsource.

---

## Level 1: Value Edits

### The question to ask
"Is the idea good enough? Is the argument compelling? Is it new, different, unique? Would a well-read prospect think 'I've seen this before'?"

The moment a reader thinks "I've seen this before" → you've lost them. Value editing is where you brutally assess whether the foundational concept justifies the reader's attention.

### How to value-edit

1. **Read the big idea in one sentence.** If you can't, the piece doesn't have one. Write one. Test if it holds the piece.
2. **Sniff-test for clichés.** Would 10 other pieces in this niche have the same idea? If yes → not good enough.
3. **Sniff-test for specificity.** Does the idea rest on a specific mechanism / insight / angle that the reader hasn't encountered? Or is it a retread of the market's standard claims?
4. **Sniff-test for emotional weight.** Does the idea touch a nerve, a hope, a yearning? Or does it just... inform?

If any sniff-test fails → rewrite the big idea. Don't proceed to other edits.

### Tactics to ADD value when a piece fails

#### Frame the idea as NEW
- Attach an origin story — "I developed this after [transformation]"
- Position it as insider information — "the [method] industry insiders use but never discuss"
- Frame it as a recent discovery — "new research from [authority] reveals..."

#### Systematize a basic claim
Turn generic benefits into named "systems" with stories:
- BAD: "leak-proof flap"
- GOOD: "Triple-Knock Anti-Tsunami System" (Huggies real copy)
- BAD: "lose weight by cutting food and exercising"
- GOOD: "shed buckets of fat with my unique Shin-Bone Method in less than 44 days"

The system doesn't need to be elaborate. A NAME + a few distinguishing features is enough to make a commodity claim feel novel.

#### Introduce a new mechanism (Schwartz stage 3+)
If the market is saturated with "lose weight fast" claims, introduce a mechanism the reader hasn't heard:
- "The Japanese tea ceremony ritual that triggers fat-melting" (identification + mechanism)
- "The gut bacterium that controls fat storage" (mechanism)
- "The hormone your body produces at week 3 of any diet — and how to bypass it" (mechanism + insider knowledge)

#### Pivot to identification
If claims and mechanisms are exhausted (Schwartz stage 5), stop describing what the product does and describe WHO it's for:
- "For the mom who's done watching her kids eat junk"
- "For the solopreneur who refuses to build a 'lifestyle business'"

### When to STOP and rewrite
If after reasonable effort you still can't frame the idea as new/unique/compelling — the problem is at the RESEARCH level, not the writing level. Go back to Phase 1 and find a different angle.

## Level 2: Function Edits

### The question to ask
"Is the structure right? Does every section tie to the golden thread? Does the copy move the reader from hook to close without losing them?"

### How to function-edit

#### Reverse-outline the draft
Read the draft. For each section / paragraph, write down WHAT it's doing (not what it says). Examples:
- "Hook: curiosity gap about weight-loss failures"
- "Credibility: establish author authority"
- "Problem: reveal hidden mechanism causing weight gain"
- "Proof: medical study reference"
- "Objection handling: cost concern"
- "Offer: pricing + bonuses"
- "Close: CTA + urgency"

#### Check the golden thread
The golden thread is the single idea/claim that runs through the piece. Every section should tie to it. Cut anything that doesn't.

**Common failures:**
- Tangent paragraphs that are "interesting" but don't advance the argument → CUT
- Proof for claims you haven't made yet → MOVE
- Objection-handling for an objection the reader won't have → CUT
- Multiple competing ideas trying to share the spotlight → PICK ONE

#### Check the order
Ask: "If a skimmer reads only the headline, the first paragraph, the bullets, and the close — do they get the full argument?" Skim-readers are the majority. Your structure must work for both deep-reading and skimming.

Common reorders:
- Move the biggest proof closer to the claim (proof 3 paragraphs later = wasted proof)
- Move the strongest benefit earlier (prospects bounce within 15% of a page)
- Move objection-handling closer to where the objection naturally arises in the reader's mind
- Split a single long section into two smaller ones for readability

#### Fix transitions
Between each section, does the reader naturally move to the next? If there's friction, add a transition phrase (see `writing-mechanics.md`).

### When function-editing is done
The piece reads continuously. You can skim-read it and still get the argument. Every section is earning its place.

## Level 3: Line Edits

### The question to ask
"Is each sentence doing its job as cleanly and sharply as possible?"

### How to line-edit

#### Read aloud
Stumble spots = rewrite spots. Punctuation weirdness = rewrite spots. Sentences where you lose breath = too long; split them.

This is the single best line-editing tactic. It catches more problems than any other technique.

#### Remove weak verbs
Replace forms of "to be" / "helps" / "does" with stronger, more specific verbs when possible.

- ❌ "Our product helps with joint pain"
- ✅ "Our product eliminates joint pain" / "destroys joint pain" / "fixes joint pain at the source"

- ❌ "Is a company that makes..."
- ✅ "Makes..." (cut the filler construction)

Caution: don't over-use intense verbs. Save them for where emphasis is needed. "Annihilates" works once; not twenty times.

#### Vary sentence length
Mix short (3-8 words) with medium (15-20 words). Avoid long (25+) in body copy.

Pattern to avoid: 5 sentences in a row at the same length. Feels robotic.

Good pattern: short. Medium. Short. Longer sentence that flows because the previous short one built the expectation. Short.

#### Cut "salesy" tone
If the copy sounds like a press release, something is overwrought. Usually one of:
- Adjective stacking: "revolutionary, cutting-edge, world-class"
- Weak qualifier words: "really", "truly", "very", "actually" (most are removable)
- Industry jargon: "solutions", "leverage", "synergize", "utilize"
- Aggrandizing the product: "amazing", "incredible", "game-changing"

Replace with specifics. "Revolutionary" → "first of its kind since 2007" or whatever specific fact backs the claim.

#### Cut filler
Words that add no meaning:
- "It is important to note that..."
- "As I mentioned earlier..."
- "In order to..." → "to"
- "Due to the fact that..." → "because"
- "At this point in time..." → "now"

#### Remove abstract nouns; replace with concrete pictures
- ❌ "gain success" → ✅ "see your revenue hit $30K/month"
- ❌ "achieve freedom" → ✅ "fire your alarm clock"
- ❌ "improve your health" → ✅ "run a 5K without getting winded"

#### Active voice, second person
- Prefer "you" over "one" or "people"
- Prefer active voice ("we ship same-day") over passive ("orders are shipped")

### When line-editing is done
The copy reads effortlessly. No stumbles when read aloud. No weak verbs in key positions. No jargon. No abstract nouns without concrete pictures behind them.

## Final Validation: The 5 Subconscious Questions

After all 3 levels of editing, run the draft past these 5 questions that every reader silently asks. A "no" to any = not ready to ship.

1. **Is this relevant to me right now?**
   - Hook must speak to reader's CURRENT state, not a hypothetical future state.
2. **Is this information new to me?**
   - If the reader can mentally tag it as "I've heard this before", they bounce. Novelty in framing, angle, mechanism, or specific is required.
3. **Do I understand the benefits I'll gain?**
   - Benefits must be CLEAR and CONCRETE (not generic or abstract).
4. **Do I understand the mechanism for HOW those benefits are delivered?**
   - Modern audiences won't accept black-box claims. Show the mechanism — even briefly.
5. **Do I trust the person/business speaking?**
   - Credibility must be established. Proof, testimonials, credentials, specific track record, or social proof.

Any "no" → find which level of edit addresses it, and re-edit.

## Validation against the 20 Convergent Principles

See `references/principles.md`. During validation:
- Read the full list
- For each principle, score the draft 0-2 (0 = violates, 1 = neutral, 2 = exemplifies)
- Sum the score
- If 3+ zeros: the draft has structural problems. Return to function or value edits.
- If score < 25/40: the draft is mediocre. Value-edit.
- If score > 30/40: shippable.

## Practical workflow for self-editing

1. Write the draft in one sitting. Don't edit as you write.
2. Walk away for 1-2 weeks if possible. Minimum 24 hours.
3. Re-read. Value-edit first. If it fails → rewrite big idea, then re-draft.
4. Function-edit. Reverse-outline. Fix structure and order.
5. Line-edit. Read aloud. Cut ruthlessly.
6. Validate against 5 subconscious questions + 20 principles.
7. Ship.

### The critique trap
Don't ask for critique on a Level-1 draft. Don't ask for critique on anything that's failed your own value edit. Get the piece to "I can't see what else to improve" before seeking outside eyes. Otherwise you waste the critiquer's time and you learn less.

## Editing metrics (if you need quantitative signal)

- Sentence length variance (mix of short/medium)
- "you"/"your" count vs "I"/"we"/"us" count — aim for 3:1 "you"
- Weak verb count ("to be" + "helps" + "does") — should be low
- Specific numbers / names / dates count — should be high for good copy
- Reading grade level — aim for 5th-7th grade for most B2C copy; 8th-10th for B2B or financial; lower is almost always better

If using AI tools (Hemingway, Grammarly) — use ONLY for catching mistakes, not for "improving" voice. Those tools make copy bland. Your voice is part of your edge.
