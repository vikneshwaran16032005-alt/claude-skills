# Product Analysis: FAB-DB Framework + 21 Reasons to Buy

Two frameworks that together answer: "What am I selling, and why will someone buy it?"

The second question is where most copy dies — copyriters describe the product (features) when they should be selling the transformation the buyer becomes (deeper benefits).

---

## FAB-DB: Features → Advantages → Benefits → Deeper Benefits (Sean McIntyre)

Sean's expansion of the classic Features/Benefits exercise. Most copyriters operate at levels 1-2. Level 3 is where good copy lives. **Level 4 is where 7-figure copy lives.**

### Level 1: FEATURES — what the product IS or DOES

The objective, descriptive facts. Specs, dimensions, materials, ingredients, mechanism names, runtimes.

**Examples:**
- "14 inches in diameter"
- "Made of food-grade stainless steel"
- "247 customer support"
- "Uses polyphenol-A extract"
- "6-month money-back guarantee"

**When to use alone:** rarely. Only when the nascent demand is so high that the prospect just needs to confirm the spec matches their mental checklist (commodity electronics shopping, some ecom).

**Exception:** a *weird* or *surprising* feature can hook attention. Hopkins's "Shot from guns!" puffed wheat. "It's toasted" for tobacco. Iron Neck's "air-fit bladder." The feature becomes the angle because the specificity is unusual.

### Level 2: ADVANTAGES — what it does BETTER than alternatives

Comparative claims. Only meaningful when the prospect knows alternatives exist and is comparing.

**Examples:**
- "3.33× the light of the carbon lamp" (Schweppes-style specificity)
- "Twice the protein of regular yogurt" (Chobani)
- "Smaller than a credit card" (vs. bulky alternatives)
- "Results in 48 hours vs. months with competing products"

**When to use:** Product-aware and Solution-aware audiences comparing options. High market sophistication (Stage 2+).

**Note:** an advantage can reference a FEATURE ("twice the screen resolution") or a BENEFIT ("twice the weight loss"). Don't confuse the two.

### Level 3: BENEFITS — the OUTCOME the user gets

The desire being fulfilled. What actually happens in the user's life if the feature delivers.

**Examples:**
- "Lose 5 pounds in the first month"
- "Sleep through the night"
- "Get more clients without cold-calling"
- "Your dog plays again like he's a puppy"

**Key rule:** every benefit must connect to one of the 21 reasons people buy (see below).

**Direct benefit:** explicitly stated outcome. "You'll make more money."
**Implied benefit:** shown through proof/testimonial/imagery; the reader infers the outcome themselves. Often more powerful because the reader's brain does the work and feels ownership of the conclusion.

### Level 4: DEEPER BENEFITS — the YEARNING behind the desire

The unconscious "self" the prospect is buying. Most copyriters never reach this level. **This is where 7-figure copy lives.**

#### How to find deeper benefits: the "but what does that REALLY mean?" cascade

1. State the feature
2. Ask: "What does this DO for the buyer?" → benefit
3. Ask: "But what does that REALLY mean? WHY do they want THAT?" → deeper benefit
4. Keep asking "why?" until you hit the visceral emotional core
5. **Rule:** when you write something that makes YOU go "oh damn", you've hit it

#### Classic case study — Dan Kennedy's hearing aid copy

The company marketed hearing aids with: "take a walk and hear the birds again."

Kennedy interviewed real buyers. He discovered:
- Buyers feared being mistaken for having **dementia** (mishearing → blurting wrong things)
- They feared being put in a **nursing home** (separated from family)
- They feared being seen as **untrustworthy as a babysitter** (can't hear a baby cry)

The *real* product wasn't "hearing the birds". It was "remaining a trusted, valued member of the family". That reframing produced the breakthrough copy.

#### Case study — pickup artistry "Pandora's Box" sales letter

The surface hook: sex with hot women. Hooks the immature audience.
The body reveals the deeper benefit: testimonials where the women "constantly reassure me by telling me how much she loves me, can't live without me, would never leave me."

The real yearning: to be **loved** and **never abandoned**. The buyer of this product has deep attachment insecurity. The copy that converts addresses that insecurity, even though the marketing opens with the surface desire.

#### Financial copy example (Sean's own)

Surface benefit: make a lot of money.
Cascade:
- Why? → freedom, security, respect
- Why? → to say "I made it" to yourself / your family
- Why? → to overcome feelings of inadequacy, to prove something
- But more importantly: → to not feel guilty about being rich. (Deep benefit layer.)

Sean's copy solved the guilt problem: "you'll be making money while helping cure genetic diseases." Freed the reader from the moral friction of "making money is selfish."

### When to use all 4 levels vs. fewer

| Product type | Levels needed |
|---|---|
| Commodities with strong nascent demand (chocolate, computers, candles) | Mostly features + light benefits. People already want them. |
| Tools / utilities in sophisticated markets | Features + Advantages + Benefits |
| Info products, coaching, newsletters, anything with LOW nascent demand | ALL FOUR levels — must dig to deeper benefit because nobody wakes up wanting it |
| Selling against alternatives | Lean on Advantages |
| Stage 4-5 market sophistication | Lean on Deeper Benefits + new mechanism |

### The cascade method, formalized

For each feature, fill in this chain:

```
FEATURE: [objective fact about the product]
    ↓
ADVANTAGE (optional): [how it's better than alternative X]
    ↓
BENEFIT: [the outcome the user gets]
    ↓ ask "why do they want that?"
DEEPER BENEFIT 1: [the yearning behind the desire]
    ↓ ask "why do they want THAT?"
DEEPER BENEFIT 2: [deeper still]
    ↓ continue until you hit visceral emotion
```

Stop when you hit something that feels uncomfortably personal — that's usually the right layer.

## The 21 Reasons People Buy

Every purchase decision maps to at least one of these emotional reasons. Copy that doesn't hook into one will not convert.

1. **To be liked** — acceptance, belonging, being welcomed
2. **To be appreciated** — recognition, thanks, attention
3. **To be right** — validation, vindication, "I told you so"
4. **To feel important** — status, prestige, recognition as someone special
5. **To make money** — income, wealth, financial gain
6. **To save money** — value, thrift, smart shopping
7. **To save time** — efficiency, convenience, not wasting life
8. **To make work easier** — effort reduction, smoother processes, less grind
9. **To be secure** — safety, stability, protection from loss
10. **To be attractive** — beauty, physical appeal, being noticed
11. **To feel sexy** — desire, romantic power, chemistry
12. **To be comfortable** — physical ease, emotional ease, familiarity
13. **To be distinctive** — standing out, uniqueness, being remembered
14. **To be happy** — joy, fulfillment, lightness
15. **To have fun** — entertainment, play, enjoyment
16. **To gain knowledge** — learning, mastery, understanding
17. **To be healthy** — physical wellbeing, vitality, longevity
18. **To gratify curiosity** — discovery, the thrill of knowing
19. **For convenience** — frictionless access, at-hand availability
20. **Out of fear** — avoidance of loss / pain / embarrassment / consequence
21. **Out of greed** — wanting more, wanting it now, wanting what they don't have (sometimes distinguished as its own category; sometimes a mode of #5/#20)

### Implied reasons often missed
- **Out of guilt** — buying for someone to make up for something
- **Out of identity** — to be a certain kind of person (covered in Schwartz's identification technique)

### How to use this list

**In Phase 1 research:** for each product feature, map it to 1-3 reasons. That gives you the emotional vocabulary for the copy.

**In Phase 3 writing:** EVERY benefit stated in the copy should tie back to one of these reasons — usually 2-3 of them. If a benefit doesn't tie to any, it's a feature in disguise and won't motivate buying.

**In Phase 4 editing:** run the copy through the list. Does it hit the reasons the target cares about most? Does it avoid hitting reasons the target doesn't care about (or worse, feels guilty about)?

## Worked example: senior dog joint supplement

### Features
- 30 tablets per bottle
- Glucosamine + chondroitin
- Natural sources, no prescription required
- 30-day money-back guarantee
- Approved by American Vets Association
- Crushable into food

### Advantages (vs. competitors)
- No prescription needed (vs. vet-prescribed meds)
- Works in 48 hours (vs. weeks for other supplements)
- Cheaper than vet visits ($40/mo vs. $1000s)
- Suitable for all breeds (vs. breed-specific formulations)

### Benefits
- Your senior dog moves easier
- No more wincing when jumping on the couch
- Plays with the kids again
- You stop worrying about watching him suffer

### Deeper benefits
- You're not failing him (age is not your dog's fault — or yours)
- You're giving him the golden years he deserves
- You remain the caretaker you pictured yourself being when you first got him
- You avoid the guilt of "I should have done something sooner"
- Your family enjoys the dog together again (memories being made, not lost)

### Which reasons to buy?
- #17 healthy (for dog)
- #9 secure (protection from loss of dog's quality of life)
- #20 fear (of watching him suffer, of guilt)
- #2 appreciated (the dog's gratitude, the family's gratitude)
- #12 comfortable (your own emotional comfort — not watching him hurt)

### Copy implication
Don't lead with "glucosamine supplement." Lead with the pain the owner feels watching their dog struggle, and the relief of giving back a few more good years. The glucosamine is just the mechanism.
