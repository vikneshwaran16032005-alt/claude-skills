## How to harvest VOC (field methods — feed these to `ivoc-researcher`)
*Strongly recurring across Josh Braun + 30MPC; the "how" behind a good language bank.*
- **The coffee-machine / spouse test:** phrase a problem exactly as the buyer would vent it to a colleague at the coffee machine or "at home in the evening bitching to their spouse" — their words, never product voice. ("All the best auditors get snatched up by the big four offering crazy salaries.")
- **JTBD interview (buyers in the last 30–45 days):** ask *"Why this? Why now? Why not keep doing what you were doing?"* — never "Why did you buy?" Mine the exact lingo into a running doc. McDonald's-milkshake rule: real buying reasons (one-handed long-commute breakfast) differ from brand language (flavors/viscosity).
- **Case-study / success-story mining:** skip the polished marketing page; read the "customer success stories" before/after — "the black-and-white version of the infomercial." Lift the quotes verbatim.
- **Lingo Library (Josh Braun):** bank the verbatim *"before story"* quotes from those success stories (only the text inside quote marks) and reuse them as gap questions, cold openers, and email lines. The whole bank is *discovered*, not invented: **"good copy and good questions aren't written, they're found."**
- **Dig past the stated reason:** the nightlight buyer's surface reason ("to read in bed") masked the real outcome ("to save my marriage — read without waking my wife"). Surface "want" → ask "why now" → reach the real need a layer beneath.
- **Mine testimonials for repeated emotional words:** "calm" and "love" appeared 6+ times on one page — rare words worth swiping. **"Explain it like I'm 10":** paste the prospect's URL into ChatGPT and have it explain their problem at a 4th-grade level to strip buzzwords.
- **Mine the gap between how prospects talk and how reps write:** Carta laid SDR email language next to what CFOs literally said in fireside chats — "completely different" — and rewrote their emails; jargon like "single source of truth" leaves buyers thinking "I had no idea what you meant." (30MPC/Ken Amar)
- **"Talk their talk":** drop one insider phrase a buyer only says to peers — for data engineers, "Python scripts wrapped in duct tape" — earns a chuckle and the right to ask heavier questions. (30MPC/Mallika Sahay)
- **Surface objections in the customer's own words (discovery script):** "Have you ever tried to use [X]? You haven't — but you've wanted to? Well, *why haven't you?*" The "why haven't you already" answer is the objection bank. (MFM)
- **Keep asking "but why":** to reach the real dream outcome, ask CS/implementation/execs "Why do buyers buy our solution?" then keep digging — "but why — in pursuit of what, to solve what, what's the impact?" (30MPC/Kyle Coleman)
- **Mine call transcripts for triggers + build a Messaging Matrix (30MPC):** feed call recordings to AI and ask "what are prospects talking about that would make great cold-email triggers?" Structure the output in the *buyer's* POV, never product language — **Priorities · Current Solutions · Problems · Aspirations.**
- **Magic-moment question (turns pain into a liftable story):** "When was the moment you realized that was a problem?" — abstract pain becomes a vivid, quotable scene you can drop straight into copy.
- **The 4 silent questions every prospect asks (in order; a "no" to any drops you out):** 1) Should I pay attention to you? 2) Do I like you? 3) Can you help me do something better? 4) Are you credible? `[Josh Braun — repeated]`

## Market: Aspiring solopreneurs / "9-to-5 escapees" / wannabe creators
*(a dominant market in the corpus — Patrick Dang + Myron Golden audiences; the single richest VOC vein.)*

### Desires
- "I just want to achieve freedom" — "work whenever I want, wherever I want, with whoever I want." The root desire; recurs verbatim dozens of times. `[strong]`
- "Turn your yearly income into your monthly income" / "make my golf channel pay my mortgage" / "automatic money higher than my expenses every month." (the money-speed dream — Myron's audience)
- "I want to travel the world, make money online, and be my own boss."
- "If I can build pipeline for a company, I can do it for myself too."
- "If I could do my journey over? Get it done faster so I could enjoy it longer." / "spend money to buy back the rest of my life."

### Firm notions (validate, don't fight)
- "People buy emotionally and justify logically" — they say it about themselves: "I trust this guy, boom, done."
- "If he helps me make an extra $100K and only charges 3K, that's a steal — basically free." (the ROI rationalization that licenses a premium price)
- "I'd rather pay MORE to know for sure this gets done fast than go cheaper and be at 50%."

### Shakeable notions (limiting beliefs you can flip)
- **"It won't work for ME."** "Of course YOU can — you're handsome, smart, you talk well. But I'm shy. This isn't me." → flip with a same-background testimonial.
- **"Nobody would pay me for what I know."** / "I don't have unique skills." → "If you got paid for a skill at your job, you're a professional."
- **"I'm not an expert / I'll look like a fraud."** → "You only need to be one step ahead." / "become an interviewer."
- **Beginner/hobby shame (reusable across any skill/hobby niche):** "I can't go to the group yet, I'm not good enough" — fear of the "[hobby] police." → counter with "finished is better than perfect."
- **"It's too saturated / too late."** → "There's always room for the best" / "you're early if you start now."
- **"The coaches, gurus, course-sellers are all scams."** (default skepticism toward the whole category)
- **Money-guilt / wealth-shame (Myron's vein):** "I don't really care about money" · "I'm not that greedy, I want some left over for somebody else" · "I don't want people to judge me / think I'm a bad person for having money" · "money is the root of all evil" · "I want to get into heaven." → reframe: "It's the *love* of money; love people, use money."

### Identifications
- "[Coach] was just like me when he started… maybe if I work with him, I can too." (aspirational-mirror — they buy the coach, not the coaching)
- "I'm the type of person who invests in my skill set to become a better person" — spending reframed as identity/flex.
- B2C buying driver: "people want to *be* like you" (vs B2B: they want you to work *for* them).

### Characteristics
- Golden handcuffs: "VP title, six-figure salary, big house — but empty inside, making another person rich while my dreams collect dust."
- "More month left at the end of the money than money at the end of the month." / "robbing Peter to pay Paul." (Myron — recurs constantly)
- Decision paralysis: "there's billions of directions — if I just knew which one, I'd go full speed."
- Consumes ~7 hrs of content (Dunbar 7-11) before buying.

### Common words / phrases
- "freedom" · "stuck / trapped" · "golden handcuffs" · "the matrix" · "bet on yourself" · "escape the 9-to-5" · "throwing spaghetti at the wall" · "dying inside every Monday" · "stuck like Chuck in a pickup truck" · "broke as a joke" · "sick and tired of being sick and tired" · "day one or one day."

### Other (objections in their own words)
- "I'm too busy with a 9-to-5 / I don't have enough time." (the #1 stall)
- "Do I want the 97 or the 397? I don't want to pay 400." / "2K feels like a lot — can I pay you $50 an hour?" (price-objection self-talk)
- "I tried it, it didn't work" / "I was posting, nobody liked them, so I went back to what I knew."
- "I want to write a book / tell my story." → (the trap: "nobody cares about your story but you and your mama").
- "Everything sounds good… maybe I can get started a month from now." (the soft stall)

## Market: B2B / enterprise software buyers (the brush-off & objection bank)
*(the largest vein — Trent Dressel + Ian Koniak + Josh Braun + 30MPC corpora; invaluable verbatim objection language for any B2B sales copy. Now the deepest section, since the corpus is cold-outreach-heavy.)*

### Characteristics (the defensive, risk-averse mindset)
- "I'm on the defensive — it's a salesperson trying to push something I don't need." (system-1 reaction)
- **Risk-aversion, not ROI, is the hidden driver:** "I have two kids and a Fortune 500 job — I don't want to make noise and risk losing the company money." Buyers play *not to lose* (their money, their job, face). "It's their neck on the line — they won't get fired for doing nothing, but a failed multi-million bet could end them."
- The busy reality (verbatim, to mirror): "I've got 25–89 unopened emails, a proposal due, someone just quit, and I want to leave early for my kid's choir concert — the last thing I want is to hear from you."
- **Shelfware fear (the executive's real buying question):** "Most executives want to know — are my people actually going to use it?" Adoption risk, not price, is what they're quietly buying down.
- **Late-stage risk = pain (verbatim fears):** "What if we don't adopt this? What if people hate it? What if the 3-month implementation is actually 18 months?" Late in the cycle, send change-management / rollout proof that de-risks the decision.
- **IT's real fear (implementation surprise):** "you'll ask me to plug in Snowflake, it'll take 30 hours, and you sales guys never talk about it pre-sale — we sign, get pulled into implementation, and it's a whole show."
- Gartner/Macnamara stat to write against: ~75–85% of buyers don't want to involve a salesperson; when they do, it's at the very end to verify you're right. **Counter-stat (John Barrows):** 43% want a *rep-free* experience — yet reported **23% higher regret** when no rep was involved; the unguided buyer often buys *worse*.

### Brush-offs (verbatim — the canonical bank)
- **First-5-seconds:** "Who is this?" · "How'd you get my number?" · "Is this a sales call / are you a telemarketer?" · "Your name doesn't ring a bell." · "I'm running into a meeting / I'm right in the middle of something." · "What are you selling?" · "Take me off your list." · the clipped exasperated **"Fine — how can I help you?"** (= "I know you're a telemarketer, get on with it so I can reject you").
- **Post-ask / back-end:** "We already have something in place." · "We're using [competitor] / we just signed a contract." · "We don't have any budget / there's a spending freeze / budget's already allocated." · "Now's not the right time / we're really busy right now." · "Call me back in 6 months / next quarter." · "Just send me an email / some info." · "That's not my responsibility." · "We do it all in-house."
- **Over-demoed brush-offs (you showed too much):** "You've given me a lot to digest here." · "It feels like a bit more than what we need at this stage." · "We're not ready for this / not there yet."
- **Inflated-requirements / mismatch tells:** "They want the BMW 7-Series at a Toyota Corolla price." · "Every prospect wants every appetizer on the menu — until they see the check." · "We're just looking around because we're growing." · "How can we sharpen the pencil?" · "We have other vendors who give us an 80% discount."
- **Price/budget objection (verbatim):** "It's not that I don't see value in what you do, but what you're offering seems a little bit expensive." · "You're priced at $1,500 a month and honestly we just can't swing that budget — I need your best price." · the **always-needs-a-win buyer:** "I always need my 20% off… even if the suit fits, I look in the mirror and say 'that doesn't look that good' so I get my deal" (price resistance is often a *ritual*, not a real blocker).
- **2025–26 build-vs-buy (dominant objection, ~73% of sellers hit it):** "We can build it ourselves with AI." · "We don't want to lock into a 3–5-year contract." · "We're a Microsoft shop / Google's giving us credits."
- **Hard rejections:** "F you, never call here again." · "Not interested. Please don't reach out again. This isn't for us." · "Our supplier is my sister-in-law and we're never changing unless she dies."

### What the brush-offs really mean (decode)
- "Just send me an email" = "I'm going to hit delete — polite way to end the call." (the socially-agreed-upon exit; "you're asking me to email because you don't plan to read it.")
- "Call me back in 6 months / next quarter" = "I'm too nice to tell you no" / "I want you to lose my number."
- **"I'm too busy"** = too busy to *rip-and-replace software*, not too busy for a 15-minute look. The real objection is the disruption, not your offer.
- "I need to think about it" = "if I were sold I wouldn't need to think — there's a hidden objection."
- "We have a vendor for that" = the truth, not an objection — everyone has a solution (sometimes a spreadsheet); "buyers are not liars," probe its Achilles heel rather than fighting it.
- A **price deflection** ("well, it depends how much it costs") usually means they don't yet believe it solves the problem — not that price is the real blocker.
- The real competitor is almost always the **status quo / "no decision"** — "nobody got fired for not making a decision; people get fired for making bad decisions"; "the devil I know beats the one I don't."
- Buyers **read the 4-, 3-, 2- and 1-star reviews first**, skipping the 5-stars — so case studies that "sound like five-star reviews" get ignored.
- **Third-party trust > your marketing:** "The stuff we say about ourselves doesn't count. It's not believable." They weight peers, reviews and outside sources over anything you claim about yourself.
- **Halo-effect logic buyers run silently:** "If their sales team is this all over the deal, their engineers must be this good too" — and the inverse (a sloppy sales process taxes belief in the product itself).
- **"It won't work for me" snowflake disbelief:** "I could see how this solves a problem for someone, but my situation is its own unique snowflake."
- **Buyers know their job, not how to buy yours:** "Buyers are experts in how to do their job — not experts in how to buy our stuff" — they need help running the *change*, not just the product.

### Firm notions / trust-killers
- Every seller claims "best service, best price, best coverage" — "I've heard it from every rep," so it commoditizes you and *lowers* trust.
- Buyers smell weakness: "a lot of words but you haven't said anything yet" — charlatans "lead with flattery and not substance," flowery language going nowhere, instead of numbers + a plain resume. Verbatim consultant/agency objection: "we don't understand what they're saying" — buyers want "you do this and this much revenue will come," not "change your match types / do more lookalikes."
- Trust-killer phrases the buyer hears: "to be honest / honestly" = "everything before was a lie"; "trust me" = instant distrust; "just following up / checking in" = "calling for my money."
- Buyer-side gold (Grady Ligon, CIO): "Nobody likes to be sold to." · "People trying to impress me is creepy and off-putting." · "I'll tune you out in the first minute if it's clear you don't understand my business." · "The email is so easy to send, so easy to ignore." · "Why would I work with a company I've never heard of because YOU think you meet my needs?" · Deals that go well "always start by talking about something other than business."
- Deck aversion: "More than 10 slides of bullets and I assume you don't know your product — show me it working."
- **Cold-email "tells" buyers instantly clock (and stop reading):** "I hope this email finds you well" (the siren song of a cold email) · "Quick question" · subject-line flattery ("they're about to ask me something") · generic praise ("I admire how your team invests in pet wellness" = "sounds so AI") · em-dashes. Exec read-time is brutal: **~3 seconds to earn the open, ~9 seconds (~37 words) of reading if you get it.**
- **Two emotional drivers of change:** present pain **+** fear of future pain — "without both, the prospect feels no need to change… that's why you get tons of objections and they don't buy" (Jeremy Miner).

### What "good" feels like (the reaction to aim for — north-star for copy)
- **The dream sales experience (why a buyer actually chose them, verbatim):** "I've tried a variety of tools… the difference was your rep — they asked these questions, understood my reasons *why*, could tell my story back to me, and made me look good in front of my boss." Telling their story back + making them look good is the whole game.
- **"Humans deeply want to be understood":** the reaction to engineer is "I know" / "oh my God, this person finally gets it." Nail the playback and they feel *seen* — the strongest signal your copy landed.
- **The well-qualified buyer (pain quantified before price):** "This is a no-brainer. This is something we need." If they're not saying this, you named price before you built the pain.

### Other (urgency & buying-signal tells)
- Low urgency: "if it's not solved in six months it'll be annoying, but we'll deal." High urgency: "if this isn't solved in six months I probably won't have a job."
- **Three buyer urgency modes (verbatim, to qualify):** "in education mode, just looking" · "biggest problem, needed it solved six months ago" · "some pain, but if I don't solve it in 12 months nothing terrible happens." Only the middle is a real now-deal.
- **Laggard FOMO trigger:** "Oh God, what am I missing? How are we not doing this — we're the only one in the region."
- **CEO budget-gate questions to pre-answer in any problem-aware copy:** "How are we solving this today? Why won't what we're doing work anymore? What's the expected upside?"
- Buy-signal phrases: "What can I do for you?" / "Why are we talking?" / "I'm doing well, how are you?" (asks back = green light).
- Why they switch / buy at all: to "make more money, save money, or mitigate risk" — and personally, to "look good, get promoted, keep their job."
- Out-of-budget-cycle reality: "I agree it's important, but you missed the budget window" — out-of-cycle asks need sub-1-yr ROI or an acute problem.

## Market: Underperforming / burned-out sales professionals + anxious SDRs (high-ticket coaching buyers)
*(Ian Koniak's audience + Josh Braun's SDRs/AEs; the richest emotional vein, ideal for transformation/coaching copy.)*

### Desires / dream outcome
- "Do what I want, when I want, with who I want, for as long as I want." / "I don't collect things, I collect experiences."
- "Send my kids to whatever school, choose my zip code, give my spouse the flexibility to not work" — freedom = never worrying about money, *not* bling.
- "I want to have it all — not either/or. Great career AND be a present parent."
- The relief test: "How would you feel with a faucet of money in your home?" → "free," "relieved," "limitless."

### Firm notions
- "Buying is emotional — everybody wants a deal." / "People buy on narrative, then justify with numbers."
- "At the very least I'd get my money back in commission" (the ROI that licenses a $20–45K coaching spend).

### Shakeable notions / limiting beliefs (verbatim)
- **Income set-point:** "For over a decade, no matter how hard I worked, I made the same amount." / "I always believed the most I could make was $200–300K, because that's what I'd made."
- **Identity tied to the number:** "I 100% attached my identity to my quota." / "My self-worth equals my sales performance." / "I was my number."
- **Imposter syndrome:** "How long can I keep faking it before I get found out? I got lucky, I don't deserve to be here." / "I'm just a salesperson — why would an executive meet with me?"
- **"It only works for you / I'm in the wrong role":** "Maybe I'm not cut out for enterprise." / "I'm a transactional guy, I can't do strategic." / blame-shift: "my territory sucks, my manager sucks, my product sucks, my comp plan sucks."

### Characteristics (the pain state — twist this knife)
- "There's no worse feeling than untapped potential — it feels like laziness, a treadmill, a grind."
- "I show up and just be reactive — a horrible hamster wheel of self-doubt and anxiety." / "Every day is Groundhog Day; I set goals and get nothing done."
- The achiever's hollow: "Making $200–400K a year, I should be happy — yet it feels empty." / "Is this it? Is this all I have — just money?" / "How long can I be on this hamster wheel — 10, 15, 20 years?"
- Cost to family: "I was crushing it at work but not showing up at home." / "I don't want my gravestone to say President's Club member." / "Mom, can you play with me? — Sorry babe, one more phone call."
- The nights-and-weekends loop: "I beat myself up till bed, then wake up and do it all over again." / "at night I fire up the laptop and work nights and weekends." / shame, guilt, "I wasted the day."
- **Call reluctance / cold-call anxiety (Josh Braun's SDRs):** "Every time I pick up the phone, I feel small — the person I'm calling, I feel big. I reach for it, but just when I'm about to pick it up, I reach away." / "You make a hundred cold calls and barely talk to two or three people; send email after email and get little to no response." / the fear stack: "I'm going to miss my number, miss my bonus, maybe have to lay people off, maybe lose my job."
- Stats to cite: 63% of sellers struggling with mental health (up from 43% pre-COVID); 79% stressed or highly stressed.

### Common words / phrases
- "the grind" · "hamster wheel" · "treadmill" · "burned out / burnt out" · "Groundhog Day" · "stuck at the $200–300K level" · "the fire's dimmed" · "imposter" · "I feel like a fraud / a phony / a loser" · "this is happening FOR me, not TO me."

### Other (objections to the coaching offer + the buying triggers)
- "I can't afford it / it's intimidating." / "Should I really invest in myself?" → (rock-bottom is the trigger: "I'd missed quota three years in a row — I need help.")
- "Watching free videos/podcasts is enough." → "information is free, you pay for application + accountability." (Reinforced by completion data: even a $2K+ course isn't enough skin in the game — ~6% finish, ~20% reach halfway; self-serve on-demand kills completion, so sell live/accountability as the differentiator.)
- The DFY desire (what the offer must promise): "I wanted somebody to put their arm around me and say: here's exactly what to do, and everything's going to be okay."
- "Why not me? I work as hard as anybody, I'm as smart as anyone — why aren't I getting the results I know I'm capable of?"

## Market: High-ticket coaching / course buyers (general)

### Desires / firm notions
- "It's not harder to sell at $10K than $500 — the higher-priced offer with more support is exactly what they want."
- "I don't like selling, I want to be as hands-off as possible" (underpinned by "it'll be easier to sell at $100 than $500").

### Shakeable notions
- "What's the difference between my free content and my paid content? Am I giving too much away?"
- "I'm already overdelivering — how do I 10x the price when I give so much of myself?"

### Other (buyer self-talk on a sales page)
- "Should I buy this? Who else has used this and is happy? How do I know I'm not wasting my money and time?"
- The reply that proves great copy: "I know you're trying to sell me, yet I read the whole thing — why am I opening your newsletter if I know I'll be sold?"
- "It sounds too good to be true." (instinctive reaction to a big claim — name it and keep going)

## Market: Founders & operators (pitching investors, selling/exiting, buying for the business)
*(new this cycle — My First Million operator/founder corpus; useful for pitch decks, investor outreach, M&A, and B2B copy aimed at decision-making operators.)*
- **Investor's internal monologue reading a pitch (verbatim):** "Should I even care? Should I listen? Is this big? Am I going to make money? Am I betting on credible people?" — answer these in order, design last.
- **Operator pain pulled verbatim into copy (e-commerce working capital):** financing that takes "a percentage of your sales," "uncapped daily remittances" that "spike exactly when you need cash to restock," "draining profits during peak season." Name the competitor's misaligned incentive (Shopify Capital takes a % and won't let you spend funds elsewhere) as your differentiator.
- **Firm notion / trust filter:** operators screen out flattery-without-substance — "a lot of words but you haven't said anything yet"; they want numbers + a plain track record, not flowery language going nowhere.
- **Other (selling/exiting self-talk):** signal interest without looking desperate — "We love our business and we're not for sale, but we respect you — we owe it to ourselves to listen." Manufacture leverage — get a weak-but-known buyer to offer first, then approach real targets: "we have an offer in hand and you're the best strategic fit." Radical transparency disarms diligence — hand over "every skeleton in our closet" up front.

## Market: Agency owners / SMMA / service providers (stuck at the plateau)
- **Desire:** escape the "yo-yo effect" — "3K, 2K, 7K, 14K, back to 6," stuck at the "$10K plateau" → "a consistent 20 meetings per month."
- **Firm/shakeable:** "How do I get a client if I don't know anyone who wants ads… I'm too busy fulfilling what I sold." Retainer-killer: client made $100K from your ads, you got $1,500, "so they locked in gains and cancelled."
- **Common phrases:** "show up and throw up" · "split-test myself into poverty" · "one-legged stool" (channel concentration) · "bleeding-neck problem."
- **Other:** "We don't have a budget, but we'd love a proposal." (cue to pivot done-for-you → done-with-you)

## Market: B2B SaaS / AI sales founders (selling to startups + enterprise)
*(retained from v0 — First Round Capital; smaller slice now but distinct.)*
- **Desire:** a vendor who'll "actually show up and help if I run into an issue" — reliability over feature count; "strongly opinionated about the best way," not "a vending machine for software." `[1 src]`
- **Firm notion:** "I bet on the founder, not feature parity." "Caring is contagious through the sales process."
- **Shakeable notion:** "Software vendors don't actually show up — since we've been buying AI tools, not one company sent teams." A vendor who *does* flips it instantly.
