## Market: Aspiring solopreneurs / "9-to-5 escapees" / wannabe creators
*(a dominant market in the corpus — Patrick Dang + Myron Golden audiences; the single richest VOC vein.)*

### Desires
- "I just want to achieve freedom" — "work whenever I want, wherever I want, with whoever I want." The root desire; recurs verbatim dozens of times. `[strong]`
- "Turn your yearly income into your monthly income" / "make my golf channel pay my mortgage" / "automatic money higher than my expenses every month." (the money-speed dream — Myron's audience)
- "I want to travel the world, make money online, and be my own boss."
- "If I can build pipeline for a company, I can do it for myself too."
- "If I could do my journey over? Get it done faster so I could enjoy it longer." / "spend money to buy back the rest of my life."

### Firm notions (validate, don't fight)
- "People buy emotionally and justify logically" — they say it about themselves: "I trust this guy, boom, done."
- "If he helps me make an extra $100K and only charges 3K, that's a steal — basically free." (the ROI rationalization that licenses a premium price)
- "I'd rather pay MORE to know for sure this gets done fast than go cheaper and be at 50%."

### Shakeable notions (limiting beliefs you can flip)
- **"It won't work for ME."** "Of course YOU can — you're handsome, smart, you talk well. But I'm shy. This isn't me." → flip with a same-background testimonial.
- **"Nobody would pay me for what I know."** / "I don't have unique skills." → "If you got paid for a skill at your job, you're a professional."
- **"I'm not an expert / I'll look like a fraud."** → "You only need to be one step ahead." / "become an interviewer."
- **"It's too saturated / too late."** → "There's always room for the best" / "you're early if you start now."
- **"The coaches, gurus, course-sellers are all scams."** (default skepticism toward the whole category)
- **Money-guilt / wealth-shame (Myron's vein):** "I don't really care about money" · "I'm not that greedy, I want some left over for somebody else" · "I don't want people to judge me / think I'm a bad person for having money" · "money is the root of all evil" · "I want to get into heaven." → reframe: "It's the *love* of money; love people, use money."

### Identifications
- "[Coach] was just like me when he started… maybe if I work with him, I can too." (aspirational-mirror — they buy the coach, not the coaching)
- "I'm the type of person who invests in my skill set to become a better person" — spending reframed as identity/flex.
- B2C buying driver: "people want to *be* like you" (vs B2B: they want you to work *for* them).

### Characteristics
- Golden handcuffs: "VP title, six-figure salary, big house — but empty inside, making another person rich while my dreams collect dust."
- "More month left at the end of the money than money at the end of the month." / "robbing Peter to pay Paul." (Myron — recurs constantly)
- Decision paralysis: "there's billions of directions — if I just knew which one, I'd go full speed."
- Consumes ~7 hrs of content (Dunbar 7-11) before buying.

### Common words / phrases
- "freedom" · "stuck / trapped" · "golden handcuffs" · "the matrix" · "bet on yourself" · "escape the 9-to-5" · "throwing spaghetti at the wall" · "dying inside every Monday" · "stuck like Chuck in a pickup truck" · "broke as a joke" · "sick and tired of being sick and tired" · "day one or one day."

### Other (objections in their own words)
- "I'm too busy with a 9-to-5 / I don't have enough time." (the #1 stall)
- "Do I want the 97 or the 397? I don't want to pay 400." / "2K feels like a lot — can I pay you $50 an hour?" (price-objection self-talk)
- "I tried it, it didn't work" / "I was posting, nobody liked them, so I went back to what I knew."
- "I want to write a book / tell my story." → (the trap: "nobody cares about your story but you and your mama").
- "Everything sounds good… maybe I can get started a month from now." (the soft stall)

## Market: B2B / enterprise software buyers (the brush-off & objection bank)
*(the largest new vein — Trent Dressel + Ian Koniak corpora; invaluable verbatim objection language for any B2B sales copy.)*

### Characteristics (the defensive, risk-averse mindset)
- "I'm on the defensive — it's a salesperson trying to push something I don't need." (system-1 reaction)
- **Risk-aversion, not ROI, is the hidden driver:** "I have two kids and a Fortune 500 job — I don't want to make noise and risk losing the company money." Buyers play *not to lose* (their money, their job, face). "It's their neck on the line — they won't get fired for doing nothing, but a failed multi-million bet could end them."
- The busy reality (verbatim, to mirror): "I've got 25–89 unopened emails, a proposal due, someone just quit, and I want to leave early for my kid's choir concert — the last thing I want is to hear from you."
- Gartner/Macnamara stat to write against: ~75–85% of buyers don't want to involve a salesperson; when they do, it's at the very end to verify you're right.

### Brush-offs (verbatim — the canonical bank)
- **First-5-seconds:** "Who is this?" · "How'd you get my number?" · "Is this a sales call / are you a telemarketer?" · "Your name doesn't ring a bell." · "I'm running into a meeting." · "What are you selling?" · "Take me off your list."
- **Post-ask / back-end:** "We already have something in place." · "We're using [competitor]." · "We don't have any budget / there's a spending freeze." · "Now's not the right time." · "We just renewed." · "Call me back next quarter." · "Just send me an email / some info." · "Send me a proposal and we'll circle back."
- **2025–26 build-vs-buy (NEW dominant objection, ~73% of sellers hit it):** "We can build it ourselves with AI." · "We don't want to lock into a 3–5-year contract." · "We're a Microsoft shop / Google's giving us credits."
- **Hard rejections:** "F you, never call here again." · "I'll put you in a body bag — I signed a three-year contract with your competitor."

### What the brush-offs really mean (decode)
- "Just send me an email" = "I'm going to hit delete — polite way to end the call." (it's the socially-agreed-upon exit)
- "Call me back next quarter" = "I want you to lose my number."
- "I need to think about it" = "if I were sold I wouldn't need to think — there's a hidden objection."
- "We have a vendor for that" = the truth, not an objection — everyone has a solution (sometimes a spreadsheet); probe its Achilles heel.
- The real competitor is almost always the **status quo / "no decision"** — "the devil we know is better than the one we don't."

### Firm notions / trust-killers
- Every seller claims "best service, best price, best coverage" — "I've heard it from every rep," so it commoditizes you and *lowers* trust.
- Trust-killer phrases the buyer hears: "to be honest / honestly" = "everything before was a lie"; "trust me" = instant distrust; "just following up / checking in" = "calling for my money."
- Buyer-side gold (Grady Ligon, CIO): "Nobody likes to be sold to." · "People trying to impress me is creepy and off-putting." · "I'll tune you out in the first minute if it's clear you don't understand my business." · "The email is so easy to send, so easy to ignore." · "Why would I work with a company I've never heard of because YOU think you meet my needs?" · Deals that go well "always start by talking about something other than business."
- Deck aversion: "More than 10 slides of bullets and I assume you don't know your product — show me it working."

### Other (urgency & buying-signal tells)
- Low urgency: "if it's not solved in six months it'll be annoying, but we'll deal." High urgency: "if this isn't solved in six months I probably won't have a job."
- Buy-signal phrases: "What can I do for you?" / "Why are we talking?" / "I'm doing well, how are you?" (asks back = green light).
- Why they switch / buy at all: to "make more money, save money, or mitigate risk" — and personally, to "look good, get promoted, keep their job."
- Out-of-budget-cycle reality: "I agree it's important, but you missed the budget window" — out-of-cycle asks need sub-1-yr ROI or an acute problem.

## Market: Underperforming / burned-out sales professionals (high-ticket coaching buyers)
*(NEW — Ian Koniak's audience; the richest new emotional vein, ideal for transformation/coaching copy.)*

### Desires / dream outcome
- "Do what I want, when I want, with who I want, for as long as I want." / "I don't collect things, I collect experiences."
- "Send my kids to whatever school, choose my zip code, give my spouse the flexibility to not work" — freedom = never worrying about money, *not* bling.
- "I want to have it all — not either/or. Great career AND be a present parent."
- The relief test: "How would you feel with a faucet of money in your home?" → "free," "relieved," "limitless."

### Firm notions
- "Buying is emotional — everybody wants a deal." / "People buy on narrative, then justify with numbers."
- "At the very least I'd get my money back in commission" (the ROI that licenses a $20–45K coaching spend).

### Shakeable notions / limiting beliefs (verbatim)
- **Income set-point:** "For over a decade, no matter how hard I worked, I made the same amount." / "I always believed the most I could make was $200–300K, because that's what I'd made."
- **Identity tied to the number:** "I 100% attached my identity to my quota." / "My self-worth equals my sales performance." / "I was my number."
- **Imposter syndrome:** "How long can I keep faking it before I get found out? I got lucky, I don't deserve to be here." / "I'm just a salesperson — why would an executive meet with me?"
- **"It only works for you / I'm in the wrong role":** "Maybe I'm not cut out for enterprise." / "I'm a transactional guy, I can't do strategic." / blame-shift: "my territory sucks, my manager sucks, my product sucks, my comp plan sucks."

### Characteristics (the pain state — twist this knife)
- "There's no worse feeling than untapped potential — it feels like laziness, a treadmill, a grind."
- "I show up and just be reactive — a horrible hamster wheel of self-doubt and anxiety." / "Every day is Groundhog Day; I set goals and get nothing done."
- The achiever's hollow: "Making $200–400K a year, I should be happy — yet it feels empty." / "Is this it? Is this all I have — just money?" / "How long can I be on this hamster wheel — 10, 15, 20 years?"
- Cost to family: "I was crushing it at work but not showing up at home." / "I don't want my gravestone to say President's Club member." / "Mom, can you play with me? — Sorry babe, one more phone call."
- The nights-and-weekends loop: "I beat myself up till bed, then wake up and do it all over again." / "at night I fire up the laptop and work nights and weekends." / shame, guilt, "I wasted the day."
- Stats to cite: 63% of sellers struggling with mental health (up from 43% pre-COVID); 79% stressed or highly stressed.

### Common words / phrases
- "the grind" · "hamster wheel" · "treadmill" · "burned out / burnt out" · "Groundhog Day" · "stuck at the $200–300K level" · "the fire's dimmed" · "imposter" · "I feel like a fraud / a phony / a loser" · "this is happening FOR me, not TO me."

### Other (objections to the coaching offer + the buying triggers)
- "I can't afford it / it's intimidating." / "Should I really invest in myself?" → (rock-bottom is the trigger: "I'd missed quota three years in a row — I need help.")
- "Watching free videos/podcasts is enough." → "information is free, you pay for application + accountability."
- The DFY desire (what the offer must promise): "I wanted somebody to put their arm around me and say: here's exactly what to do, and everything's going to be okay."
- "Why not me? I work as hard as anybody, I'm as smart as anyone — why aren't I getting the results I know I'm capable of?"

## Market: High-ticket coaching / course buyers (general)

### Desires / firm notions
- "It's not harder to sell at $10K than $500 — the higher-priced offer with more support is exactly what they want."
- "I don't like selling, I want to be as hands-off as possible" (underpinned by "it'll be easier to sell at $100 than $500").

### Shakeable notions
- "What's the difference between my free content and my paid content? Am I giving too much away?"
- "I'm already overdelivering — how do I 10x the price when I give so much of myself?"

### Other (buyer self-talk on a sales page)
- "Should I buy this? Who else has used this and is happy? How do I know I'm not wasting my money and time?"
- The reply that proves great copy: "I know you're trying to sell me, yet I read the whole thing — why am I opening your newsletter if I know I'll be sold?"
- "It sounds too good to be true." (instinctive reaction to a big claim — name it and keep going)

## Market: Agency owners / SMMA / service providers (stuck at the plateau)
- **Desire:** escape the "yo-yo effect" — "3K, 2K, 7K, 14K, back to 6," stuck at the "$10K plateau" → "a consistent 20 meetings per month."
- **Firm/shakeable:** "How do I get a client if I don't know anyone who wants ads… I'm too busy fulfilling what I sold." Retainer-killer: client made $100K from your ads, you got $1,500, "so they locked in gains and cancelled."
- **Common phrases:** "show up and throw up" · "split-test myself into poverty" · "one-legged stool" (channel concentration) · "bleeding-neck problem."
- **Other:** "We don't have a budget, but we'd love a proposal." (cue to pivot done-for-you → done-with-you)

## Market: B2B SaaS / AI sales founders (selling to startups + enterprise)
*(retained from v0 — First Round Capital; smaller slice now but distinct.)*
- **Desire:** a vendor who'll "actually show up and help if I run into an issue" — reliability over feature count; "strongly opinionated about the best way," not "a vending machine for software." `[1 src]`
- **Firm notion:** "I bet on the founder, not feature parity." "Caring is contagious through the sales process."
- **Shakeable notion:** "Software vendors don't actually show up — since we've been buying AI tools, not one company sent teams." A vendor who *does* flips it instantly.
