## ✅ Promoted — persuasion / offer / copy core (corroborated by 3+ distinct creators)

| Framework | What it is | When to use | Corroboration |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Risk-reversal ladder (weak→strong)** | Money-back-if-no-result → result/performance guarantee → "you don't pay until I deliver." Buyers fear *losing money* more than they want the win, so decrease the risk rather than inflate the promise. Field variants: "10× better than money back" + *proactive refund reminder* (Myron Golden); "if I can't save you ≥20% I won't even propose" (Koniak); guarantee only what you control. No case studies = a risk reversal is **mandatory**. | Any offer, especially cold traffic or thin proof. | **12** — Daniel Fazio, Patrick Dang, Joel Kaplan, Frank Kern, Rory Vaden, Stephen Pope, Jose Rosado, Matt Macnamara, **Myron Golden, Trent Dressel, Ian Koniak, Grant Cardone** |
| **Sell the result, not the mechanism** | "Sell the vacation, not the flight." Lead with the outcome/experience; the *how* is nearly irrelevant. Field language: "sell the payoff not the pieces / the grass not the seed / nobody buys the product, they buy the offer = your description of the transformation" (Myron Golden); "people buy business outcomes, not speeds & feeds" (Koniak); "thirst-quencher, not room-temp water" (Dressel). | Every offer, headline, pitch. The #1 convergent field principle. | **11** — Hormozi, Daniel Fazio, Patrick Dang, R.W. Bly, Joel Kaplan, Stephen Pope, April Dunford, **Myron Golden, Ian Koniak, Trent Dressel, Josh Braun** |
| **Take-away / disqualification close** | Be the matchmaker, not the closer: "It's not for everybody," "I only take N clients," willing to walk. "You probably shouldn't" / "buy theirs, you're not ready for me" (Myron Golden); "this deal is closed, you can't put your money in" (Cardone); "don't book the call if you're not serious" (Koniak). The enemy is "maybe," not "no." | When a prospect hesitates or over-eagerly agrees. (Canon: **Meta Close** / **Doctor Not Waiter**.) | **10** — Patrick Dang, Jose Rosado, Frank Kern, Rory Vaden, April Dunford, Matt Macnamara, **Myron Golden, Grant Cardone, Ian Koniak, Trent Dressel** |
| **Detach from the outcome / kill "commission breath"** | The *posture* under the take-away: neediness repels, service attracts. "Lean out and they lean in" / "divorce yourself from the need for a yes" (Myron Golden); "quota breath — the person willing to walk away has the most leverage" (Dressel); "when you want to sell you repel; know your runway so you don't take bad deals" (Koniak); "sorting, not selling — stay neutral" (Josh Braun). Shapes copy *tone*: write from abundance, never desperation. | All sales copy & conversation; the emotional register of the whole piece. | **6** — Myron Golden, Trent Dressel, Ian Koniak, Grant Cardone, Josh Braun (+ Patrick Dang, Frank Kern prior) |
| **Pain → Dream → Bridge (A→B / gap-island)** | Make painful Point A vivid, make dream Point B vivid (and numeric), position your offer as the only bridge; raise the stakes on the pain side so they jump. "Here's where you are / where you want to be / the shortcut I built" (Myron, Path of Least Resistance); POV "dream→obstacle→bridge" (Koniak); "away-from vs toward" (Josh Braun). | Discovery, leads, sales pages, demos — structural spine of a pitch. | **9** — Patrick Dang, Cole Gordon, Matt Macnamara (GAP), Joel Kaplan, Stephen Pope, Frank Kern, **Ian Koniak, Trent Dressel, Josh Braun** |
| **Make the prospect sell themselves** | Ask, don't pitch — they voice the pain and the solution. "WFA: Wait For an Answer; the gift is listening, not gab" (Myron); "be interested, not interesting" (Koniak, Brian Tracy); ask one question, then *silence* — "the second thing they say is more valuable than the first" (Dressel). 80/20: listen 80%, talk 20%. | Discovery & close on any call; content that asks the reader's own questions back. | **9** — Patrick Dang, Cole Gordon, Jose Rosado, Vanessa Lau, Joel Kaplan, **Myron Golden, Ian Koniak, Brian Tracy, Trent Dressel** |
| **Anchor high + trade every concession** | Quote above your floor; never drop price for free — every discount buys a sign-by date, prepay, longer term, or fewer deliverables; make the discount hard to get. "Trade never give: if I get you this price, then you sign by [date]" (Dressel); "don't lead with discounts — validate commitment first" (Koniak); raise price the instant proof shows up (Myron). | Pricing & negotiation on any call/page. | **9** — Patrick Dang, Jacob Cass, Joel Kaplan, Frank Kern, Cole Gordon, Matt Macnamara, **Trent Dressel, Ian Koniak, Myron Golden** |
| **Cost of inaction / loss aversion** | Buyers move ~2× harder to avoid a loss than to chase a gain — so quantify the cost of *doing nothing*. "Make the cost of NO higher than the cost of yes" (Myron); "every month you wait costs ~$100k" / "they fear messing up more than missing out" (Koniak); "pain of change vs pain of the same" (Mike Fiascone); "people act faster to fix a problem than gain a benefit" (Chet Holmes); heighten pain before price matters (Josh Braun). | Closing stalls, "no decision," urgency in any sales page/email. | **6** — Ian Koniak, Trent Dressel, Myron Golden, Josh Braun, Mike Fiascone, Chet Holmes |
| **Niche down to escape price competition** | "If you're for everybody, you're for nobody." Hyper-specific avatar + named mechanism commands a premium ("to be perceived *better*, first be perceived *different*"). Premium-as-proof: "if you're the cheapest no one believes you're the best." | Positioning, pricing, offer design. | **8** — Patrick Dang, Vanessa Lau, Tom Noske, Daniel Fazio, Liam James Kay, Jose Rosado, April Dunford, **Myron Golden** |
| **Price the value/result, never the hour** | Price = a fraction of the value/revenue created. "Charge one-tenth of the result" / "$40k/hr because *I* value my time" (Myron, 10× rule); ROI-anchor "spend $20k to make $720k" (Koniak); value-anchor against the cost of the problem, not the product (Dressel). Hourly punishes speed and caps you. | Any service/offer pricing & the copy that justifies it. | **8** — Patrick Dang, Jose Rosado, Stephen Pope, Daniel Fazio, April Dunford, **Myron Golden, Ian Koniak, Trent Dressel** |
| **Install belief before the ask (two sales)** | Sell the *idea/method* before the product — buyer must believe in (1) YOU, (2) the VEHICLE, (3) that THEY can do it. "Sales is a transference of belief — they don't have to believe what you say, they have to believe that *you* believe it" (Koniak); faith vs doubt = belief in the outcome you do/don't want (Myron). Diagnose which belief is missing when a deal stalls. | Whole sales conversation + the content that precedes it. | **7** — Cole Gordon, Patrick Dang, April Dunford, Frank Kern, Joel Kaplan, **Ian Koniak, Myron Golden** |
| **Content pre-sells the call (authority-first)** | Prospects consume ~7 hrs / ~11 touchpoints before buying (Dunbar 7-11); "the call should feel like taking the order." Give "community-service content" ~10:1 value-to-ask; build the audience *before* the product so it "sells out in two days"; "become the person people say yes to *before* they know the offer" (Myron). | Content strategy feeding any offer. | **7** — Patrick Dang, Daniel Fazio, Frank Kern, Matt Gray, Stephen Pope, **Myron Golden, Ian Koniak** |
| **Isolate the objection** | First objection is usually a smokescreen: "[objection] aside, is there anything *else* holding you back?" "If budget weren't an issue, would you still move forward? Is this the ONLY thing?" (Koniak); "budget aside, would that move it up your list?" (Josh Braun). Surface the real blocker before handling anything. | Any objection on a call or in FAQ/close copy. | **7** — Matt Macnamara, Cole Gordon, Patrick Dang, Joel Kaplan, **Ian Koniak, Trent Dressel, Josh Braun** |
| **Spouse / "think about it" handler** | Don't sell through it. Isolate → confirm THEIR own certainty → surface the hidden real objection (often the buyer's own scarcity, not the spouse) → reframe → only then handle the external blocker. A champion who isn't 100% convinced can't sell internally. | Stalls at the close. | **6** — Cole Gordon, Matt Macnamara, Patrick Dang, Joel Kaplan, Hormozi, **Ian Koniak** |
| **No-oriented Qs + Mirror + Label (Chris Voss)** | Ask for the "no" (feels safe): "Would it be a ridiculous idea if…"; mirror the last 1–3 words; label emotions ("it seems like…"). Re-engage ghosts via the psychology of quitting: "Have you given up on this?" / "Are you alive?" (people resist feeling like quitters). | Cold calls, objection handling, re-engaging ghosts. | **6** — Matt Macnamara, Jose Rosado, Rory Vaden, Patrick Dang, **Josh Braun, Ian Koniak** (Voss = source) |
| **AI + HI copy workflow** | Don't one-shot. Brain-dump → AI builds the rough draft → "make it sound cool" → final "rewrite in my voice" pass (trained on 8–10 of your own pieces). "Iron Man / Jarvis model — AI is augmentation, you're the last mile" (Koniak); ChatGPT prompt-refinement loop that makes the email *about the customer*, not you. AI alone = generic "copies of copies." | Drafting any copy with AI. | **6** — Frank Kern, Patrick Dang, Tiago Forte, Ami Williamson, **Ian Koniak, John Barrows** |
| **Swipe-then-remix ("reference / nothing is original")** | Find the top 3–5 in your niche, study the *packaging* (~70% of success), copy structure line-for-line to internalize, then inject your story. "Model 3 things: beliefs, mental syntax, physiology" / "find someone like you only smarter, do what they do until you find your own" (Myron); model top reps + get their decks (Koniak). | Idea generation; building hooks/offers. | **6** — Patrick Dang, Jose Rosado (ECHO), Matt Gray, Daniel Fazio, **Myron Golden, Ian Koniak** |
| **Problem-first discovery: dig & quantify the pain** | After permission, name 2–3 crispy 5th-grade problems your buyers face; ~90% one lands, else they correct you. Then ladder: "Why is that a problem? What's it costing you?" — "a problem isn't a problem until you quantify the pain." Josh Braun's *Poke-the-Bear / illumination* question: find a problem, don't ask about one. | Cold calls, problem-aware leads, demos, VSL pain-stacking. | **5** — Matt Macnamara, Patrick Dang, **Trent Dressel, Myron Golden, Josh Braun** |
| **Cold-call / cold-DM opening formula** | (1) "Hey [Name], this is [You] from [Company], how are you?" (2) **"The reason I'm calling you *specifically* is…"** (humans need a reason; it settles "who/why me") (3) permission micro-ask: "I know I'm calling out of the blue — 30 seconds?" (~80–90% earn it) (4) value as OUTCOME not feature (5) interest-based CTA. | Any cold outbound — call, DM, or email open. | **5** — Trent Dressel, Matt Macnamara, Patrick Dang, Rory Vaden, **Ian Koniak** |
| **OPPS cold-email anatomy** | Observation (buyer-focused, never open with "I") → Problem tied to it → one-sentence Solution → interest-based CTA → PS. 50–150 words, 5th-grade English, white space, mobile eye-test, follow up 3–4×. (Dressel's OPS-CTA-PS sourced 73 meetings.) | Writing cold email at scale. | **5** — Matt Macnamara, Patrick Dang, Daniel Fazio, Joel Kaplan, **Trent Dressel** |
| **Tonality > words (~70% of a call)** | "It's not what you say, it's how you say it." Lower inflection, slower pace, strategic pauses; "slow and low" before an important question; "if the call feels rushed, *you're* rushing it." Audit by re-listening to your own calls. Write the desired *feeling* next to each script line. | Every spoken sales interaction; VSL/video delivery. | **5** — Patrick Dang, Matt Macnamara, Cole Gordon, **Trent Dressel, Myron Golden** |
| **Upfront agenda + permission to say no (two-outcome close)** | Open by naming the two acceptable endings and getting buy-in: *"If we both see alignment we continue; if not, we part ways — how does that sound?"* Risk-free framing: *"By the end you'll either feel more confident in your current solution, or see a real opportunity."* Controls the call while the buyer feels in control. | Start (and meeting-booking ask) of every discovery call / demo. | **6** — Matt Macnamara, Patrick Dang, Vanessa Lau, Stephen Pope, **Trent Dressel, Ian Koniak** |
| **Positioning one-liner: "I help [X] do [Y] so they can [Z]"** | The verbal value prop / "what do you do" answer. X = specific avatar/vertical, Y = what you deliver, Z = business outcome. Must be **Desirable + Measurable + Statable** in a <60-sec soundbite; "sell the grass, not the seed"; clarity test = "so simple your grandma gets it." | Bios, headlines, elevator pitch, ad/LP one-liners. | **3** — Ian Koniak, Myron Golden, Trent Dressel |
| **Same content, escalating delivery vehicle = escalating price** | Identical IP repriced by *how it's delivered*: book → course → mastermind → VIP day; "move the zero" (same content, 1/10th the volume for the same money). "People pay more for the exact same content packaged differently" (Russell Brunson: $25k/$50k/$115k/$250k). | Building an offer ladder / productizing a service. | **3** — Myron Golden, Russell Brunson, Ian Koniak |
| **Sell the discovery process / diagnose-before-prescribe** | Be the *consultant* (no product to show, must show you can help), not the seller. "Doctor of selling: examine → diagnose → prescribe" (Brian Tracy); "sell the discovery, not the solution — be the buyer, not the seller; interview, don't pitch" (Koniak). Earn the right to prescribe; never lead with credentials/product. Maps to canon **Doctor-Not-Waiter**. | Enterprise/high-ticket discovery; consultative sales pages. | **4** — Ian Koniak, Brian Tracy, Myron Golden (trusted-advisor), Josh Braun |

## 📈 Promoted — prospecting & sales-motion (corroborated, less copy-central)
*Real and cross-corroborated, but operational rather than persuasion-copy. Kept compact.*
- **Trigger-event / buying-signal prospecting** (5: Patrick Dang, Matt Macnamara, Daniel Fazio, Trent Dressel, Ian Koniak/John Barrows) — reach out right after a signal (funding, new exec, hiring the role you replace, used-to-work-at-your-best-customer alumni filter ~32% reply).
- **Dream 100 / hand-picked best-buyer list** (5: Chet Holmes [source], Daniel Fazio, Patrick Dang, Ian Koniak, Trent Dressel) — concentrate outreach on a small set of ideal targets; "more touches for fewer people in the right accounts." "If you can't get a meeting manually, you won't automate it."
- **Master one channel before adding a second** (3: Cole Gordon, Patrick Dang, Daniel Fazio) — scale the channel doing ~80% of meetings before channel-hopping.
- **Show-up-rate engineering** (3: Cole Gordon, Hormozi, Trent Dressel) — book ≤2 days out, confirm live, send the invite immediately, "Call Reminder" email night-before with a value deposit; worth more than close rate.
- **Reverse-math the funnel ("Moneyball" / "move the zero")** (3: Trent Dressel, Ian Koniak, Myron Golden) — back-calculate daily activity from the revenue target; copy-relevant cousin: "pick a price point, reverse-engineer volume" for offer design.

## ⚠️ Currency flags
- **AI = augmentation, not automation (2025–26) — now strongly corroborated.** Pure-AI copy is burning out ("everyone sounds the same," "feels like a scam" to high-ticket buyers). Winning move is AI+HI: "be the last mile / Iron Man + Jarvis" (Koniak), "we won't be replaced by AI, we'll be replaced by reps who use AI" (John Barrows). The em-dash now reads as an AI tell ("remove the dashes"). *(Frank Kern, Patrick Dang, Ian Koniak, John Barrows.)*
- **NEW — "Build it ourselves with AI" is the dominant B2B objection (2025–26):** ~73% of surveyed sellers hit "we can build it with AI / don't want a 3–5yr lock-in" (Koniak). Counter by quantifying who *owns/maintains/secures* the build long-term + the opportunity cost of engineering time. Bake this into B2B offer copy.
- **NEW — "Cold calling is dead" is itself a saturated contrarian hook.** Dozens of "cold calling is dead/not dead" titles in the corpus; the contrarian frame is now a cliché — the *permission opener* and *reason-I'm-calling* tactics still work, the headline gimmick is tired.
- **"Dark psychology" packaging** remains a titling trend, not new technique (anchoring, Feel-Felt-Found, mirroring, takeaway dressed up). Treat the label as ephemeral.
- **Coaching / "make money online" saturation** (Fazio): crowded (CPA $4–5K, median seller ~$87k/yr). Note the *meta*-layer too — "how to get rich / sell / cold-call" coaching is now the single most-harvested category, so its tactics are the most widely *copied* (and thus fastest-burning).
