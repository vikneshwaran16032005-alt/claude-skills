| **Risk-reversal ladder (weak→strong)** | Money-back-if-no-result → result/performance guarantee → "you don't pay until I deliver." Buyers fear *losing money* more than they want the win, so decrease the risk rather than inflate the promise. Field variants: "10× better than money back" + *proactive refund reminder* (Myron Golden); "if I can't save you ≥20% I won't even propose" (Koniak); guarantee only what you control; *micro risk-reversal* "send me your cold email, I'll send a revision — no cost, no ask; if you like it, let's talk" (Josh Braun); "remove the excuse — money-back guarantee" (30MPC); **bake the guarantee into the headline itself** — "Increase your store's conversions in 3 days or I'll refund your money" (Neville Medhora); **radical transparency as risk-reversal** — hand the buyer a written list of every reason *not* to buy / every skeleton in the closet up front; they'll find them in diligence anyway, so disclosing first builds trust (Suli Ali). | **Take-away / disqualification close** | Be the matchmaker, not the closer: "It's not for everybody," "I only take N clients," willing to walk. | **Sell the result, not the mechanism** | "Sell the vacation, not the flight." Lead with the outcome/experience; the *how* is nearly irrelevant. Field language: "nobody buys swords, they buy the superpower" / "you sell fireballs, not flowers — the flower is the feature, the fireball is what they can now do" (Josh Braun); "people buy business outcomes, not speeds & feeds" (Koniak); "sell the payoff not the pieces / the grass not the seed" (Myron Golden); "say what it *means*, not what it *is*" (30MPC, demo-the-why); the headline is the end-result + customer + timeframe, the mechanism never appears (Neville Medhora); **sell the *initiative*, not the feature** — tie the feature to a funded business priority all the way to revenue ("help sellers forecast better SO THAT revenue is predictable and investors gain confidence"); closer to revenue = more believable (30MPC/Kyle Coleman). | Every offer, headline, pitch. The #1 convergent field principle. | **13** — Hormozi, Daniel Fazio, Patrick Dang, R.W. Bly, Joel Kaplan, Stephen Pope, April Dunford, Myron Golden, Ian Koniak, Trent Dressel, Josh Braun, 30MPC, **Neville Medhora** |
| **Anchor high + trade every concession** | Quote above your floor; never drop price for free. **Four Levers of Negotiation (Todd Caponi):** every discount must be *paid for* with one of — (1) volume, (2) timing of cash / prepay, (3) length of commitment, (4) timing of the deal. "Trade never give" (Dressel); "anchor high then invite a counter — there's no cost to anchoring high" (30MPC); **concede on terms, not value** — teach the legitimate lanes to a discount (longer term, a committed sign-by date), and tie the discount to *signature speed* (15% if signed in 2 weeks, 10% in 4) so it drives velocity instead of eroding price (Firenzi); **anchor against the prize, not the competitor** — quantify their #1 initiative's value ($5M) first so a $50K gap feels trivial (30MPC); a *"light lock-in"* (pre-schedule the kickoff) worth ~10% of ACV beats discounting; **popcorn pricing** — price tiers non-linearly so the biggest is wildly the best value-per-unit (Belal Batrawy); make the discount hard to get (defer to a manager / CC the CEO, send 4 hrs later); anchor against premium competitors then be the value pick — "we charge $50K/week; McKinsey/Bain/BCG charge $200K/week — we're 75% cheaper but better, and we're actual practitioners" (Jesse Pujji). | Pricing & negotiation on any call/page. | **14** — Patrick Dang, Jacob Cass, Joel Kaplan, Frank Kern, Cole Gordon, Matt Macnamara, Trent Dressel, Ian Koniak, Myron Golden, 30MPC, Todd Caponi, Belal Batrawy, Jesse Pujji, **Firenzi** |
| **Pain → Dream → Bridge (A→B / gap-island)** | Make painful Point A vivid, make dream Point B vivid (and numeric), position your offer as the only bridge; raise the stakes on the pain side so they jump. Field: "before & after — what sucked before they paid vs. what got better after; mine LinkedIn comments for the before, testimonial pages for the after" (Josh Braun); "existing state → promised land" (30MPC); **Heaven & Hell** — paint the vision *and* what happens if unsolved; the gap creates urgency (Alex Kremer). | Discovery, leads, sales pages, demos — structural spine of a pitch. | **11** — Patrick Dang, Cole Gordon, Matt Macnamara (GAP), Joel Kaplan, Stephen Pope, Frank Kern, Ian Koniak, Trent Dressel, Josh Braun, **30MPC, Alex Kremer** |
| **Make the prospect sell themselves** | Ask, don't pitch — they voice the pain and the solution. "Be interested, not interesting; hand them the mic" (Koniak, 30MPC); ask one question then *silence* — "the second thing they say is worth more than the first" (Dressel). **Make them hold the benefit (BLC, Kevin Dorsey):** instead of "does that make sense?", ask "how much faster is that than what you do now?" / "who'd you roll this out to first?" so the benefit comes out in THEIR words. 80/20: listen 80%, talk 20%. **The Mom Test (Rob Fitzpatrick):** never ask "would you buy this"; confirm (a) they have the problem and (b) they're the archetype, then close "you said you wanted X, I'm offering X — do you want it?" (~80% close). **Diagnose by trying to close mid-discovery** — if they balk at the offer, the need was weak; the balk itself is the diagnostic (MFM). **Push-pull / permission to deprioritize** — give explicit permission to say it's NOT a priority so the honest answer surfaces; **no-lose confirmation** — "here's what I'm gathering, do I have this right?" (yes = you look sharp, no = they hand you the truth). | Discovery & close on any call; content that asks the reader's own questions back. | **12** — Patrick Dang, Cole Gordon, Jose Rosado, Vanessa Lau, Joel Kaplan, Myron Golden, Ian Koniak, Brian Tracy, Trent Dressel, Josh Braun, Kevin Dorsey, **Rob Fitzpatrick** |
| **Problem-first discovery: dig & quantify the pain** | After permission, name 2–3 crispy 5th-grade problems your buyers face; ~90% one lands, else they correct you. **Problem Proposition (vs value prop):** spend ~80% on the problem, ~one sentence on the solution — Gong (300M+ calls): buzzword value-prop ≈5.5% success vs problem-led ≈16% (**3×**). **Problem/Discovery Tree:** Situation → Operational problem (champion's daily annoyance, 4–5-fig pain) → Executive problem (C-suite risk, 6–7-fig pain) — climb it to sell bigger. **Chunk up by seniority** (above/below the line): managers get the *process* problem, VP/C-suite get the *business* problem attached. Then ladder: "Why is that a problem? What's it costing you?" — a problem isn't a problem until you quantify it. **Invert your benefit/feature copy into pain** to *generate* the questions — flip each benefit line into the painful before-state it implies ("a more scalable way" → "so today it's taking tons of time / things are breaking"); **"So what?" laddering** pushes a small pain up to C-level/existential impact; **"typically" + pylon** language ("a lot of folks also deal with C and D") surfaces more pain and makes them feel understood. *Poke-the-Bear / illumination* (Josh Braun): find a problem, don't ask about one; **Magic-Moment** Q: "when did you realize that was a problem?" turns pain into a story. **Value-before-validation:** "nobody cares if your product works if they're not already convinced that it working matters" — validate "if this worked, does it matter to you?" *before* any demo (30MPC/Mallika Sahay). **Pattern-match on initiatives, not industry/persona:** find why customers actually bought (IPO path, M&A, intl expansion) and target prospects showing that same trigger (30MPC/Kyle Coleman). | Cold calls, problem-aware leads, demos, VSL pain-stacking. | **10** — Matt Macnamara, Patrick Dang, Trent Dressel, Myron Golden, Josh Braun, 30MPC, Kevin Dorsey, Keenan, Jason Bay, **Kyle Coleman** |
| **Cold-call / cold-DM opening formula** | **Tailored Permission Opener** (Gong-best, 11.18% vs 2.15% for "did I catch you at a bad time"): (1) lead with something about THEM (a trigger/research) — *context first is what breaks the telemarketer stigma*, (2) own that it's a cold call ("I'll be honest, this is a cold call — but a well-researched one"), (3) permission micro-ask ("can I steal 30 seconds on why I called, then you can hang up on me?"). Variant: **"heard the name tossed around"** (11.24%) — lead with a shared thread (investor/competitor/geo) before your name. Then value as OUTCOME not feature; interest-based CTA. | Any cold outbound — call, DM, or email open. | **9** — Trent Dressel, Matt Macnamara, Patrick Dang, Rory Vaden, Ian Koniak, **Josh Braun, 30MPC, Giulio Segantini, Armand Farrokh** |
| **OPPS / 3x3 cold-email anatomy** | Observation/trigger (buyer-focused, never open with "I") → Problem tied to it + its impact → one-sentence Solution → **interest- or offer-based** CTA → PS. 50–100 words, 5th-grade English, one idea per email, white space, mobile eye-test, follow up 3–4×. Named field variants that all reduce to this: **REPLY method**, **3x3**, Josh Braun's **4-line** (emotion/true-situation → problem/stakes → 3rd-party credibility → low-friction ask) and **Mad Libs** fill-in system. **Personal > personalized:** a true situation the whole segment faces *scales* when 1:1 personalization isn't possible. Gong (85M emails): personalized opener ≈5× reply; problem + social proof up to +42%. **Length discipline (30MPC reply-method):** keep it **under ~140 words** — "if you have to take a breath in the middle of a sentence, it's too long." Competitor-displacement open that beats company-bragging: "We see you're using [competitor] — we sell ours 40% cheaper. Interested?" (don't even lead with your own company name). | Writing cold email at scale. | **9** — Matt Macnamara, Patrick Dang, Daniel Fazio, Joel Kaplan, Trent Dressel, **Josh Braun, 30MPC, Kevin Dorsey, Jason Bay** |
| **Price the value/result, never the hour** | Price = a fraction of the value/revenue created. "Charge one-tenth of the result" (Myron, 10× rule); ROI-anchor "spend $20k to make $720k" (Koniak); "you're either making them money, saving them money, or preventing a lawsuit — that's the only reason they buy" (30MPC/Josh Braun); value-anchor against the cost of the problem, not the product. **Deliver price in one confident sentence, then go silent** — never add "list price" / "I'll see what I can do" / "end of quarter" (each implies a discount); **Ferrari-dealership posture** — "this is our $250K car; cash, check, or financing — what'll it be?" — never pre-apologize or pre-discount (30MPC). **Gate price behind value** — don't show a number until 3–5 pains resonate and it's a real short-list deal; **never blind-send a pricing PDF** — "this isn't Louie & Ernie's pizzeria, I can't show you a menu — license count, term, integrations all dictate price; let's do 15 min live" (Johnny Larson); show a *cost-avoidance box*, never list price. **Let the prospect price it:** ask "if you used it, how much would it help — in revenue or time saved?", quantify, then price against *that* number (MFM/Neville); **price = willingness to pay, not your formula** — call every customer, ask "what would you pay?", charge exactly that (→ +20% EBITDA in month one); don't undersell info — a $500 PDF reframed into $12K/$25K/$100K tiers made ~$300K (Jesse Pujji). Hourly punishes speed and caps you. | Any service/offer pricing & the copy that justifies it. | **12** — Patrick Dang, Jose Rosado, Stephen Pope, Daniel Fazio, April Dunford, Myron Golden, Ian Koniak, Trent Dressel, 30MPC, Jesse Pujji, Neville Medhora, **Johnny Larson** |
| **Niche down to escape price competition** | "If you're for everybody, you're for nobody." Hyper-specific avatar + named mechanism commands a premium ("to be perceived *better*, first be perceived *different*"). Premium-as-proof: "if you're the cheapest no one believes you're the best." | Positioning, pricing, offer design. | **8** — Patrick Dang, Vanessa Lau, Tom Noske, Daniel Fazio, Liam James Kay, Jose Rosado, April Dunford, Myron Golden |
| **Cost of inaction / loss aversion** | Buyers move ~2× harder to avoid a loss than to chase a gain — so quantify the cost of *doing nothing*. "Make the cost of NO higher than the cost of yes" (Myron); manufacture an **"artificial compelling event"** from cost-of-inaction — "every month you don't adopt costs labor + lost revenue" — better than discounting to quarter-end (30MPC); **quantify the pain in 4 layers** — time spent → opportunity cost of that time → cost of errors → downstream time/attrition — to turn a vague problem into 7 figures, then **"compared to what?"** reframes price against *that* number, never against a discount (30MPC); **work the deadline backwards** from the buyer's go-live date as the compelling event ("if you want this live by June 15, the contract signs no later than Jan 5"); the two drivers of change = **present pain + fear of future pain**; without both, "they feel no need to change and you get tons of objections" (Jeremy Miner). The real competitor is **"no decision" / the status quo.** | Closing stalls, "no decision," urgency in any sales page/email. | **8** — Ian Koniak, Trent Dressel, Myron Golden, Josh Braun, Mike Fiascone, Chet Holmes, **30MPC, Jeremy Miner** |
| **Isolate the objection** | First objection is usually a smokescreen: "[objection] aside, is there anything *else* holding you back?" "Independent of price — are we even your vendor of choice? If not, you haven't earned the negotiation" (30MPC); solution-isolation: "if this were *free*, could you see yourself using it?" separates fit from price (30MPC); "budget aside, would that move it up your list?" (Josh Braun). Surface the real blocker before handling anything. | Any objection on a call or in FAQ/close copy. | **8** — Matt Macnamara, Cole Gordon, Patrick Dang, Joel Kaplan, Ian Koniak, Trent Dressel, Josh Braun, **30MPC** |
| **Disarm before logic / agree-don't-fight** | **"When you're explaining, you're losing."** An objection is emotional (amygdala) — meet it with logic and you trigger reactance. **Mr. Miyagi / Defuse→Discover→Discuss / ACE (Accept-Consent-Explore):** (1) genuinely agree to drop the pressure (even gas up the competitor), (2) ask permission + incentivize them to share ("just so no one calls you again, is it A, B, or C?"), (3) sell the test drive, not the car. **Reframe the objection as data, not resistance** — it reveals what they care about; shift from emotion to strategy, then ask "what matters most right now?" (John Barrows/Tim Castle). Never say "sorry" (lowers status); **The Ledge** = a pre-scripted reflex line to buy a beat ("Shoot, this one's totally on me"); a **humbling disclaimer** lowers the guard before a hard question ("I feel a little awkward asking, but what'd be the top one or two reasons you wouldn't move forward, if any?"); the **"do-over"** — if you missed a question, just go back ("I felt silly, I didn't ask about X — got a second?"); **off-the-record reset** ("mind if I ask a few off the record? I'll pause the recording") surfaces the real competitor. "The prospect is the best person to change the prospect's mind." | Objection handling on calls AND objection pre-emption in FAQ/close copy — validate the reader's resistance before answering it. | **7** — Josh Braun, 30MPC, Giulio Segantini, Jeremy Miner, Jeb Blount, **John Barrows** (Chris Voss = source) |
| **No-oriented Qs + Mirror + Label + "because" (Chris Voss)** | Ask for the "no" (feels safe): "Would it be a ridiculous/terrible idea if…"; mirror the last 1–3 words; **label** emotions ("it seems like…") — even a wrong label gets corrected; a **label beats a question** — "seems like the value just isn't there for you" makes them feel understood (oxytocin) where a question feels like interrogation (label positives to grow them, negatives to shrink them); **negotiate for the "no"** — reformat so the answer you want is a *no* ("Do you disagree?"), which feels safer and forces a decision; **accusation audit** — pre-name the negative ("I know my timing is terrible"); kill the word **"why"** (triggers defensiveness), use "what/how"; justify any ask with **"because"** (the printer-line study). Re-engage ghosts via the psychology of quitting: "Have you given up on this?" | Cold calls, objection handling, re-engaging ghosts. | **8** — Matt Macnamara, Jose Rosado, Rory Vaden, Patrick Dang, Josh Braun, Ian Koniak, **30MPC, Jeremy Miner** (Voss = source) |
| **Tonality > words (~70–90% of a call)** | "It's not what you say, it's how you say it." Lower inflection, slower pace, strategic pauses; "slow your tonality down more than feels natural so the brain registers *stranger* before you deliver value" (Josh Braun); the **5 Tones** + familiar-tone open — repeat the name twice, stutter/shuffle papers to trigger curiosity not fight-or-flight (Jeremy Miner); "strong and wrong over weak and meek." Audit by re-listening to your own calls; write the desired *feeling* next to each script line. | Every spoken sales interaction; VSL/video delivery. | **8** — Patrick Dang, Matt Macnamara, Cole Gordon, Trent Dressel, Myron Golden, **Josh Braun, 30MPC, Jeremy Miner** |
| **Upfront agenda + permission to say no (PPO / two-outcome close)** | Open by naming how the time will be spent and the two acceptable endings, then get buy-in. **PPO/PAO — Purpose · Plan · Outcome:** "why we're here → how we'll spend the time → the go/no-go decision we'll make at the end"; each call's outcome seeds the next call's purpose. Risk-free framing: "by the end you'll either feel more confident in your current solution, or see a real opportunity." **90-second rule:** in the first 90 sec prove you know their business and respect their time, to be categorized as a *peer, not a vendor*; stating up front you'll ask for a decision at the end keeps later questions from triggering alarm. Controls the call while the buyer feels in control. | Start (and meeting-booking ask) of every discovery call / demo. | **8** — Matt Macnamara, Patrick Dang, Vanessa Lau, Stephen Pope, Trent Dressel, Ian Koniak, **30MPC, Armand Farrokh** |
| **Install belief before the ask (two sales)** | Sell the *idea/method* before the product — buyer must believe in (1) YOU, (2) the VEHICLE, (3) that THEY can do it. "Sales is a transference of feeling/passion — they still buy from people they like and trust" (Zig Ziglar via 30MPC); "buy with emotion, justify with reason"; **System 1 / System 2** — people run reactive ~90% of the day, find the way into System 2 to change behavior (30MPC). Diagnose which belief is missing when a deal stalls. | Whole sales conversation + the content that precedes it. | **8** — Cole Gordon, Patrick Dang, April Dunford, Frank Kern, Joel Kaplan, Ian Koniak, Myron Golden, **30MPC** |
| **Content pre-sells the call (authority-first)** | Prospects consume ~7 hrs / ~11 touchpoints before buying (Dunbar 7-11); "the call should feel like taking the order." Give "community-service content" ~10:1 value-to-ask; build the audience *before* the product so it "sells out in two days." | Content strategy feeding any offer. | **7** — Patrick Dang, Daniel Fazio, Frank Kern, Matt Gray, Stephen Pope, Myron Golden, Ian Koniak |
| **Detach from the outcome / kill "commission breath"** | The *posture* under the take-away: neediness repels, service attracts. **"Commission breath"** (you can smell desperation — Josh Braun's signature term, all over the corpus) / "quota breath"; "lean out and they lean in" (Myron); "the person willing to walk away has the most leverage" (Dressel); "detach from the *get* — aim for the truth (yes-interested / no-not-now), not the meeting" (Josh Braun); "in the first 30 sec you have *negative* trust — don't go assumptive" (30MPC). Shapes copy *tone*: write from abundance, never desperation. | All sales copy & conversation; the emotional register of the whole piece. | **7** — Myron Golden, Trent Dressel, Ian Koniak, Grant Cardone, Josh Braun, **30MPC** (+ Patrick Dang, Frank Kern prior) |
| **AI + HI copy workflow** | Don't one-shot. Brain-dump → AI builds the rough draft → "make it sound cool" → final "rewrite in my voice" pass (trained on 8–10 of your own pieces). **"Garbage in / garbage out":** humans build the principles/scorecard/value-prop bullets, THEN feed AI the raw material; **"red pen vs. black pen"** — let AI get you ~80%, you're the last mile (30MPC); "Iron Man / Jarvis" (Koniak). AI alone = generic "copies of copies." *(See Currency flags: the literal word "AI" now tanks reply rates.)* | Drafting any copy with AI. | **7** — Frank Kern, Patrick Dang, Tiago Forte, Ami Williamson, Ian Koniak, John Barrows, **30MPC** |
| **Swipe-then-remix ("reference / nothing is original")** | Find the top 3–5 in your niche, study the *packaging* (~70% of success), copy structure line-for-line to internalize, then inject your story. "Macaroni-art standard — write cold emails good enough to put on the fridge" (Josh Braun); model top reps + get their decks (Koniak); **keep a running swipe bank** — great copy often hinges on one memorable phrase (the underlined "I AM a man" protest sign as the gold standard); **copywork** — hand-copy the 100 best sales letters (+ great fiction/scripts) ~2–3 hrs/day for ~6 months, "like learning an instrument" (Neville Medhora — classic Halbert practice). | Idea generation; building hooks/offers; deliberate practice. | **7** — Patrick Dang, Jose Rosado (ECHO), Matt Gray, Daniel Fazio, Myron Golden, Ian Koniak, **Neville Medhora** |
| **Sell the discovery process / diagnose-before-prescribe** | Be the *consultant* (no product to show, must show you can help), not the seller. "Doctor of selling: examine → diagnose → prescribe" (Brian Tracy); "sell the meeting, not the product" (Josh Braun); **shadow-based discovery** — ask to watch the painful manual process end-to-end, "a shadow says a million words, drag them through the glass to see where the blood is" (30MPC). Earn the right to prescribe; never lead with credentials/product. Maps to canon **Doctor-Not-Waiter**. | Enterprise/high-ticket discovery; consultative sales pages. | **5** — Ian Koniak, Brian Tracy, Myron Golden, Josh Braun, **30MPC** |
| **Offer-based CTA / "world-class offer" beats asking for time** | The CTA is where most emails die. **Hierarchy (Gong, 85M emails):** ask for time ≈ **−44%** < open-ended question < interest-based ("worth a quick look?") < **offer-based ≈ +28%**. **3 offer tiers (Jason Bay):** (1) "sell the blind date" — hype the expert they'll meet, not the product; (2) one-to-many — a reusable benchmark/report ("we mystery-shopped 300 e-comm cos, your chat is 3× slower"); (3) one-to-one — a custom audit built for them. "An offer is not a demo, a gift card, or a coffee chat." Give a reason to take the meeting *even if they never buy.* 30MPC's Outbound Masterclass reconfirms the swing (asking for time ≈ −28%, an offer ≈ +28%) — "you want their time, but it's the last thing you should ask for"; pair it with a **low-friction risk-reversal CTA**: "I'll send a short video/doc — you tell me if it's worth a look, or if it makes sense to stop here." | The CTA of any cold email / outbound; lead-magnet design. | **4** — Jason Bay, 30MPC, Josh Braun, Vin Matano |
| **Lead with a hypothesis / seek-correction** | It's easier for a prospect to *correct* a wrong statement than to answer an open-ended question — so give them something to react to. **Correct & Confirm (Kevin Dorsey):** present a hypothesis they confirm 100% or correct. **Lead with a hypothesis (John Barrows):** "Based on my research, here's where you're at, which likely means problem X/Y/Z — is that right?" **Cunningham's Law (Nate Nasralla):** don't ask the question — write down the *wrong* answer (2–3 competing problem framings) and let them correct you; rewrite a problem statement 2–3 ways and test which they bite on. "Seek to be corrected, not correct." | Discovery openers; writing/testing problem statements & cold-email problem lines. | **4** — Kevin Dorsey, John Barrows, Nate Nasralla, Brent Adamson |
| **Positioning one-liner: "I help [X] do [Y] so they can [Z]"** | The verbal value prop / "what do you do" answer. X = specific avatar/vertical, Y = what you deliver, Z = business outcome. Must be **Desirable + Measurable + Statable** in a <60-sec soundbite; field LinkedIn-headline form "We help [WHO] [achieve WHAT]" beats "Account Executive at Acme" (Josh Braun); clarity test = "so simple your grandma gets it." | Bios, headlines, elevator pitch, ad/LP one-liners. | **4** — Ian Koniak, Myron Golden, Trent Dressel, **Josh Braun** |
| **Crispy/specific problem — "Hairy Lollipop" / Infomercial test** | Make the problem so specifically, sensorially detailed the reader can *see* it. **Hairy Lollipop Test (30MPC):** if they can't picture it, it's not specific enough — layer persona + annoyances + scenery (time/place) + emotion ("CFOs of insurance-defense firms find it *ridiculous* they fight deductions right after winning a big case"). **Infomercial / black-and-white test (Josh Braun):** describe the BEFORE state so vividly (soggy fries, $325 cashmere sweater shrunk in the dryer) they feel it — no product talk. Harry Dry's 3 tests: can I visualize it? falsify it? can nobody else say it? Generic "save time/money" fails all three. | Editing any problem line, hook, or lead for concreteness. | **3** — Josh Braun, 30MPC, Harry Dry |
| **Same content, escalating delivery vehicle = escalating price** | Identical IP repriced by *how it's delivered*: book → course → mastermind → VIP day; "move the zero." "People pay more for the exact same content packaged differently" (Russell Brunson); a $500 PDF reframed into $12K/$25K/$100K tiers made ~$300K — perceived value rises with price (Jesse Pujji). | Building an offer ladder / productizing a service. | **4** — Myron Golden, Russell Brunson, Ian Koniak, **Jesse Pujji** |

## 📈 Promoted — prospecting & sales-motion (corroborated, less copy-central)

- **Trigger-event / buying-signal prospecting** (6: Patrick Dang, Matt Macnamara, Daniel Fazio, Trent Dressel, Ian Koniak/John Barrows, 30MPC) — reach out right after a signal; **"don't sell the signal"** (30MPC): tie a *fresh* trigger to your value, never "you opened my email 5×." **First-is-best signal stacking** — lead with the first/strongest signal the prospect hits.
- **Dream 100 / hand-picked best-buyer list** (5: Chet Holmes [source], Daniel Fazio, Patrick Dang, Ian Koniak, Trent Dressel) — concentrate outreach on a small set; "if you can't get a meeting manually, you won't automate it." **Martini-glass / "the list is the strategy"** (30MPC) — go narrower and deeper into the accounts most likely to engage, prioritized by the **Hierarchy of Relevance** (persona > industry > company > individual).
- **Burst / multichannel prospecting** (4: 30MPC, Josh Braun, Patrick Dang, Daniel Fazio) — cluster call+email+LinkedIn in tight succession ("spikes, not tidal waves"; patterns = ignorable, bursts = attention); **never a "naked dial."** First touch = triple-tap (call→VM→email→social, ~+3.1× contact rate); **KISS sequencing** — 12–15 touches over 3–4 weeks, batched same-day across all 3 channels to lift contact rate up to 3×; **voicemail double-tap** (only ever 2 VMs, context not pitch, point to the email — *hard data:* 0 VMs = 2.73% reply, 1 = 6.11%, **2 = 5.34%**, 3+ = 2.2%; VM1 = 15s context only, VM2 = 30s context + social proof, both "no need to call back, I'll email — mind replying if moderately interesting?").
- **Champion-building & multithreading** (3: Maddy Jackson, Krysten Conner, 30MPC) — a real **champion** has political + technical clout AND personal pain and can get you to power; test by asking them to set the meeting ("if they can't, they failed" — an influencer ≠ a champion); **"no champion, no deal."** **Sell "betweens"** — 1-to-1 touches to each buying-committee member between meetings (curiosity / insight / FUD about the status quo, *no CTA*); **match message to org altitude** — end users = "does this make my day easier?", managers = "does this move a metric I'm measured on?", execs = "does this hit a goal or mitigate risk?"
- **Gatekeeper triple-bypass** (3: 30MPC, Josh Braun, John Barrows) — dose info bit-by-bit only when asked (slide-by → context/trigger → social proof), end each line on a question; "act like you own the building"; never lie, pitch, or befriend.
- **Stage/agreement-based (not meeting-based) deal progression** (4: Mark Kosoglow, 30MPC, Skip Miller, Ian Koniak) — deals move on exit criteria you *get* (Problem → Solution → Power → Commercial → Vendor), not meetings you *do*; "gives-and-gets" — every give extracts a get.
- **Show-up-rate engineering** (3: Cole Gordon, Hormozi, Trent Dressel) — book ≤2 days out, confirm live, send the invite immediately, value-deposit reminder; "don't drop the baby."
- **Reverse-math the funnel ("Moneyball" / "move the zero")** (3: Trent Dressel, Ian Koniak, Myron Golden) — back-calculate daily activity from the revenue target.

## ⚠️ Currency flags
- **NEW — the literal word "AI" now tanks cold-email reply rates (hard data, 2025–26).** Gong's 85M-cold-email study: **"AI" ≈ −33% to −36% reply rate**, buzzwords ≈ **−57%**, asking for time ≈ **−44%**; offer-based CTA ≈ **+28%**, personalized opener ≈ **5×**. The em-dash and over-polished phrasing read as an AI tell ("strip the dashes"). Sell the emotional outcome, not "AI-powered [feature]." *(30MPC/Gong, Josh Braun.)*
- **NEW — data-backed B2B buzzword blacklist ("SNS test" — Sounds Nice, Signifies Nothing):** "optimize," "streamline," "accelerate," "single source of truth," "single pane of glass," "platform," "world-class," "cutting-edge," "leading provider," "end-to-end," "scalable." Replace with "dumb human language" at a 4th-grade level. *(30MPC, Josh Braun.)*
- **AI = augmentation, not automation (2025–26) — strongly corroborated.** Winning move is AI+HI ("be the last mile / Iron Man + Jarvis"; "we won't be replaced by AI, we'll be replaced by reps who use AI" — Barrows). *(Frank Kern, Patrick Dang, Ian Koniak, John Barrows, 30MPC.)*
- **"Build it ourselves with AI" is the dominant B2B objection (2025–26):** ~73% of sellers hit it. Counter by quantifying who *owns/maintains/secures* the build + opportunity cost of engineering time.
- **"Cold calling is dead/sucks/that's why it works" is a fully saturated contrarian hook.** The corpus is now drowning in these titles — the *permission opener* and *reason-I'm-calling* tactics still work; the headline gimmick is tired.
- **"Dark psychology" packaging** remains a titling trend, not new technique. Treat the label as ephemeral.
- **Coaching / "make money online" + "how to sell/cold-call" saturation:** now the single most-harvested category, so its tactics are the most widely *copied* and fastest-burning. The corpus skews hard to sales-*conversation* content — extract copy-relevant essence (hooks, VOC, offer/guarantee construction, objection pre-emption), not pipeline ops.
