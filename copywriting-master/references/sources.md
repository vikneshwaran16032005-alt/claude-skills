## The Canon (Books in Memory)

### Claude Hopkins — *Scientific Advertising* (1923)
The foundational text. Establishes advertising as a testable, measurable discipline. 21 chapters. Key contributions:
- **Advertising = salesmanship in print** — measure by sales, not aesthetics
- **Keyed returns** — the invention that turned advertising into a science
- **Specificity beats generality** — "3.33× the light" principle
- **Tell the full story** — long copy wins if it sustains interest
- **Don't try to change habits** — channel existing desires; changing habits costs 20× more
- **Sampling** — give samples only to interested people who show interest
- **The only purpose of advertising is to make sales** — not awareness, not "keep the name before the people"

Reference file: `C:\Users\Admin\.claude\projects\E--Copywritter-Claude\memory\book_scientific_advertising.md`

### Gary Halbert — *The Boron Letters* (1984) and *The Gary Halbert Letter*
25 letters from federal prison to his son Bond, later published. Cult-status in direct-response. Key contributions:
- **A-pile / B-pile** — the envelope (or subject line) decides everything
- **Starving Crowd** — find the market first, then the product
- **RFU** (Recency/Frequency/Unit-of-sale) — list quality lens
- **AIDA** — his go-to four-part structure
- **Customization** — personalize copy to segment attributes
- **Reason-why for deals** — "going out of business", "limited to..." etc.
- **Copy-by-hand** — the essential practice regime
- **HALT** (Hungry/Angry/Lonely/Tired) — don't make decisions in these states
- **The $40M Coat-of-Arms letter** — his flagship. A letter with a Polaroid attached. Editorial look beats ad look.

Reference file: `C:\Users\Admin\.claude\projects\E--Copywritter-Claude\memory\book_boron_letters.md`

### Eugene Schwartz — *Breakthrough Advertising* (1966)
The most expensive and frequently-stolen copywriting book ever written. The theoretical apex of the canon. Key contributions:
- **5 Stages of Awareness** (Most / Product / Solution / Problem / Unaware)
- **5 Stages of Market Sophistication** (stages 1-5)
- **7 Techniques of Breakthrough Copy** (Intensification / Identification / Gradualization / Redefinition / Mechanization / Concentration / Camouflage)
- **38 Headline Verbalizations** — idea-generation templates
- **3 Dimensions of Body Copy** (Desires / Identifications / Beliefs)
- **Mass desire** — you channel it, never create it
- **Gradualization of belief** — chain accepted statements to lead to the big claim

Covered via Alex's notes. Reference file: `C:\Users\Admin\.claude\projects\E--Copywritter-Claude\memory\ct_breakthrough_advertising.md`

### Victor Schwab — *How to Write a Good Advertisement* (1962)
The practical companion to Hopkins. Key contributions:
- **5 Fundamentals** — Attention / Advantage / Proof / Persuade / Action
- **22 Interest-Holding Tactics** — readability toolkit
- **4 Categories of Proof** (Construction / Performance / Testimony / Test)
- **8 Milestones to Sale** — gauge how far copy must take the reader

Covered via Alex's notes. Reference file: `C:\Users\Admin\.claude\projects\E--Copywritter-Claude\memory\ct_how_to_write_good_ad.md`

### Mark Ford / Michael Masterson — *The Architecture of Persuasion*; and with John Forde — *Great Leads*
Ford/Masterson is the dean of direct-response in the modern era. Founded Agora. Key contributions:
- **5-Part Sales Letter** (envelope/headline/lead/argument/close)
- **80% of the sale is the lead**
- **6 Lead Types** (Offer / Promise / Problem-Solution / Secret / Proclamation / Story)
- **Rule of One (5 elements)** (idea / emotion / story / benefit / response)
- **Lead must be unified** — one idea, one emotion, one story

Covered via Alex's notes. Reference files: `ct_architecture_of_persuasion.md`, `ct_great_leads.md`

### John Caples — *Tested Advertising Methods* (1932; multiple editions)
The headline-testing classic. Key contributions:
- **Headline variance** — same ad, different headlines → 5-10× variance in returns
- **"They laughed when I sat down at the piano"** — the legendary curiosity-gap headline
- **Testing discipline** — keyed returns applied specifically to headlines

NOT yet read directly. Covered partly via consensus in other sources.

### Robert Collier — *The Robert Collier Letter Book* (1931)
The direct-mail bible. Halbert cites Collier repeatedly. Key contributions:
- **The Dollar Bill Letter** (famous 90%+ response direct-mail technique — attach a dollar bill to a donation letter)
- **Emotional appeals in direct mail**
- **Reason-why for deals**

NOT yet read directly. Highest priority for next read.

### Evaldo Albuquerque — *The 16-Word Sales Letter*
The modern VSL framework. Key contributions:
- **One Belief + 10 Questions** — scalable long-form VSL / sales-letter structure
- **The 10-question chain** — guides the reader from current belief to the One Belief

Covered via Alex's notes. Reference file: `ct_16_word_sales_letter.md`

## Cross-reference notes

- Hopkins → Halbert → Schwartz is a direct intellectual line. Read in that order for the full evolution.
- Masterson / Forde are building on Schwartz and Halbert — lead architecture is a refinement of Schwartz's awareness theory combined with Halbert's practical emphasis on the opening.
- Schwab is practical-companion to Hopkins — do both.
- Caples is laser-focused on headlines; use as a supplementary source.
- Collier predates Hopkins and heavily influenced Halbert; read after Halbert to spot the references.
- Evaldo Albuquerque synthesizes the modern era — useful AFTER the historical grounding, not before.

## What's NOT in the canon (but adjacent)

For comprehensive marketing-not-just-copy:
- David Ogilvy — *Ogilvy on Advertising*, *Confessions of an Advertising Man* (brand advertising, Madison Avenue tradition)
- Russell Brunson — *DotCom Secrets*, *Expert Secrets* (modern funnel playbook — more strategy than copy)
- Bob Bly — *The Copywriter's Handbook* (practical reference, especially for B2B)
- Joe Sugarman — *The Adweek Copywriting Handbook* (slippery-slope / transition mastery)
- Robert Cialdini — *Influence* (psychology underneath all direct response)
- Rory Sutherland — *Alchemy* (behavioral economics applied to marketing)

Some of these are pending in the user's reading queue.
