# Schwartz's Two Dimensions: Awareness + Sophistication

Eugene Schwartz's *Breakthrough Advertising* identified two independent dimensions every copywriter must diagnose before choosing a lead. Misjudge either and the reader bounces in 2 seconds.

## Dimension 1: Stages of Awareness

How much does the prospect already know about their problem, the existence of solutions, your specific solution, and your specific product?

### Stage 1 — MOST Aware
- Knows your product by name. Already wants it. Needs only a reason to buy *now*.
- **Lead type**: OFFER — just state the deal. "40% off Black Friday — ends midnight."
- **Mechanics**: price, gimmicks, deadlines, reason-why for the deal.
- **Don't**: waste their time explaining the product. They know.
- **Schwartz's brutal note**: offer copywriters are "nothing more than the merchandise manager's phrase maker — should receive the lowest possible scale of pay."

### Stage 2 — PRODUCT Aware
- Knows your product exists but hasn't decided to buy. Aware of competitors too.
- **Lead type**: PROMISE — strengthen the desire, demonstrate, prove.
- **Mechanics** (Schwartz's 7 techniques for Product-Aware):
  1. Strengthen the desire for the product
  2. Demonstrate the product meeting needs
  3. Highlight situations where the product is most useful
  4. Provide NEW proof / details / evidence
  5. Announce a new mechanism or improvement
  6. Reveal updates that fix prior limitations
  7. Redefine the product to stand out from competitors

### Stage 3 — SOLUTION Aware
- Aware a solution category exists but doesn't know your specific product. E.g., "I know there are supplements for joint pain, but I don't know which one."
- **Lead type**: PROMISE or PROBLEM-SOLUTION — call out the desire/problem, prove the solution category delivers, show why YOUR version is the one.
- **Mechanics**: call out desire → prove solution category works → introduce your product as the best form of it.
- **Example** (P90X): "transform your body in 90 days with P90X" — called out desire (transformation), delivered in a solution the reader understood (workout program), positioned the product specifically.

### Stage 4 — PROBLEM Aware
- Knows they have a problem; doesn't yet know solutions exist, or doesn't believe any will work.
- **Lead type**: PROBLEM-SOLUTION or SECRET — meet them in the pain first. Build trust. THEN introduce the solution.
- **Mechanics**: empathize with the pain first (show you *feel it*). Never open with the product. Reveal that a solution exists (often as a "secret" or hidden knowledge).
- **Common error**: over-explaining the problem. Don't teach — show you understand, then offer hope.

### Stage 5 — UNAWARE
- Doesn't know they have a problem, or doesn't admit it. The hardest state.
- **Lead type**: PROCLAMATION or STORY — must hook with something UNRELATED to the product, then gradually build awareness across the copy.
- **Mechanics**: NEVER mention price, NEVER name the product early, NEVER state benefits the prospect doesn't know they want.
- Open with: a bold proclamation, a shocking stat, a future prediction, a story the prospect finds relatable.
- This is where big financial direct response and natural health ads live. Hardest to write; highest-paid.

### Quick diagnostic
- Do they know the problem/desire consciously? → NO = Unaware. YES → continue.
- Do they know a solution category exists? → NO = Problem-aware. YES → continue.
- Do they know your product by name or similar named products? → NO = Solution-aware. YES → continue.
- Do they know YOUR product and price and need only a reason to buy now? → YES = Most-aware. NO = Product-aware.

### 99% rule
You'll almost never need to write for truly Unaware audiences. Most copyriters go whole careers without it. But understanding the spectrum is essential for calibrating leads for Problem-aware and Solution-aware (where most real work happens).

## Dimension 2: Stages of Market Sophistication

How many competing products / ads has the reader ALREADY seen making similar claims? Independent of awareness — a prospect can be Most-aware but in an unsophisticated market (few ads seen) or Solution-aware but in a hyper-sophisticated one (saturated market).

### Stage 1 — First to market
- The reader has never seen anything like this before.
- **Approach**: simple, direct claim. "Make money from home." "Lose weight fast." The unvarnished promise wins.
- **Rare today** outside of truly new categories (new tech, new mechanisms, new niches).

### Stage 2 — Competitors enter with same claim
- Several competitors making the same basic claim. The reader has seen "lose weight fast" from 5 brands.
- **Approach**: make the claim BIGGER or more specific. "Lose 20 pounds in 30 days without exercise."
- **Rule**: enlarge OR specify. Both work until the market saturates them.

### Stage 3 — Claims become unbelievable; time for a NEW MECHANISM
- Claims have been enlarged to the point of absurdity. "Make $1 million in 5 minutes online" — nobody believes.
- **Approach**: introduce a NEW MECHANISM — a different process, method, system, technology, formula — that explains HOW the old promise is now achievable.
- "Lose weight with this gut bacterium" beats "lose weight fast" at this stage.
- **This is where most profitable direct-response copy lives** in 2025 — financial, health, info-product niches are almost all at stage 3.

### Stage 4 — Mechanism gets copied
- The new mechanism is now claimed by multiple competitors. "Gut bacterium" is now in 20 supplement ads.
- **Approach**: enlarge or specify the MECHANISM. "The Japanese gut bacterium discovered in 1987 monks."
- Then the mechanism gets exhausted too.

### Stage 5 — Mechanisms exhausted; shift to IDENTIFICATION
- No new claim or mechanism will grab attention. The market has seen everything.
- **Approach**: pivot from "here's what the product does" to "this product is for people LIKE YOU." Lean on identification — show the reader in their aspired or actual identity.
- "The briefcase the serious entrepreneur carries." "The toothpaste real mothers choose."
- **This is where Nike / Apple / high-end brand advertising lives** — the mass-market side of copywriting. Less common in direct response but still happens.

### Quick diagnostic
- How many ads in this space has the prospect probably seen? 0-2 → Stage 1. 3-10 → Stage 2. 10+ with absurd claims → Stage 3. Mechanism already claimed by 3+ competitors → Stage 4. Mechanisms stale → Stage 5.
- What's working in the market right now? Study competitor ads. If they've all enlarged claims to cartoonish levels → you're at stage 3. Time for a new mechanism.

## How awareness + sophistication interact

These are ORTHOGONAL. A prospect can be any combination:

| | Sophistication Low | Sophistication High |
|---|---|---|
| **Unaware** | Rare — a new problem nobody knows about | Very rare — would require educating into existing saturated market |
| **Problem-aware** | "People finally realizing back pain isn't normal" — story/proclamation lead with simple solution | Most direct-response health niches — need story/secret lead + new mechanism |
| **Solution-aware** | Emerging category — plain promise works | Saturated category (diet, joint supplements) — need new mechanism or identification |
| **Product-aware** | Niche product in low-competition space — demonstrate works | Mature product in competitive market — USP + new proof or positioning |
| **Most-aware** | Direct offer always works | Offer + genuine urgency/reason-why |

## Worked examples

**Example A — Weight loss ad to 45-year-old mom who has tried 5 diets**
- Awareness: Solution-aware (she knows supplements exist) OR Product-aware (she's seen specific brands)
- Sophistication: Stage 4-5 (she's seen every possible claim)
- **Lead choice**: Secret lead with new mechanism, leaning on identification. "The Japanese ceremony-tea ritual tired moms are using to melt belly fat" (new mechanism + identification pivot).

**Example B — Software tool for beginning podcasters**
- Awareness: Problem-aware (they know podcasting is hard) or Solution-aware (they know tools exist)
- Sophistication: Stage 2 (competitors emerging but not yet saturated)
- **Lead choice**: Promise lead with specific benefit. "Start your podcast in one afternoon — no editing required."

**Example C — Luxury watch to affluent men**
- Awareness: Most-aware (they know the brand)
- Sophistication: Stage 5 (luxury watch market is fully saturated)
- **Lead choice**: Pure identification. "The watch worn by men who build empires, not followers."

## Choosing the lead type by awareness

See `lead-types.md` for the six Masterson/Forde lead types. Pairing:
- Most aware → OFFER lead
- Product aware → PROMISE lead
- Solution aware → PROMISE or PROBLEM-SOLUTION lead
- Problem aware → PROBLEM-SOLUTION or SECRET lead
- Unaware → PROCLAMATION or STORY lead
