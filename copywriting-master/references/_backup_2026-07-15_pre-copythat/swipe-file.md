## Headlines / hooks (ranked by reusable pattern)

**Top patterns (recur across many creators — use these first):**

**Conversion-copy craft patterns (Joanna Wiebe / Copyhackers — `[1 src]` each, but high-authority):**
- **Concrete visceral image beats the flat fact.** "Are you sleeping in a Petri dish?" out-pulled "Your pillow is filled with bacteria" — make the reader *see and feel* it, don't state it. (Joanna Wiebe)
- **Believable claim beats bold guarantee (don't trip the skeptic).** "Over the course of a year, nine in 10 of our clients more than double their conversion rates" out-pulls "Double your conversion rate or your money back" — a too-good number triggers "wait, *double? really?*" disbelief, not desire. The time frame + "9 in 10" + "more than double" disarms skepticism; tune the claim to the *edge* of believable, not past it. (Joanna Wiebe) `[corroborates Risk-Reversal believability caveat]`
- **Rewrite a yes/no headline into a curiosity hook (never risk an early "no").** "Is this cosmetic breakthrough better than Botox?" → "What if this cosmetic breakthrough *ends* Botox?" / "Who wants to be wrinkle-free without shooting Botox into their face?" A question they could answer "no" to loses them; an open curiosity loop keeps them. (Joanna Wiebe)
- **Classic Caples open-loop (emotional tension = "what's going on here?").** "They laughed when I sat down at the piano, but when I started to play…" — the open loop runs on the reader's need to resolve the tension. (Joanna Wiebe, via John Caples)
- **⚠️ Negativity wins VIEWS, not SALES — mirror the reader's self-image, never threaten it.** "Most marketers get this completely wrong" gets clicks but *kills conversion* (the reader feels attacked → "wait, are they saying I'm getting it wrong?" → bounce); "For marketers who already know the basics" converts (the reader feels *seen*). Before publishing a contrarian/attack hook, ask "am I reflecting their self-image or threatening it?" Pattern-interrupt your *category*, not your *reader*. (Joanna Wiebe) `[important caveat to the contrarian-hook pattern above]`
- **"If it embarrasses your mother, you're doing it right" — permission to write with edge.** First-person headlines work; *stories* sell. Don't sand the personality out of a hook to make it "safe." (Joanna Wiebe)

## Subject lines

**Cold-email subject lines (corroborated rules + Gong 85M-email data):** 1–5 words, lowercase, read like an *internal email from a peer* — vague + emotional or loss-framed so they think "*what?*". No buzzwords, no numbers, no questions, no "AI". One-word winners that pulled 90%+ opens: **"welders," "dropouts," "delays," "missing time."** Lowercase lifts opens ~10% (looks like someone they know). `[5 src]` (Matt Macnamara, Patrick Dang, Daniel Fazio, Trent Dressel, Josh Braun, 30MPC)

First line must stay true to the subject — no bait-and-switch.
- **Write the subject like ad-copy a peer would forward:** "Ronald's beef isn't with us" (McDonald's vegan-patty pitch) · "Austin envy — SF's answer just landed" · "did you see the McDonald's news?" (30MPC)

- **Inversion as a copy method (Berman, via Munger):** before writing, list every way to do the email WRONG — vague, all-about-you, no personalization, weak subject, no CTA, no follow-up — then do the exact opposite. The fastest route to a clean draft is cataloguing the failure modes first. `[a reusable editing/ideation move]`
- **The 3-question QA pass on any cold email (Berman):** (1) *Am I lying?* (no unverifiable claims), (2) *Is my data clean?* (no stray spaces, typos, ALLCAPS — and never an undecodable subject acronym like "CM question"; "Hi from Alex" reads human), (3) *Does it make sense read aloud by a stranger?* Run every email through this before send. `[editing discipline; pairs with don't-lie below]`
- **3 follow-up angles that aren't "just bumping this" (Berman):** **personal-connection** (their sports team / city), **excitement** ("*talked to the team — we're pumped*"), **news-item** (call out their blog post / funding / anniversary). Give the follow-up a *reason to exist* beyond your own impatience. `[complements the escalating post-call sequence above]`
- **Sell the thing using the thing / congruence (Berman):** a cold-email agency that doesn't run cold email doesn't believe its own offer — and buyers Google you before replying, so website + LinkedIn + signature + offer must all say the same thing. Eat your own dog food, then make the proof visible. `[RIOA — sell cold-outreach by running it]`
- **Five pitches, one product — lead with their pain, not your features (Berman):** the same product sells five ways (lose 30 lbs / scary cholesterol / first marathon); the one-pitch rep closes ~20%, the rep who finds the buyer's specific pain *first* closes all five. Find the leverage before you open your mouth. `[the teaching version of Sell-the-result + Problem-first discovery]`
- **The statistical-significance trap in A/B copy tests (Berman):** a subject line hit 100% open on 10 sends, then 19% on 168 — never crown a winner under ~50 sends per variant. "Whatever you track gets improved," but only if the sample is real. `[testing discipline; tempers the "90% open" claims]`
- **Inbound re-qualifier that stays direct (Berman):** "*Saw you downloaded [whitepaper] — great one. Are you thinking about switching to [tool]? A 10-minute call will save your team hours of research.*" Turn a content download into a dated, low-friction ask framed as *saving them time*. `[reusable for RIOA's lead-magnet follow-up]`

## Cold-call openers (verbatim, ranked)
- **Tailored Permission Opener — the single most-corroborated cold-call structure (Gong-best, 300M calls: 11.18% vs 2.15% for "did I catch you at a bad time"):** lead with something about THEM → own it's a cold call → ask permission. "[Name], I just finished reading the press release about your new office in Tacoma. I'll be honest — this is a cold call, but a well-researched one. Can I get 30 seconds on why I called, then you can totally hang up on me?" *Context-first is what breaks the telemarketer stigma.* Reason-I'm-calling variant: "The reason I'm calling you *specifically* is [you lead X / just promoted]…" `[9 src]` (Trent Dressel — dominant, Matt Macnamara, Patrick Dang, Rory Vaden, Ian Koniak, Josh Braun, 30MPC, Giulio Segantini, Armand Farrokh)
- **"Heard the name tossed around" opener (Gong 11.24%):** lead with a shared thread (investor / competitor / geography) *before* your name, dipped low tone + a shrug — "We work with a few other Andreessen portfolio companies — it's Armand from Pave, have you heard our name tossed around?" Goal: sound like they *should* know you, in 15 seconds. `[2 src]` (30MPC, Josh Braun)
- **Good-news/bad-news + permission:** "Good news — this is *not* about your car warranty expiring. Bad news — this is still indeed a cold call. Mind if I take 22 seconds, then you can hang up on me?" (30MPC)
- **Pattern-interrupt openers (Giulio):** "If I told you this is a sales call, would you smash your phone against the wall?" · "Am I going to ruin your Wednesday if I tell you this is a sales call?" (Giulio Segantini via 30MPC)
- **Familiar-tone open (Jeremy Miner):** never "Is this John?" (guard goes up) — "Nick, it's Jeremy… Jeremy Miner with XYZ. I was wondering if you could help me out for a moment?" Repeat the name twice, slight stutter/paper-shuffle; their brain loops on "how do I know this person?" (Jeremy Miner via 30MPC)
- **Permission / self-outing variant:** "You're probably going to hate me for this — this is actually a cold call. Want to hang up, or can I steal 30 seconds?" Delivered slow, warm, light laugh. (Matt Macnamara, Patrick Dang, Josh Braun)
- **Humility re-open for burned/dark accounts (Josh Braun):** "You have absolutely no idea who I am — my name is Josh with DocuSign and I'm just calling to apologize. Do you have a couple minutes?"
- **"How have you been?" beats "How are you?"** — manufactures false familiarity; Gong rated it a top opener. `[2 src]` (Matt Macnamara, Trent Dressel)
- **Disarm-the-stranger / name-the-cliché first (Josh Braun):** "I got your name from a B2B list — congrats on being list-worthy." · "We've never met, but I noticed something interesting about your post — hoping I could ask a couple quick questions."
- **Permission opener with relevance up front (verbatim):** "Hey Dave, it's Jason. I was calling about the recent plant you opened in Dallas… you got a minute for me to share the reason for my call?" Skip "this is X from [company]" (instant wall) — lead with the trigger. `[reinforces the Tailored Permission Opener]` (30MPC)
- **Offer-as-question opener (Jordan Platten):** after a quick permission line ("I know you're busy so I'll be quick"), open with the offer phrased as an ego-feeding question — "Could you handle another 15 appointments for your clinic right now?" "Could you handle new clients?" beats "Are you taking on clients?" (feeds the ego, gets a truthful answer, and banks a preliminary "yes"). Never pitch on the call — the only goal is "enough desire for a meeting"; give a vague explanation and say "paid ads," not "Facebook ads."
- **Voicemail — the double-tap formula:** lead with *context, not your name*, so it survives the auto-delete — "Hey Nick, we work with a few other Skadden partners in the LA office." Structure = relevance + social proof + a low-lift CTA pointing to the email: "…I emailed you, subject line 'estimates.' No need to call me back — mind replying if it's even moderately interesting?" Leave exactly two (VM1 ≈15s context only, VM2 ≈30s context + social proof). `[1 src]` (30MPC)
- **Reason-for-call built from a case-study result (Chris Orlob / John Barrows):** "Hey John, thanks for taking my call — real quick, the reason for my call is we're showing VPs of sales like you how to drive [sector result] with our solution; I just wanted to see if that's worth a deeper-dive conversation." **"The reason for my call is ___" is the most timeless opener** — humans crave reasons (Cialdini's "because"); if you can't finish that sentence, don't dial. Beats trendy, short-shelf-life openers. `[reinforces the Tailored Permission Opener]` (Chris Orlob, John Barrows)
- **Internal-referral cold open (the line execs actually open):** "Someone inside your company thought you might be interested in this." Implied warm intro beats any clever cold line. (Jonathan Lister via Barrows)
- **Hypothesis opener (Karen Kelly via Barrows):** "We're working with a lot of sales execs like you, and what we're seeing right now is X, Y, Z. My hunch is you're dealing with X — am I on the right track?" Give them a point of view to react to, not a blank question.
- **Story-based pain opener (replaces "what keeps you up at night?"):** list 3 specific persona pains, then "I don't suppose any of those sound familiar?" — they insert *themselves* into the story instead of being interrogated. (Dave Mattson/Sandler via Barrows)
- **"Ask for help" cold open (disarm via the helping reflex):** "I'm actually a little lost here — do you mind if I take a second to tell you why I'm calling?" Asking a stranger for help triggers a hardwired urge to help; lowers the guard a pitch raises. Gatekeeper variant: a plain **"Can you help me?"** — EAs took the role *because* they like helping, so a real, relevant reason beats every trick. `[2 src]` (Patrick Dang — opener; John Barrows — gatekeeper)
- **AIDA framing for the open (Barrows):** on a cold call you're **not selling the product, you're selling TIME** — the first ~5 seconds dictate the rest, so the open's only job is to buy the next 30 seconds (Attention → Interest), never to pitch. (John Barrows)
- **No-oriented opener (Chris Voss via Barrows) — use with care:** "Is now a bad time?" / "Have I caught you in the middle of something?" A "no" makes people feel protected and in control, which dials them in — Barrows reports the "middle of something" variant gets the **second meeting ~80% of the time** (answer is almost always "No, but what's this about?" = full attention). ⚠️ *Caveat: Gong's data rates the near-identical "Did I catch you at a bad time?" the single WORST opener (2.15%) — the field conflicts on this exact line. The permission / reason-for-call opener out-performs both, so prefer it for a true cold open; reserve the no-oriented version for re-engaging an already-resistant prospect.*
- **Anti-patterns (worst openers, data-backed):** "How's your day going?" (Gong 7.6%) · "Did I catch you at a bad time?" (2.15% — "they'll say of course you did"). Both instantly flag *telemarketer*. `[4 src]` (Matt Macnamara, Patrick Dang, Trent Dressel, 30MPC)
- **Cold Calling 2.0 — never dial truly cold (Ravi Abuvala):** only call people a scalable first touch (cold email, YouTube, a lead magnet) has already warmed; "open-then-dial" — phone within ~5 minutes of an email open. One-tenth the dials, far higher pickup. *(Ravi's literal opener is "did I catch you at a bad time?" — carries the standing CONFLICT caveat above; prefer the tailored permission opener for the cold open itself.)*

## Sales page / VSL leads
- **Lead with the quantified result, never the mechanism:** "get 20 to 60 extra sales calls each month" beats "we do cold email." (Daniel Fazio)
- **Sell the transformation, not the pieces (Myron Golden):** "Nobody ever buys your product — they buy your *offer*: your description of the transformation they get when they say yes." Offer definition to swipe: "a valuable opportunity you give somebody the chance to pay for."

- **Sell-first-build-later offer line (Patrick Dang):** "A service is really just a promise — sell the thing first, then build what you promised." Validate the offer sells *before* you build it or spend on brand ("strategy without execution is just dreaming" — Jeremy Miller).

- **Price-then-silence:** state the price plainly, then "I'm going to shut up" — whoever talks first loses. `[3 src]` (Matt Macnamara, Jose Rosado, Patrick Dang)
- **Strategic silence after the offer (Joanna Wiebe):** state the price and *hold the silence* — "7 seconds feels like an eternity," extended silence after an offer reportedly raises acceptance ~35% and signals confidence; pause ~3 sec before answering any hard question too. `[corroborates price-then-silence]`
- **Confirm-back close (Joanna Wiebe):** "You said you have problem A. Feature X addresses problem A — which is good for you, because solving A was one of the things you confirmed you wanted." Closes the loop using *their own* stated want, so the yes feels self-evident.
- **Live diagnostic-model close (Joanna Wiebe):** on the call, draw your framework live (e.g. a triangle — List / Offer / Copy) and have *them* mark each component red/yellow/green. They self-diagnose, see exactly what's broken, and start thinking "I hope I can afford this person" — self-identified problems build the most trust and pre-sell the price.
- **Cost-of-NO close (Myron Golden):** "I don't emphasize how much it costs if they say yes — I emphasize how much it costs if they say *no*, and I make the cost of no higher than the cost of yes."
- **Cost-contrast close (anchor price against the quantified problem):** after stacking a $1.7M problem — "Do you want to pay now or later? My solution's only 30K." The price reads as trivial next to the number you just built. `[1 src]` (30MPC)
- **Time-comparison / break-it-to-pennies (Dan Kennedy / Bornancin):** "$49/mo → $1.61/day → 7 cents/hour" · "$100/license ÷ 30 days = $3/day." Compare to "stuff they'd buy anyway." `[2 src]` (Frank Kern, Brandon Bornancin)
- **Low-CTA / no-CTA (Josh Braun):** "If someone wants to respond, they know how — you don't tell them 'respond to my email.'"
- **CTA close (high-ticket):** "Congratulations on making it this far — now you just gotta bet on yourself and click the buy button." (Ian Koniak)
- **Free lead-magnet CTA (compile-and-give-away):** take everything a guest/expert said across all their appearances, "took all the best stuff and put it in one place… it's dense, it's a document, it's available now, it's totally free. Link in the description." Stack the value-density, then a zero-friction ask. (Scott Galloway via MFM)
- **Value-anchoring close question (Liam Ottley):** "How much would you have paid for this?" — let the buyer name the value first, then anchor ROI on hours saved across the team (10 people × 4 focus hrs/day = 2,000+ hrs/mo) so a five-figure price lands as obvious.
- **Referral CTA at peak happiness (a16z / Waseem Daher):** a plain personal email — "if you're happy, we'd love an intro to anyone in your network who'd benefit." Later formalize it with a referral leaderboard (in customer/investor updates) to gamify intros.
- **Repeatable refrain close:** "That's what happens when you stop winging it and start executing with intention." A one-line drumbeat you can repeat after each proof point. (John Barrows)
- **Inbound-spam qualification gate (reverse-CTA):** "Send me a one-minute voice note or Loom on exactly why you can help my company, and I'll take your demo." ~9/10 never reply; the 10% who do convert — friction *is* the filter. (John Barrows)
- **Anti-pattern — don't bribe for time:** "$5 Starbucks card for 30 minutes" / "a penny for your thoughts" reads as desperate and *insulting* — "you're telling me my hourly rate is 20 bucks an hour." Lead with a real value-add (a relevant insight/audit), never a gift card. `[avoid]` (John Barrows, Kelly Cheng)
- **CTA-clarity anti-pattern — never end vague:** kill "drop me a line," "let's grab a coffee," "look me up when you're in LA" — they hand the reader nothing concrete to do. Name the exact next step: "let me know what your calendar looks like" / "schedule a time on my calendar." One unmistakable action per ask — "people don't like to think." (Patrick Dang)
- **CTA mechanics (Joanna Wiebe):** in a long email, repeat the CTA **mid and end**; use a **button, not a text link**; one contrasting color kept consistent across every button; verb-first and short — "Click / Get / Join / Download." Reduce the decision to one obvious click.
- **Finish EVERYTHING with a close (Mike Killen):** end questions with a close, objections with a close, the pitch with a close — *never* end on "does anyone have any questions?" (open-ended invites the stall). Pressure isn't a hard sell; it tells a first-time buyer the literal next step — "sign here, send the 50% deposit, we get started today" (concrete script: "it's a $3,500 deposit, then $2,500/mo for 11 months — we can start today, is that card payment?"). Spell out the next action because it may be their first time hiring a pro and they genuinely don't know what to do. `[corroborates "name the exact next step" — Patrick Dang]`
- **Decision-maker rebook + know when to STOP selling (Mike Killen):** if they're not the sole decision-maker, stop and rebook with everyone present — "it'd be unfair to expect you to relay a pitch I've practised for years." And once the buyer says "send the proposal," *stop selling* — going back to selling re-opens doubt; switch to closing (deposit + signature).
- **Budget temp-check before you demo (Greg Hickman — disqualify on price early):** "Say we hop on and everything's a fit — to work together, our minimum engagement is X. Assuming you love what you see, is that something that works for you, or is that an issue?" Filters tire-kickers before you spend a demo; pair with price-transparency in your pre-call content so only real-budget buyers book. `[corroborates the "we're not for everyone" / budget-floor disqualifier — Take-away, field-frameworks]`
- **Action-taker fee waiver (scarcity close, Jordan Platten):** "Our best clients are action-takers — decide on this call today and I'll waive the £1,000 upfront setup fee." A reward-for-deciding-now that flatters the buyer instead of discounting the work.
- **Assume-the-kickoff first-call close (Andrew Argue):** skip the invoice/proposal/engagement-letter — close by booking the start: "Great, let's set up a kickoff — Tuesday at 4? I'll send a calendar invite and a request list, and the first payment is $1,500/month — Visa, Mastercard, or Amex?" Treat payment as the *logical next step*, not a separate hard sell. `[distinct from Myron's payment-bypass — this assumes the kickoff, not just the card]`
- **Money/urgency tie-down close (Ravi Abuvala):** plant a dollar number on their problem early, then call it back to close — "You've done this six months on your own and it hasn't worked. We identified you're losing $50k/month. I'm asking for a fraction of that — how much longer are you going to keep losing $50k a month?"
- **Show-up bonus (Ravi Abuvala):** offer a bonus for *showing up* (not just booking) the call — lifted booking-to-show 50%→60%; lock the bonus inside the product, "only for those who show up."
- **Stacked guarantee close (Ravi Abuvala):** "We guarantee you'll get your result or we give you your money back AND write you a check for $10,000." Principle: "if you're not afraid of the guarantee, it's probably not strong enough."
- **Replace weak open-ended closers (Ravi Abuvala):** kill "Would you be interested in exploring this further?" — end on one concrete next step (a booking link, "leave your number").

### v22 — The Futur (Chris Do) + YAP
- **The fishing close (The Futur):** don't grab the net (price/packages) until the fish points its face at the boat — i.e. until *they* ask "how do we start? what does it cost?" — then close, and **don't go past the sale.**
- **"Permission to set the agenda" open (The Futur):** "May I have your permission to set the agenda for this call?" Earns the right to lead and redirect tangents without friction.
- **The 1–10 readiness gate + "why isn't it lower?" (The Futur):** after value lands, "on a scale of 1–10, how important is fixing this in the next 30–60 days?" then "**why isn't your score lower?**" — to defend the number they sell themselves on the buy. `[see Make-them-sell-themselves, field-frameworks]`
- **The 3 F's follow-up discipline (Ryan Serhant):** **Follow Up** (recent leads) · **Follow Through** (do exactly what you said, when you said) · **Follow Back** (past clients, dead deals, even the not-interested) — and "**Balls Up**": keep many deals in the air so when one drops you've got 16 others. (Cadence, not copy — but the antidote to "the fortune is in the followup.")

- **"A no is okay, but a maybe is just a waste of time" (Patrick Dang):** push for a clear yes-or-no; the enemy is the maybe. `[corroborates Vince Beese / Mike Weinberg — "no is the second-best answer"]`
- **Loaded-word reframe swap-table (Patrick Dang — "7 words to avoid"):** the words quietly trigger distrust; swap them every time. *problem → challenge* · *buy → invest / move forward / take advantage of* · *pitch → presentation / demonstration* · *contract → agreement* · *"any objections?" → "is there anything else you'd need to see before moving forward?"* · drop *"honestly / to be honest"* (implies the rest was a lie) and *"trust me"* (instant distrust) entirely. - **Price-negotiation cluster (Arman Farrokh / 30 Minutes to President's Club):** four verbatim moves for the discount fight —
  - **Mirror their surprise:** if the buyer acts shocked at the number, act surprised right back, then hand it back to them: "Whoa — that was way more than I thought too. We're never the cheapest, but we're never out of the realm either. Do you actually know what this kind of thing costs, or are you just asking for a discount?"
  - **Create a cost to every concession:** never give a discount free — "if they ask for a lower price, you ask for a longer term." Eventually they stop asking, because every ask costs them something.
  - **"Negotiate like a grandma":** once they've won a *fair* discount, switch buckets — stop cutting price, trade tiny non-price items (billing terms, net-45) until they sense you're tapped out.
  - **The "fake walk" on an extreme ask:** "I think I might've screwed up — we're so far off it probably doesn't even make sense for me to take this back to my CFO. I just want to confirm you're really okay with that?" Forces *them* to step toward *you*.

- **"Aren't you just selling to us?" → admit it (Liam Ottley):** "I'm not going to pretend I'm not trying to make money — of course I am, as are you," then show the incentive alignment. `[corroborates Hormozi's selfish-honesty disarm]`

- **"Be trusted, not liked" reframe (Joanna Wiebe):** stop "agreeing your way to a yes" — trust comes from understanding the problem *better than the person who has it*; lead the conversation like the expert instead of people-pleasing toward the close.

- **"We're bringing it in-house" → it's an upsell, not a loss (Greg Hickman):** "Sometimes that happens *because* you did the job so well it now makes sense to own it — and if you did it right, they'll PAY you to help them do it well in-house." Reframe client graduation as the trigger for a documentation/enablement/advisory offer, not a churn event. (RIOA-relevant: agency end-market.)
- **"You're throwing out a price before I've seen everything" → mutual time-respect (Greg Hickman):** "I value my time more than I value a bad-fit client.
