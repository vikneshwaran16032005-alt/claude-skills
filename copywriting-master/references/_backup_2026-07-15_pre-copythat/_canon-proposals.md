### 1. [OPEN] Add a **Risk-Reversal Ladder** entry to `frameworks-table.md` (+ a note in `product-analysis.md`)
**Corroboration: 20 creators** (↑ from 19) — Daniel Fazio, Patrick Dang, Joel Kaplan, Frank Kern, Rory Vaden, Stephen Pope, Jose Rosado, Matt Macnamara, Myron Golden, Trent Dressel, Ian Koniak, Grant Cardone, Josh Braun, 30MPC, Neville Medhora, Suli Ali, Jordan Platten, **Iman Gadzhi**, **Ravi Abuvala**, **Brandon Bornancin**. *Still the most-corroborated pattern in the entire corpus.* New flavor this cycle (Brandon Bornancin, genuinely-new copy-core channel): **lead WITH the guarantee and make it emotional** — position on it ("$1M–$5M in sales in 12 months; if our clients don't sell at least $1M a year, we're pissed off") so the conviction sells, and frame inaction as the risk-free-but-worthless option in a two-choice close ("do nothing, which is 100% risk-free… or invest this tiny amount for all this value"). New flavor this cycle (Ravi Abuvala): **lead the cold outreach WITH the guarantee** — on cold traffic the risk-reversal *is* the hook ("you only pay if you have show-up appointments," "you only pay when you close a deal," "we guarantee 30 bookings / 5 clients a month"), fused with offer-specificity ("we've helped 378 businesses just like you get the loan in 30 days, under 7%, no collateral"). Prior flavor (Iman Gadzhi): **make the buyer's risk as close to zero as humanly possible** — with high-margin products, run **rev-share** ("you only spend money once you make money — zero risk"; hand the buyer 20–50% and still keep most), and "keep beginner risk as little as humanly possible so you can keep spinning the slot machine." Prior flavor: **pay-on-results stated up FRONT as the door-opener** (not just a close) — "I'm so confident this'll work that if I don't get you clients, you don't pay a penny" — the guarantee *is* the cold-call/first-meeting hook (Jordan Platten); and **performance-aligned offers carry it natively** — "$2k–$5k upfront + commission, guaranteeing five figures within the first 7 days," or commission-only when proof is thin (Liam Ottley). New field flavor to fold in: the **"10× better than money-back" + proactive refund reminder** (Myron Golden), **"if I can't save you ≥20%, I won't even propose"** (Koniak), **guarantee-only-what-you-control** (Koniak/Vaden), the **micro risk-reversal** "send me your cold email, I'll send a free revision — no cost, no ask; if you like it, let's talk" (Josh Braun), **baking the guarantee into the headline** ("…in 3 days or I'll refund your money" — Neville Medhora), and **radical transparency as risk-reversal** — hand the buyer a written list of every reason *not* to buy / every skeleton up front; they'll find them in diligence anyway, so disclosing first builds trust (Suli Ali). New this cycle (30MPC, already counted): the **conservative-ROI / confidence risk-reversal** — "if you can't find one deal, I don't want you to buy this, because that means you don't know what you're doing" (over-built ROI cases read as BS; a conservative, walk-away-confident guarantee converts harder). **Believability caveat to fold in (Joanna Wiebe, single-source, no count change):** a guarantee can be *too good* and backfire — "Double your conversion rate or your money back" can trigger "wait, *double? really?*" skepticism, where a softer, more believable claim ("over a year, 9 in 10 of our clients more than double their conversion rates") sometimes out-pulls the bold guarantee. Tune the promise to the *edge* of believable, not past it. New flavor this cycle (Frank Kern via Mike Killen, already counted — no count change): the **keep-it-anyway guarantee** — "you'll love this or I return your $5.60 *and* you keep the book and training" (no date, nothing to send back) — removes even the friction of *claiming* the refund, the lowest-resistance form of the ladder.
The single most-corroborated *offer-construction* pattern in the corpus, and the canon has no dedicated risk-reversal framework (closest is Principle 16, Reason Why). Proposed one-liner: **"Risk-Reversal Ladder — weak→strong: money-back-if-no-result → result/performance guarantee → pay-only-on-results. Buyers fear losing money more than they desire the win, so decrease risk rather than inflate the promise; with no case studies, a risk reversal is mandatory."** Field rule to capture: *guarantee only what you control* (Rory Vaden, Patrick Dang) and *make the guarantee a trade* — "what do you want guaranteed? In order for me to guarantee that, I want you to do X. Deal?" (Daniel Fazio).

### 7. [OPEN] Add a **Cost-of-Inaction / Loss-Aversion** entry to `frameworks-table.md` (+ a note in `awareness-sophistication.md`)
**Corroboration: 12 creators** (↑ from 11) — Ian Koniak, Trent Dressel, Myron Golden, Josh Braun, Mike Fiascone, Chet Holmes, 30MPC, Jeremy Miner, Jake Stahl, Doug Landis, **Greg Hickman**, **Jordan Platten** (the **"quick-fire maths of losses"** — quantify inaction live, "300 leads/month in 2 hrs vs a 10-min speed-to-lead doubles conversions × LTV × 3 years… can you afford NOT to act today?" + the AI-anxiety variant "if your competitors are using AI right now, how much business are you already losing?"; cold-open scale version from Liam Ottley — "a missed call at 4 p.m. Sunday could mean a $2M buyer walks away… $136B/year lost in missed calls"). New flavor this cycle (Doug Landis): the **status quo IS the competitor** — "**>60% of well-qualified opportunities end in 'do nothing'**" and "nobody got fired for *not* deciding — people get fired for making *bad* decisions," so flip it by asking "what does a *bad* decision look like for you here?"; plus the cleanest hard stat to cite — "**70% of B2B decisions are made to avoid loss, not gain**" (Chris Voss via Barrows). New flavor this cycle (Greg Hickman): the **consultative stretch across time AND life** — "if we don't solve this in 3, 6, 12 months, what do those areas of your *life* look like? what's the consequence of not solving it now?" — surface the impact inside *and* outside the business before making the offer. Prior flavor: manufacture an *"artificial compelling event"* from cost-of-inaction (30MPC); the two drivers of change = *present pain + fear of future pain* — "without both, they feel no need to change and you get tons of objections" (Jeremy Miner); buyers are *"~2× more likely to buy to avoid a loss than to acquire a gain"* (30MPC) — and Jake Stahl puts the multiple at *"~3–4× more driven to avoid a loss,"* so **the field range is now ~2–4×**; the cleanest action line: *"if you don't do this today, what does it look like in 3 months?"* (Jake Stahl); don't pitch the upside — *show what staying the same costs them every single day* (the golden-handcuffs risk-reversal — Chris Voss via Barrows); plus a copy-ready **4-layer pain-quantification stack** (time spent → opportunity cost of that time → cost of errors → downstream time/attrition) that turns a vague problem into 7 figures, and the **"compared to what?"** reframe (price vs. the quantified cost of the problem, never vs. a discount).
The canon's Pain→Dream→Bridge captures "no pain, no sale," but the field converges on a *sharper, distinct* mechanism the canon doesn't name explicitly: buyers move **~2× harder to avoid a loss than to chase an equal gain**, so the strongest lever is to **quantify the cost of doing nothing**. Field language to capture: **"make the cost of NO higher than the cost of yes"** (Myron Golden), **"every month you wait costs ~$100k" / "they fear messing up more than missing out"** (Koniak), **"pain of change vs. pain of the same — if the pain of staying is greater, you have a deal"** (Fiascone/Myron), **"people act faster to fix a problem than to gain a benefit"** (Chet Holmes). Proposed one-liner: *"Cost-of-Inaction — quantify in dollars/time what staying the same costs per month; loss aversion (~2–4×) makes the cost of NO the strongest motivator. The real competitor is 'no decision' / the status quo."* High confidence.

### 9. [OPEN] Add a **"When you're explaining, you're losing" — Disarm-before-Logic** objection principle (`editing.md` / a new objection note in `frameworks-table.md`)
**Corroboration: 10 creators** (↑ from 9) — Josh Braun, 30MPC (Armand/Nick), Giulio Segantini, Jeremy Miner, Jeb Blount, John Barrows, Colleen Stanley, Jordan Platten, **Iman Gadzhi** (Chris Voss = source). *Clears the 5+ bar.* New flavor this cycle (Iman Gadzhi): **acknowledge, never block — "loop around" the objection** ("you won't win by telling the other guy he's wrong; acknowledge, add more info, and he's cool"), and treat most early objections as a **reflexive shit-test**, not a real no — "the same reflex as brushing off a stranger on the street before he could offer you a million bucks; hold your character and they settle." Prior flavor (Jordan Platten): **LAER** (Listen → Acknowledge → Explore → Respond) + the framing **"an objection is not a rejection, it's a request for more information,"** defuse by repeating part of what they said back as a question, and the **feel/felt/found reframe** for the burned-before objection; build an objection "encyclopedia" of every objection + its scripted answer. Prior flavor (Colleen Stanley): **empathy-then-assertiveness / preemptive strike** — say the feeling first ("you probably think we're too expensive — fair?") and "the emotional temperature drops," then make the ask; better, *surface the objection yourself before they raise it* — "I'm guessing you think we're way too expensive — am I on the right path? → now it's not an objection, it's a conversation"; plus the sharpest articulation of the agree-first move, the **Mr. Miyagi 3-step** (Agree as pattern-break → Incentivize the truth with a carrot → Split the objection into options they pick from). Prior flavor (John Barrows/Tim Castle): **reframe the objection as data, not resistance** — it reveals what they care about; shift from emotion to strategy, then ask "what matters most right now?"; plus the **humbling disclaimer** ("I feel a little awkward asking, but what'd be the top reasons you wouldn't move forward, if any?") and the **off-the-record reset** to surface the real competitor.
The field is unanimous on a counter-intuitive move the canon doesn't name: **never logically rebut an objection — agree/validate it FIRST to drop the emotional guard, then re-engage.** "When you're explaining, you're losing" / "fighting an objection = telling the prospect they're wrong" (30MPC); **Defuse → Discover → Discuss**, "understand, don't overcome — prospects can smell your commission breath" (Josh Braun); the **Mr. Miyagi / ACE** sequence (agree → ask permission → ask a question that makes *them* change their mind — "the prospect is the best person to change the prospect's mind"); **The Ledge** = a pre-scripted reflex line ("Shoot, this one's on me") to buy a beat. **Copy-relevant:** in a sales page's FAQ / objection-handling, *validate the reader's resistance before answering it* rather than arguing with it. High confidence. Proposed one-liner: *"Disarm before logic — meet an objection (on a call or on the page) by first agreeing it's reasonable; arguing triggers reactance, agreement reopens the mind."*
