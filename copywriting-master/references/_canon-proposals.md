### 1. [OPEN] Add a **Risk-Reversal Ladder** entry to `frameworks-table.md` (+ a note in `product-analysis.md`)
**Corroboration: 16 creators** (↑ from 14) — Daniel Fazio, Patrick Dang, Joel Kaplan, Frank Kern, Rory Vaden, Stephen Pope, Jose Rosado, Matt Macnamara, Myron Golden, Trent Dressel, Ian Koniak, Grant Cardone, Josh Braun, 30MPC, **Neville Medhora, Suli Ali**. *Still the most-corroborated pattern in the entire corpus.* New field flavor to fold in: the **"10× better than money-back" + proactive refund reminder** (Myron Golden), **"if I can't save you ≥20%, I won't even propose"** (Koniak), **guarantee-only-what-you-control** (Koniak/Vaden), the **micro risk-reversal** "send me your cold email, I'll send a free revision — no cost, no ask; if you like it, let's talk" (Josh Braun), **baking the guarantee into the headline** ("…in 3 days or I'll refund your money" — Neville Medhora), and **radical transparency as risk-reversal** — hand the buyer a written list of every reason *not* to buy / every skeleton up front; they'll find them in diligence anyway, so disclosing first builds trust (Suli Ali). New this cycle (30MPC, already counted): the **conservative-ROI / confidence risk-reversal** — "if you can't find one deal, I don't want you to buy this, because that means you don't know what you're doing" (over-built ROI cases read as BS; a conservative, walk-away-confident guarantee converts harder).
The single most-corroborated *offer-construction* pattern in the corpus, and the canon has no dedicated risk-reversal framework (closest is Principle 16, Reason Why). Proposed one-liner: **"Risk-Reversal Ladder — weak→strong: money-back-if-no-result → result/performance guarantee → pay-only-on-results. Buyers fear losing money more than they desire the win, so decrease risk rather than inflate the promise; with no case studies, a risk reversal is mandatory."** Field rule to capture: *guarantee only what you control* (Rory Vaden, Patrick Dang) and *make the guarantee a trade* — "what do you want guaranteed? In order for me to guarantee that, I want you to do X. Deal?" (Daniel Fazio).

### 5. [OPEN] Consider a new **Anchor-High + Trade-Every-Concession** negotiation entry in `frameworks-table.md`
**Corroboration: 15 creators** (↑ from 14) — Patrick Dang, Jacob Cass, Joel Kaplan, Frank Kern, Cole Gordon, Matt Macnamara, Trent Dressel, Ian Koniak, Myron Golden, 30MPC, Todd Caponi, Belal Batrawy, Jesse Pujji, Firenzi, **Giuseppe Conti**.
Canon has Pro-Rata Range Pricing for setting *your* rate but no *negotiation* framework. Now has a crisp named mechanism worth quoting: **The Four Levers of Negotiation (Todd Caponi)** — every discount must be *paid for* with one of (1) volume, (2) timing of cash/prepay, (3) length of commitment, (4) timing of the deal; plus **popcorn pricing** (Belal Batrawy — price tiers non-linearly so the biggest is the best value-per-unit). New field flavor (Firenzi): **concede on terms, not value** — tie any discount to *signature speed* (15% if signed in 2 weeks, 10% in 4) so it drives velocity; and **anchor against the prize, not the competitor** — quantify their #1 initiative ($5M) first so a $50K gap feels trivial (30MPC). New this cycle (Giuseppe Conti): **solution-first, then subtract** — present the full right-fit solution, and to reach a lower price *pull scope out* ("we can get there, but it means taking this away") so concessions cost them something; **discounting is permanent** (discount once and you discount every future deal); **good/better/best three-option** + a recommended pick (buyers default to the middle); the **anchoring rule** — go first only when you know the market, else let them; and the "War Dogs" lesson that bidding first-and-low blind can leave 10× on the table. Proposed: **"Anchor above floor; never concede price for free — every discount buys volume, prepay, a longer term, or a sign-by date (the Four Levers), or it pulls scope out; make the discount hard to get."** Now backed by 15 creators — raise its priority.

### 7. [OPEN] Add a **Cost-of-Inaction / Loss-Aversion** entry to `frameworks-table.md` (+ a note in `awareness-sophistication.md`)
**Corroboration: 10 creators** (↑ from 9) — Ian Koniak, Trent Dressel, Myron Golden, Josh Braun, Mike Fiascone, Chet Holmes, 30MPC, Jeremy Miner, Jake Stahl, **Doug Landis**. New flavor this cycle (Doug Landis): the **status quo IS the competitor** — "**>60% of well-qualified opportunities end in 'do nothing'**" and "nobody got fired for *not* deciding — people get fired for making *bad* decisions," so flip it by asking "what does a *bad* decision look like for you here?"; plus the cleanest hard stat to cite — "**70% of B2B decisions are made to avoid loss, not gain**" (Chris Voss via Barrows). Prior flavor: manufacture an *"artificial compelling event"* from cost-of-inaction (30MPC); the two drivers of change = *present pain + fear of future pain* — "without both, they feel no need to change and you get tons of objections" (Jeremy Miner); buyers are *"~2× more likely to buy to avoid a loss than to acquire a gain"* (30MPC) — and Jake Stahl puts the multiple at *"~3–4× more driven to avoid a loss,"* so **the field range is now ~2–4×**; the cleanest action line: *"if you don't do this today, what does it look like in 3 months?"* (Jake Stahl); don't pitch the upside — *show what staying the same costs them every single day* (the golden-handcuffs risk-reversal — Chris Voss via Barrows); plus a copy-ready **4-layer pain-quantification stack** (time spent → opportunity cost of that time → cost of errors → downstream time/attrition) that turns a vague problem into 7 figures, and the **"compared to what?"** reframe (price vs. the quantified cost of the problem, never vs. a discount).
The canon's Pain→Dream→Bridge captures "no pain, no sale," but the field converges on a *sharper, distinct* mechanism the canon doesn't name explicitly: buyers move **~2× harder to avoid a loss than to chase an equal gain**, so the strongest lever is to **quantify the cost of doing nothing**. Field language to capture: **"make the cost of NO higher than the cost of yes"** (Myron Golden), **"every month you wait costs ~$100k" / "they fear messing up more than missing out"** (Koniak), **"pain of change vs. pain of the same — if the pain of staying is greater, you have a deal"** (Fiascone/Myron), **"people act faster to fix a problem than to gain a benefit"** (Chet Holmes). Proposed one-liner: *"Cost-of-Inaction — quantify in dollars/time what staying the same costs per month; loss aversion (~2–4×) makes the cost of NO the strongest motivator. The real competitor is 'no decision' / the status quo."* High confidence.

### 8. [OPEN] Consider a **Detachment / Anti-"Commission-Breath"** principle (reinforces Principle on neediness; note in `writing-mechanics.md` re: tone)
**Corroboration: 8 creators** (↑ from 7) — Myron Golden, Trent Dressel, Ian Koniak, Grant Cardone, Josh Braun, 30MPC, **Matt Millen** (+ Lou Shipley, Patrick Dang, Frank Kern prior). New verbatims this cycle: **"I'm not selling, I'm solving — if you're there to sell you'll never solve; if you're there to solve you'll never sell"** (Matt Millen); "stop pitching your solution, care more about what the client needs than your commission — *that's* when the commission goes through the roof" (Lou Shipley).
The field is near-unanimous that *neediness repels and detachment attracts* — and it shapes copy **tone**, not just live selling. **"Commission breath"** (you can smell desperation) is in fact **Josh Braun's signature term and recurs all over the corpus** — worth quoting directly. Other language: "lean out and they lean in" (Myron), "the person willing to walk away has the most leverage" (Dressel), "detach from the *get* — aim for the truth, not the meeting" (Josh Braun), "in the first 30 seconds you have *negative* trust, don't go assumptive" (30MPC). The *posture under* the take-away close (#4), but distinct: it governs the emotional register of the whole piece. Suggest a one-line principle: *write/sell from abundance and service, never desperation — the reader can feel "commission breath" in copy as surely as on a call.*

### 9. [OPEN] Add a **"When you're explaining, you're losing" — Disarm-before-Logic** objection principle (`editing.md` / a new objection note in `frameworks-table.md`)
**Corroboration: 8 creators** (↑ from 7) — Josh Braun, 30MPC (Armand/Nick), Giulio Segantini, Jeremy Miner, Jeb Blount, John Barrows, **Colleen Stanley** (Chris Voss = source). *Clears the 5+ bar.* New flavor this cycle (Colleen Stanley): **empathy-then-assertiveness / preemptive strike** — say the feeling first ("you probably think we're too expensive — fair?") and "the emotional temperature drops," then make the ask; better, *surface the objection yourself before they raise it* — "I'm guessing you think we're way too expensive — am I on the right path? → now it's not an objection, it's a conversation"; plus the sharpest articulation of the agree-first move, the **Mr. Miyagi 3-step** (Agree as pattern-break → Incentivize the truth with a carrot → Split the objection into options they pick from). Prior flavor (John Barrows/Tim Castle): **reframe the objection as data, not resistance** — it reveals what they care about; shift from emotion to strategy, then ask "what matters most right now?"; plus the **humbling disclaimer** ("I feel a little awkward asking, but what'd be the top reasons you wouldn't move forward, if any?") and the **off-the-record reset** to surface the real competitor.
The field is unanimous on a counter-intuitive move the canon doesn't name: **never logically rebut an objection — agree/validate it FIRST to drop the emotional guard, then re-engage.** "When you're explaining, you're losing" / "fighting an objection = telling the prospect they're wrong" (30MPC); **Defuse → Discover → Discuss**, "understand, don't overcome — prospects can smell your commission breath" (Josh Braun); the **Mr. Miyagi / ACE** sequence (agree → ask permission → ask a question that makes *them* change their mind — "the prospect is the best person to change the prospect's mind"); **The Ledge** = a pre-scripted reflex line ("Shoot, this one's on me") to buy a beat. **Copy-relevant:** in a sales page's FAQ / objection-handling, *validate the reader's resistance before answering it* rather than arguing with it. High confidence. Proposed one-liner: *"Disarm before logic — meet an objection (on a call or on the page) by first agreeing it's reasonable; arguing triggers reactance, agreement reopens the mind."*

### 10. [OPEN] Add a **convergent direct-response headline formula** to `writing-mechanics.md` (or `frameworks-table.md`)
**Corroboration: 10 creators** — Stephen Pope, Daniel Fazio, Patrick Dang, Joel Kaplan, Frank Kern, Matt Macnamara, Myron Golden, Trent Dressel, Ian Koniak, **Neville Medhora**. *Clears the 5+ bar comfortably.*

Proposed one-liner: **"Headline = [specific end result] + [the customer] + [specific timeframe] + [the #1 objection answered], in mixable order."** Field examples: "Increase the conversions of your Shopify store in 3 days or I'll refund your money" (Neville); "get 30 qualified calls in the next 30 days or you don't pay" (Fazio); "She Earns $500K+ in Tech Sales (Without Working Nights & Weekends)" (Koniak). The number/result is the hook; the parenthetical or "or I'll…" kills the top objection *inside the headline*. Pairs with the existing specificity principle but deserves its own copy-able template. High confidence.

Suggest adding the Cialdini "because" datapoint + the "tee up every ask with a reason" rule to Principle 16. High confidence.
