## Cold outreach (open the relationship)
- **The permission opener.** "Hey [name], honestly you weren't expecting my call… can I take 30 seconds, and you tell me if it's worth continuing?" Lowers pressure, earns the floor. (Braun / Barrows)
- **Reason-for-call.** "The reason I'm reaching out is [specific, true observation about them]." Specificity kills the spam reflex. (Barrows)
- **Signal-based > volume.** One relevant trigger (a hire, a launch, a post) beats 1,000 templates. 73% delete templated outreach on sight.
- **Follow-up is the channel.** 80% of replies come from touches 2–4. Cadence: value, bump, idea, soft-close — then stop. Grace, not nagging.
- **The unsell.** "This might not even be for you, but…" Removing the push makes them lean in. (Braun)

## Discovery (where the sale is actually made)
- **Problem before product, always.** Quantify the pain in their words and numbers before you mention what you do.
- **Cost of inaction.** The status quo is the competitor. "What happens if this stays broken for another 6 months?" ~70% of B2B decisions avoid loss, not chase gain.
- **Multithread.** "Who else feels this pain besides you?" One champion = one point of failure.
- **Strip-line.** "Honestly, it doesn't sound like this is a big enough problem for you to fix." Pulls the truth out. (30MPC / Kazanjy)
- **Sell the test drive, not the car.** Lower the commitment of the next step, not the value of the outcome. (30MPC)

## Objection handling (the universal move)
1. **Isolate.** "[Objection] aside, is there anything else holding you back?" Find the ONE real objection; kill smokescreens. (Macnamara)
2. **Label.** "It sounds like price is your number-one concern." Name the emotion before the logic. (Voss)
3. **Verify it's real.** If they bring up something else, the first thing was the smokescreen.
4. **Then answer** — with reframe or value, never a reflexive discount.

Common flips:
- **"Too expensive"** → isolate, then anchor to the cost of inaction / the value-stack. Never drop price to rescue it. The guarantee is the price's bodyguard.
- **"Let me think about it"** → "Totally fair. Usually when someone says that, it's one of three things — [X, Y, Z]. Which is closest?" Surfaces the real block.
- **"Send me some info"** → "Happy to. So I send the right thing and not a brochure you'll never open — what's the one thing you'd need to see?"
- **"We're happy with our current setup"** → poke-the-bear: "Makes sense. Out of curiosity, if you could change one thing about it, what would it be?"

## Pricing & the close
- **Value equation (Hormozi):** raise dream outcome + perceived likelihood; lower time + effort. Price feels small when the equation is loud.
- **Anchor high, then frame.** The first number sets the field. Premium container matches a strong guarantee.
- **The take-away.** "Honestly, I'm not sure this is a fit — and I'd rather tell you that than sell you something." Raises value, filters tire-kickers. (Welch / 30MPC)
- **The guarantee is the close.** A loud, conditioned guarantee converts the loud half and protects the quiet half (the conditions). It IS the risk reversal.
- **One concrete next step.** Never end on "let me know." Always a booked, specific action.

## Deal triage (when something's stuck)
- Stalled late deal → the discovery was weak. Re-qualify, re-quantify the pain, go for the no.
- Ghosting after a great call → happy ears; the pain wasn't real or the buyer wasn't the buyer.
- "Maybe" that won't close → a kept no is worth more than a forced maybe. Get the no.

## Role-play / coaching mode
- Play the buyer at the user's real difficulty. Push the exact objection they fear. Debrief: trace what broke to the *earliest* point it broke. One fix, not ten.
