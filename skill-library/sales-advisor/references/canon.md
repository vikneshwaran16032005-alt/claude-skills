# Sales canon — the non-negotiables (survive across every operator in the corpus)

These are the laws. Tactics change; these don't. If a move violates one of these, it's wrong no matter who said it.

## On the whole game
1. **The sale is made in discovery, not the close.** A weak close is a symptom; the disease is upstream. ~60–70% of B2B buyers decide before they ever talk to you — your job is to be the frame they decide inside.
2. **You're a doctor, not a vendor.** Diagnose before you prescribe. A doctor who prescribes before examining is a quack. Buyers trust the operator who tells them the truth, including "this isn't for you."
3. **People buy a better version of themselves.** Never sell the product; sell the outcome and the identity. Decrease their time and effort to get there; increase the dream outcome and their belief it'll work for them (Hormozi's value equation).
4. **Trust is the tiebreaker, not likeability.** Buyers buy from people they trust, not just like. Industry-specific competence is the price of entry; trust closes.

## On prospecting & outreach
5. **Specificity beats volume.** A sharp, relevant, signal-based message out-pulls a thousand templates. ~73% of buyers delete robotic templated outreach on sight. Perceived effort earns the reply.
6. **80% of replies come from follow-ups 2–4.** Persistence (done with grace, not nagging) is the single most-corroborated prospecting truth in the corpus. Send once and quit = "outbound doesn't work."
7. **Lower the pressure to raise the truth.** Permission-based, low-pressure openers ("you weren't expecting my call…") disarm the reflex. Going for the early "no" beats chasing the "yes."

## On discovery
8. **Problem first, product never (yet).** Quantify the pain before you breathe a word about your solution. No pain, no sale — and the pain has to be THEIRS, in THEIR words.
9. **The status quo is your real competitor.** Most deals die in "do nothing," not to a rival. Sell the cost of inaction. ~70% of B2B decisions are made to avoid loss, not chase gain.
10. **Multithread.** A single champion is a single point of failure. Real deals touch the economic buyer.

## On objections
11. **Isolate, then verify.** "Price aside, is anything else holding you back?" An un-isolated objection is a smokescreen. Find the ONE real one before you answer.
12. **Label the emotion before you solve the logic.** Voss: name what they feel ("it sounds like price is your number-one concern") before you argue. Empathy precedes assertiveness.
13. **Never discount on reflex.** Price drops train the buyer that your price was fake. The guarantee, the value-stack, and the take-away protect price. Discount only as a deliberate trade.

## On closing
14. **The take-away raises value.** Walking away ("honestly, this might not be a fit") makes the right buyer lean in. Willingness to lose the deal is itself a close.
15. **Make the next step concrete and small.** A vague "let me know" is a lost deal. End every interaction with one specific, scheduled next action.
16. **A kept "no" beats a fake "maybe."** Qualify hard, decline freely. The pipeline you protect is worth more than the deal you force.

## The bans (instant rewrite)
- Pitching before diagnosing · feature-dumping · discounting to close · fake urgency · happy-ears (hearing "yes" when they said "maybe") · single-threading a big deal · arguing with an objection before labeling it · ending a call without a booked next step.

## Compliance (for anything written as RIOA / Vik)
- No income claims, no earnings promises, no "you'll make $X" — outcome and process language only. This canon advises the MOTION; the words that ship still pass the RIOA compliance gate.
